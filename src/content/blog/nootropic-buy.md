The procurement discipline most buyers skip The peptide supply chain has a persistent problem: buyers accept a purity number without asking how it was measured. A COA that reports 98.5% purity using a 15-minute gradient on a C18 column with no method parameters disclosed is not evidence of quality. It is evidence that the supplier ran a fast screen and stopped there. Epimers, deletion sequences, and oxidized variants can coelute with the target under generic gradient conditions and never appear as separate peaks. The purity number looks clean. The compound is not. The procurement discipline that actually protects a research program is insisting on method disclosure as a non-negotiable RFQ criterion, building independent verification into the payment schedule rather than treating it as optional, and requiring traceability documentation that connects the synthesis batch to the specific vials being shipped. Suppliers who resist any of those three requests are telling you something about their process. Budget for verification upfront. The cost of a failed experiment or a reformulation cycle is substantially higher than a third-party testing panel.
