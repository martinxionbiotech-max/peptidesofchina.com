Learn to verify purity with HPLC, confirm identity with Mass Spec, and spot fraudulent lab reports. Analytical testing is most useful when each method is matched to a defined question. A professional review should distinguish chromatographic profile, molecular identity, absolute content, formulation attributes, and microbiological or biological requirements rather than compressing them into one purity percentage. What common methods measure RP-HPLC separates detectable components and can report relative chromatographic area under a stated method. It does not alone prove molecular identity or the absolute amount of peptide in a vial. LC-MS measures mass-to-charge signals and can support identity or mass assignment when the observed result, expected species, adducts, and method are interpreted together. It is not, by itself, a full purity or potency assessment. NMR measures features of a sample’s molecular structure and environment. It can be useful for structural or formulation questions when an appropriate validated method and reference are available. A quantitative content assay addresses how much target material is present; it is a different question from relative chromatographic purity. Microbiological, endotoxin, stability, and biological-potency tests are separate questions and should not be inferred from HPLC or LC-MS. Read the report as evidence A useful report identifies the material, lot or batch, sample description, method, relevant instrument or conditions, result, units, and acceptance criteria. Check that the document date and identifiers are consistent with the supplied material. A polished PDF or a high percentage is not enough if the lot linkage and method details are missing. When additional testing is appropriate Additional analysis should follow the study risk, material, and intended measurement. An appropriately qualified independent laboratory may be useful when supplier documentation is incomplete or when the protocol requires confirmation. The laboratory and method should be selected for the specific material; no single panel proves every quality attribute. Avoid overclaiming Do not describe a relative HPLC percentage as proof of identity, sterility, potency, or clinical suitability. Do not describe supplier or manufacturer documentation as independent testing unless an independent party actually performed the analysis. Do not infer stability from a release result at one time point. Do not transfer a result from one batch, salt, formulation, or product to another without evidence. A concise review checklist Define the identity and measurement needed for the experiment. Check lot linkage and read the method and units. Compare orthogonal evidence when identity and content both matter. Record limitations and unresolved discrepancies before using the material.
