Direct sourcing and reseller distribution differ in who holds the batch record, not in what the vial contains. A direct channel puts the buyer in contact with the party that can name the synthesis site and release the lot; a reseller channel adds custody, repackaging, and fulfillment steps between that party and the parcel. Choose on evidence: which entity made the batch, which lot is in the carton, who tested the sample, and who still holds the record after the invoice is paid. Then price the answer.

This article is for laboratory procurement teams buying research peptides. It does not cover clinical supply or personal use. Peptides of China coordinates sourcing, orders, supplier communication, and delivery support; it does not manufacture peptides or operate an independent testing laboratory.

## Four channel models, four evidence trails

"Direct" and "reseller" are roles, not quality grades. Four models appear in the China peptide market, and each leaves a different paper trail.

**Synthesis site.** The entity that runs coupling, cleavage, purification, and lyophilization, and issues the batch production record. Buying here removes intermediaries, but the buyer takes on export documentation, consolidation, and whatever minimum the site sets for a production slot.

**Brand owner with a toll manufacturer.** One company owns the specification and label; a separate site synthesizes the batch. The brand answers commercial questions and may hold the specification, while the site holds the manufacturing record. The buyer reconciles two entities instead of one.

**Trading company or reseller.** Buys stock, may repackage into smaller vials, and resells. Small quantities and fast dispatch are real advantages. Repackaging is also where a lot code can stop matching the manufacturer's certificate, and where a new label, headspace, and closure enter the chain.

**Sourcing coordinator or platform.** Places orders with suppliers and manages communication without manufacturing. Useful when a request spans several suppliers; the value depends on whether the coordinator chases batch-linked documents or only forwards a price.

| Channel | Batch record holder | Buyer's counterparty | Strength | Exposure |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Synthesis site | Site that synthesized and released the lot | Export sales or QA | Shortest route to a batch answer | Order minimums, buyer-run logistics |
| Brand owner with toll manufacturer | Site holds record; brand holds specification | Brand commercial team | One specification across a line | Two entities to reconcile |
| Trading company or reseller | Upstream producer; trader may hold a copy | Sales desk | Small quantities, stock, consolidated shipping | Repackaging can break the lot chain |
| Sourcing coordinator | Supplier or site; coordinator mediates | Account manager | Several suppliers on one request | Responsibility ends at coordination unless agreed |

The number of parties matters less than the number of unanswered questions. A coordinator that names the site, obtains a batch record extract, and states its role in writing is easier to work with than a manufacturer that answers technical questions with a catalog page. ## The evidence matrix: who can produce which record

A channel is only as good as the records it can obtain.

| Record | Direct chain | Reseller chain | Establishes | Leaves open |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Specification | Buyer and manufacturer, dated | Reseller, derived from the manufacturer | Material, methods, limits | Whether any batch met them |
| Certificate of analysis | Manufacturer's quality unit or contract lab | Manufacturer, forwarded by reseller | Results for a stated batch | Whether the sample came from shipped vials |
| Batch record extract | Manufacturer | Manufacturer, if the reseller requests it | How the lot was made and released | Whether the method suits the assay |
| Vial label and lot code | Filler | Filler or repackager | Identity of the unit in hand | Whether the code maps to the certificate |
| Packing list | Seller or forwarder | Reseller | Count, net weight, dimensions | Whether it matches the invoice |
| Origin statement | Manufacturer or chamber of commerce | Reseller, sometimes unsigned | Origin for duty and marking | Whether declared origin is accurate |
| Import permit | Buyer | Buyer, unless the reseller imports | Authority to import | Nothing about the material |

Two rows carry most disputes. The certificate and the label are produced at different times, often by different companies, and a repackager can print a new label without touching the certificate. If the vial's lot code does not appear on the certificate, the chain is broken there, whatever the purity result says. The [documentation package](/blog/peptide-documentation-package/) checklist lists the fields each record should carry, including the ones a summary sheet tends to omit.

## Where the money moves: a total-cost comparison

A per-vial price is a starting number. The comparison that matters is landed cost per accepted milligram, assessed per supplier order, because minimums, freight, and testing are charged at that level.

| Cost line | Direct from the site | Through a reseller | Frequent omission |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Goods | Site price at the quoted tier | Reseller price with handling and stock risk | Comparing packs without normalizing to mg |
| Freight | Buyer books, or site quotes one parcel | Consolidated, possibly repacked | Assuming one parcel covers a multi-supplier order |
| Insurance | Buyer's choice under FCA or CIP | Often seller-arranged, cover unstated | Clauses A and C are not equivalent |
| Duty and tax | Buyer is importer of record | Reseller imports, then re-invoices | Using invoice value as the duty base |
| Clearance | Broker, storage, demurrage | Bundled, rarely itemized | Free-time limits at destination |
| Testing | Buyer commissions per lot | Supplier certificate may be the only record | Sampling plan and who draws the vial |
| Rejection | Return freight and schedule loss sit with the buyer | Replacement negotiated with the seller | Whether a replacement covers a missed milestone |
| Administration | Permits, broker, documents | Fewer records, less buyer work | Staff time is a real cost line |

Direct sourcing is not automatically cheaper. A site that sells only in large units, or quotes EXW, can cost more per usable milligram than a reseller that breaks a pack and ships one carton. The reverse holds too: a reseller price that bundles repacking, local stock, and one invoice can be the lower landed number for a small order. The comparison works only when both columns use the same specification and the same quantity in milligrams.

For reference, Peptides of China's public terms at the time of writing set a $600 goods minimum per supplier order, with shipping excluded. A 5% discount applies when a supplier order's gross goods subtotal reaches $3,500; spending across suppliers is not combined. A $10 processing deposit starts manual handling and stock verification and is credited toward the final invoice. Express shipping is quoted at $97–127 per parcel, with transit estimated at 7–12 days from carrier handover. Those figures describe one platform's terms, not a market average. The [bulk order minimum guide](/blog/bulk-peptide-order-minimums-2026-research-lab-guide/) works through how pack size interacts with them.

## Documentation ownership does not transfer with the vial

ISO 9001:2015 clause 8.5.2 requires identification and traceability of outputs, and clause 8.4 requires control of externally provided processes, products, and services. A research peptide purchase usually sits outside a certified quality system, but the clauses describe the problem: traceability depends on someone being assigned to keep it.

Write down who owns each record:

1. The specification, jointly, with a version number and date.
2. The certificate of analysis, by the issuing quality unit, not the sales desk.
3. The batch production record, by the manufacturer, under its own policy.
4. The import permit, by the buyer, wherever one is required.
5. The receipt record, by the buyer, from parcel opening to acceptance.
6. Retention samples, by the buyer, at the labeled storage condition.

A reseller can hold copies. A copy is not ownership, and the distinction surfaces six months later when the sales contact has changed. Ask for the issuer's name, address, and a contact route that does not pass through the seller. FDA's guidance on contract manufacturing quality agreements makes the same point for commercial supply: allocate quality responsibilities in writing instead of assuming them. For a research purchase, the purchase order and the dated specification revision are the closest equivalent, and they should name who answers for identity, purity, and content.

## Batch traceability: four identifiers, one lot

A lot number is useful only when it appears in four places: the vial label, the certificate, the invoice or packing list, and the batch record extract. Reconciliation means checking all four, not the two that are easiest to reach.

When a label carries an internal code that differs from the certificate, ask for a signed cross-reference mapping both codes to the batch. Keep the letter with the originals. Do not relabel a vial or edit a certificate to make numbers agree; an inconsistent file that is documented is easier to defend than a consistent file that was altered.

Repackaging deserves its own question. Moving lyophilized powder from a bulk container into smaller vials exposes it to ambient moisture and to a new closure, and it creates a unit the original manufacturer never released as a finished package. That can be done well, but it should be described in writing: who repacked, at what humidity and temperature, with which vial and stopper, under which lot code. A certificate for the bulk lot does not automatically describe the repacked vial.

Retention is part of traceability. A workable rule is two vials per lot beyond the vial tested: one unopened for confirmatory work, one working aliquot, stored at the labeled condition, typically -20 °C and protected from light, for the study duration plus 12 months. Label each with the lot, receipt date, and study code. The [traceability guide](/blog/why-source-traceability-matters-research/) explains why the link between vial and batch record decides whether a result can be repeated.

## Lead time, minimums, and testing responsibility

Lead time has three parts, and a single quoted number hides two of them.

1. **Stock verification.** On this platform, checking availability with the synthesis facility is manual and typically takes about 2 business days (China time, GMT+8).
2. **Processing and handover.** After final payment, assembly and handover to the carrier take 2–3 business days.
3. **Transit.** Express delivery is estimated at 7–12 days from carrier handover, not from order date.

Minimums interact with lead time. A supplier order below the $600 goods threshold either waits for consolidation or is removed; a qualifying new buyer may use one below-minimum order as a trial, and every other supplier order in the same request must still reach its own minimum. Splitting a request across suppliers multiplies parcels, tracking records, and arrival dates. The [lead-time evaluation guide](/blog/how-to-evaluate-peptide-supplier-lead-times-in-2026/) covers how to score that variability instead of averaging it away.

Testing responsibility splits along the same line as documentation. The manufacturer's certificate is release testing by the party that made the lot. A buyer-commissioned laboratory report is acceptance testing on the buyer's sample. Neither substitutes for the other: a certificate can be accurate for the batch and still not describe the vial that arrived, and an independent report can be accurate for one vial while saying nothing about the rest of the lot. Name the method in the specification — RP-HPLC conditions, LC-MS mass tolerance, Karl Fischer, ion chromatography, LAL — because a result without a method cannot be compared across lots or laboratories. ICH Q2(R2) treats that comparability as the purpose of method validation, and ICH Q6A ties acceptance criteria to the test procedure rather than to a bare number.

## Communication chain and issue handling

Each hop between buyer and synthesis site is a translation step. A question about a deviation, a resin lot, or a purification gradient passes through an account manager, possibly a trader, then to an engineer who never sees the buyer's email. The answer that returns is often shorter than the question.

Test the chain before the first order with three questions that require a site-level answer: which column and gradient produced the purity result, whether any deviation was opened during the batch, and who signs the certificate. A channel that answers within two working days with specifics is usable. A channel that answers with adjectives is one where every future problem will be negotiated rather than resolved.

Issue handling separates the models more sharply than price does. Define three classes in the purchase record:

- **Shipping discrepancy.** Short count, damage, or a broken temperature indicator. Open a record the same day, photograph the parcel and labels, and hold the material.
- **Order error.** Wrong item, wrong presentation, or an unannounced substitution. The remedy is commercial and belongs in the order terms.
- **Analytical nonconformity.** A result outside the agreed limit. The remedy depends on whether the sample was drawn under a documented plan and whether the retention vials are intact.

The recall question follows the same structure. A reseller that repackaged material may be unable to trace remaining vials from a lot, which limits a withdrawal to the units it still controls and the customers it can still reach. A synthesis site can usually trace a batch through its own distribution record. That difference outweighs a small price gap when a study depends on one lot, which is why lot-level distribution should be asked about before an issue exists rather than after.

A reseller is the right call in specific cases: a quantity below any site's minimum, a compound needed from stock within days, several items consolidated into one parcel, or a local invoice and payment route that a direct export sale cannot offer. Those are legitimate reasons to accept an extra custody step.

## A decision sequence before the first order

1. Write the specification, including methods and numeric limits, before requesting a price.
2. Name the legal entity that will invoice, and the site that will synthesize the batch.
3. Ask which records the channel can obtain, not which ones it usually provides.
4. Build the landed-cost comparison per supplier order, including testing and a rejection scenario.
5. Fix the Incoterm and the importer of record before dispatch; the 11 Incoterms 2020 rules allocate cost, risk, and clearance differently.
6. Reconcile four identifiers at receipt and open any discrepancy the same day.
7. Re-qualify at change events: new site, new synthesis route, new presentation, new specification version.

Run steps 1 to 3 as a desk review first. ## The Author's Take

**Position.** Choose a channel by the records it can obtain and the party it can reach, not by the label "direct" or "reseller."

**Reasoning.** The cost of a channel is the price of the goods plus the cost of every question it cannot answer. A reseller that holds stock, breaks packs, and ships one carton solves a real problem for a small laboratory order, and a synthesis site that will not discuss a batch record creates a problem that no discount fixes. The evidence standard stays the same in both directions: one lot, four identifiers, a named method, and a party who owns the record after the sale.
