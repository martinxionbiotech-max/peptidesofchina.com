Frequently Asked Questions What is the industry standard for research peptide purity? The baseline standard for research-grade peptides is typically 98% purity as measured by HPLC. However, purity alone is insufficient for establishing comprehensive research peptide quality standards . You must also confirm the peptide sequence through Mass Spectrometry to ensure the compound's identity matches the intended research target. High purity is meaningless if the molecular weight is incorrect or if the batch contains truncated sequences that bypass basic UV detection. How can I tell if an HPLC report has been reused or templated? Reused reports often display identical baseline noise patterns; this is statistically impossible in real-world analytical chemistry. Check for mismatched timestamps between the synthesis date and the testing date. If the batch number on the vial doesn't correlate with the manufacturer's internal logs, the documentation is likely a template. Professional procurement requires cross-referencing these reports with independent lab data to ensure the paperwork accurately reflects the physical product. Why is Mass Spectrometry essential for peptide verification? Mass Spectrometry (MS) provides the molecular weight of the compound, confirming that the synthesized peptide has the correct sequence of amino acids. While HPLC measures relative purity, it cannot distinguish between the target peptide and a different compound with a similar retention time. MS is the only way to verify that you've received the specific peptide you ordered. It serves as a critical identity check that chromatography alone cannot provide. What is the difference between acetate and TFA salt versions of a peptide? TFA is the default salt used during the purification process, but it can be toxic in certain cellular assays. Acetate salts are often preferred for research requiring higher biocompatibility or specific solubility profiles. The choice of counter-ion impacts the peptide's behavior in sterile diluents and its overall stability. You should select the salt form based on your specific research model and the required pH level of the final solution. Can I rely solely on manufacturer-provided COAs for my research?
