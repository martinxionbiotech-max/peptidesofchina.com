Authenticating research-peptide documentation means determining whether a report is attributable to the material and batch being considered. A document that looks complete can still be a generic, reused, or incomplete record. The safe conclusion is limited to what the supplied evidence supports.

## What a credible record should connect

- The material description and batch or lot number on the report should match the physical label and purchase record.
- The report should identify its issuing laboratory or facility, date, methods, results, and units clearly enough for review.
- HPLC and MS results should be understood as method-specific evidence rather than a blanket claim about every quality attribute.
- Any report identifier, QR code, or verification portal should resolve through the issuing laboratory’s own channel when such a service is provided.

## Checks for recycled or altered reports

Compare reports from different batches for repeated report identifiers, identical chromatogram images, unchanged instrument details, or inconsistent dates. Check for mismatched fonts, overwritten headers, missing pages, and a batch number that appears only in a pasted image. These are reasons to pause and request clarification; they are not by themselves proof of fraud. A supplier’s claim that a document is “verified” does not replace the buyer’s review.

## What the evidence does not establish

A COA or a high purity percentage does not by itself establish molecular identity, delivered peptide content, sterility, endotoxin status, potency, finished-product stability, or human or animal safety. HPLC and MS have different scopes, and additional attributes require their own methods.
