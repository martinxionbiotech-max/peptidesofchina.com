Purity Verification in a B2B Context: HPLC, MS, and Analytical Realities A common misconception in the B2B peptide marketplace is that a manufacturer-provided Certificate of Analysis (COA) serves as a definitive guarantee of product quality. In reality, these documents should be viewed only as baseline reference material. The global supply chain is frequently compromised by the use of "template" COAs, where a single successful analytical run is used to represent multiple subsequent batches, or worse, entirely different synthesis cycles. Relying solely on these papers without a strategy for independent verification introduces significant risk to any research project. High-Performance Liquid Chromatography (HPLC) and Mass Spectrometry (MS) are the standard tools for assessing purity and identity, yet they aren't infallible. HPLC measures the purity of a sample by separating its components, but it doesn't necessarily identify what those components are. Mass Spectrometry provides the molecular weight, helping to confirm the identity of the peptide, but it doesn't account for impurities that might share similar properties. Because these reports are digital files, they're easily manipulated. Professional procurement requires treating manufacturer data as a starting point for independent lab verification rather than a final verdict. Reading HPLC and Mass Spectrometry Reports Identifying red flags in manufacturer-provided data is a vital skill for procurement officers. Common issues include HPLC charts with missing timestamps, generic headers that don't match the manufacturer's facility, or baseline noise that appears suspiciously "clean." When reviewing MS data, the detected mass must correlate precisely with the theoretical molecular weight of the peptide. Any discrepancy, even a minor one, suggests a failure in synthesis or the presence of significant salts and trifluoroacetic acid (TFA) residues. Due to these variables, the necessity of peptide testing for every new batch cannot be overstated. It's the only way to confirm that the reference data provided by the synthesis facility matches the physical material received.
