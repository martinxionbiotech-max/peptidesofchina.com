Most peptide shipments that stall at a border are not stopped because of what is inside the vial. They are stopped because the paperwork does not let an officer answer four questions: what is this material, what is it classified as, what is it worth, and who is responsible for it. A supplier who cannot answer those questions in writing leaves the shipment unidentified, and an unidentified powder is a problem regardless of its analytical quality.

This article treats the document pack as an operating system: what each document is for, which stage produces it, how invoice and packing list reconcile, and which defects recur. It is written for the buyer who receives the file.

Two constraints apply. Import requirements vary by jurisdiction, and they change without notice. The importer of record is responsible for confirming the applicable rules with the destination customs authority or a licensed broker before dispatch. Nothing here guarantees clearance, and nothing here is legal or customs advice.

## What "Legitimate Documentation" Actually Means

The word carries a specific meaning in freight. It does not mean a document with a company stamp. It means a record a third party can verify against another record and against the goods. Four conditions follow.

It is issued by a party with standing. A certificate of analysis comes from the manufacturer or the laboratory that ran the test; a certificate of origin from a chamber of commerce; a safety data sheet from the party that knows the hazard profile. A supplier's letter restating one of these is a statement, not the document.

It identifies the goods in the same terms as the other documents. If the invoice says ten vials of a 5 mg lyophilized powder and the certificate covers a 10 mg presentation, the set is broken even when each file is tidy.

It carries an identity that ties to a batch. The lot on the certificate has to appear on the label, the packing list, and the invoice line. Otherwise the certificate proves something about some material, not about this shipment.

It survives a records request months later; a file that cannot be produced on demand converts an audit into an assessment.

Anonymity is the failure this definition catches. A reseller who will not name the manufacturer, will not put a lot number on an invoice, and stops answering when a broker asks a question has not supplied documentation. The pack is only as useful as the chain behind it.

## The Document Pack by Shipment Stage

Documents are produced in sequence, and each constrains the next.

| Stage | Document created or confirmed | Issued by | Must tie to |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quotation and order | Pro forma invoice, sales contract, Incoterm with named place | Exporter | Description, quantity, and price later repeated on the commercial invoice |
| Batch release | Certificate of analysis, safety data sheet | Manufacturer or testing laboratory | Lot number printed on the vial label |
| Export booking | Air waybill or bill of lading, packing list | Exporter, freight forwarder, carrier | Piece count, gross weight, consignee name and address |
| Export clearance | Export declaration, certificate of origin where required | Exporter, chamber of commerce | Origin statement and tariff code shown on the invoice |
| Import entry | Commercial invoice, packing list, import permit or licence where required, importer of record details | Importer or customs broker | Every line, value, and quantity declared on the entry |
| Post-entry | Entry summary, duty payment record, classification or ruling file | Importer | Retained for the statutory record period |

Two observations follow. The invoice begins as a pro forma and is finalized at entry, so a careless pro forma becomes a careless declaration. The packing list is the only document connecting a commercial line to a physical carton; as an afterthought that link disappears.

Order matters for cost. A certificate of origin requested once cargo is booked is produced under pressure, and chambers of commerce do not issue retroactively. An import permit discovered late invalidates a clean booking. Build backwards from the entry and confirm each item before handover.

## The Commercial Invoice: Fields That Get Read

The commercial invoice anchors the entry. Every other document is checked against it, so a defect here propagates.

United States rules provide a concrete field list. Under 19 CFR 141.86, an import invoice must state the port of entry, the time and place of sale with seller and buyer identified, a detailed description including the name the item is known by and its grade or quality, the marks and numbers on the packages, quantities in the exporter's units or in US units, the purchase price per item in the currency of purchase, all charges itemized by name and amount, the country of origin, and the name of a responsible employee who can answer questions about the transaction.

Two subparagraphs catch buyers repeatedly. Under 141.86(d), the invoice and all attachments must be in English or carry an accurate translation — an untranslated Chinese packing list is an incomplete file, not a bilingual courtesy. Under 141.86(e), the invoice must describe in adequate detail what merchandise sits in each package. "Ten cartons of peptide" fails when the cartons hold different compounds.

Valuation is separate from the printed number. Packing costs, assists, and selling commissions can enter the customs value; some post-importation costs do not. A broker needs the full commercial terms before deciding what belongs in the declared value. Marking a commercial shipment as a gift, a sample, or a document set fails at examination and turns an administrative error into a penalties question.

Request the invoice in editable form before dispatch and read it against the purchase order line by line. A buyer who first sees it attached to a tracking notification has lost the chance to correct it cheaply.

## Invoice-to-Packing-List Reconciliation

Reconciliation is not proofreading. It is a test that the commercial file and the physical file describe one shipment. Run the table before cargo is released.

| Field | On the invoice | On the packing list | Pass condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| Description | Product name, presentation, grade | Product name and kit configuration | Same words, same presentation, no paraphrase |
| Quantity | Units sold per line | Vials or kits per carton | Line totals and shipment total both agree |
| Net weight | Stated or derivable from fill mass | Sum of peptide mass per carton | Totals match within rounding |
| Gross weight | Usually absent | Carton weight including packaging and dry ice | Consistent with the air waybill |
| Lot or batch | Lot identifier per line | Lot identifier per carton | One lot per line, or mixed lots itemized |
| Package count | Implied by line structure | Carton numbers one through n | No gaps, no duplicate carton numbers |
| Marks | Shipping marks if any | Marks and numbers as applied | Identical strings |
| Incoterm | Three-letter rule plus named place | Not applicable | Contract and invoice cite the same version and place |

Gross and net weight are the fields most often approximated. Net weight is the peptide mass; gross weight includes vials, stoppers, cartons, and coolant. A few hundred grams is trivial commercially and conspicuous to a carrier, because the air waybill repeats the gross weight. When three documents disagree, the shipment is weighed.

Kit configuration is the second trap. "One kit, ten vials" differs from "ten vials" when the kit includes diluent or a presentation box. State the configuration.

## COA, SDS, and Batch Identity

The certificate of analysis and the safety data sheet answer different questions.

A batch certificate reports what was measured on a specific lot: identity by mass spectrometry, purity by HPLC, often water content, counter-ion, and endotoxin where relevant. Its value in a shipping file is traceability, not marketing. The lot number must match the lot printed on the vial label and repeated on the invoice. A certificate whose lot differs from the label causes buyers to reject otherwise deliverable shipments, and an officer can spot it without technical training.

A purity figure with no column, gradient, or wavelength attached is a number without provenance. Reading conventions are set out in the guide to [reading a peptide COA](/blog/how-to-read-a-peptide-coa-a-researchers-guide-to-hplc-and-ms-reports/), and the wider document set in the [peptide documentation package](/blog/peptide-documentation-package/).

A safety data sheet is a hazard communication document, not a purity record. It identifies the substance, classification, handling, storage, and transport information. Two failures recur: an SDS covering a different salt form than the material shipped — acetate and arginine salts are not interchangeable for hazard or transport purposes — and an SDS with no revision date or a manufacturer name that does not match the invoice seller. Both invite a question the exporter may not answer quickly.

Transport classification is the shipper's responsibility. Dry ice cargo is generally declared under UN 1845, class 9, with handling information on the air waybill, and the [IATA Dangerous Goods Regulations](https://www.iata.org/en/programs/cargo/dgr/) set the framework carriers apply. Whether a shipment needs a shipper's declaration depends on the material and packing instruction; that determination belongs to the shipper.

## Certificate of Origin and the HS Code Caveat

A certificate of origin is issued by a chamber of commerce or another authorized body. It states where the goods were produced and is not a supplier's letter of origin. Brokers read the two differently, and a preference claim supported only by a letter tends to fail.

Origin is not the shipping route. Material consolidated in a third country can retain Chinese origin if wholly obtained or substantially transformed there. Preferential treatment needs the specific proof the agreement recognizes, often a prescribed invoice statement rather than a chamber certificate. A non-preferential certificate creates no preference.

The tariff code is where origin, description, and value meet. The Harmonized System, maintained by the [World Customs Organization](https://www.wcoomd.org/en/topics/nomenclature/overview.aspx), places peptides across several headings. The most cited is 2937, covering hormones and derivatives "including chain modified polypeptides, used primarily as hormones." That qualifier does real work: a peptide not used primarily as a hormone does not fall into 2937 by default. Other headings in peptide entries include 2934 for other heterocyclic compounds, 3504 for peptones, and 3822 for prepared laboratory reagents. Chapter 29 requires a separate chemically defined organic compound to be exactly that, so a kit or formulated reagent changes the analysis.

The lesson for the invoice is narrow. The code has to rest on a written technical basis — structure, salt form, function, presentation — and appear on the export document, the invoice, and the entry. A code chosen for a lower rate, without that basis, is a misdeclaration. Classification detail is in the [HS code guide for peptides](/blog/hs-code-for-peptides/); the clearance sequence is in the [customs clearance guide](/blog/research-peptide-customs-clearance-a-professional-b2b-sourcing-guide-2026/).

## Incoterms, Importer of Record, and Signature Liability

Incoterms 2020, published by the [International Chamber of Commerce](https://iccwbo.org/resources-for-business/incoterms-rules/incoterms-2020/), allocate cost, risk, and some obligations. They do not determine who is the importer of record; that status comes from the law and the declaration.

- **EXW** places the goods at the seller's premises. The buyer arranges export and import clearance.
- **FCA** has the seller clear export and hand the goods to the carrier. The buyer handles import.
- **DAP** has the seller deliver to a named place ready for unloading, with the buyer clearing import and paying duty and tax.
- **DDP** has the seller clear import and pay duty. Some jurisdictions still require a local importer of record, so the buyer can remain exposed.

Name the place and cite the version: "DAP Rotterdam, Incoterms 2020." An invoice saying only "DDP" invites a dispute about included charges when a carrier bills for a liftgate or a second attempt.

The importer of record signs the entry and owes the duty. A broker files what the importer supplies and keeps its own records, but a power of attorney authorizes action; it does not transfer liability. An EORI number identifies a trader in the EU or UK, not the party that owes the duty. A buyer who is not the importer of record may still need to authorize the broker, supply an importer number, and accept the entry record.

## Common Defects, Their Cost, and Retention

Most defects are boring, which is the point: cheap to prevent, expensive to fix.

| Defect | Where it shows up | Immediate effect | Fix before dispatch |
|---|---|---|---|
| Generic description ("chemical samples") | Invoice | Entry rejected or routed to a second reviewer | Use the product name, presentation, and grade |
| Vial count does not match the invoice line | Packing list | Physical examination, count discrepancy | Reconcile cartons to lines |
| COA lot differs from the vial label | COA against label | Buyer rejection even after release | Reprint certificate or relabel at origin |
| SDS covers a different salt form | SDS | Transport classification questioned | Obtain the SDS for the shipped form |
| Supplier letter presented as a certificate of origin | Origin file | Preference claim disallowed | Obtain a certificate from a chamber of commerce |
| No importer of record identified | Entry | Shipment cannot be entered | Name the importer before booking |
| Code with no technical basis | Invoice and entry | Post-entry audit exposure | Document structure, function, and presentation |
| DDP with no named place | Invoice | Billing dispute with the carrier | Write the place and the Incoterms version |

The cost is rarely the duty. It is destination storage, a broker's amendment fee, a reshipment, and the weeks a research schedule slips. The fix needs a document from another time zone.

Retention closes the loop. In the United States, entry records must be kept for five years from the date of entry under [19 CFR 163.4](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-19/chapter-I/part-163/subpart-A/section-163.4). That means the invoice, packing list, certificate of origin, and any ruling relied on, stored where they can be produced. A buyer who cannot produce the file has no defense in an audit, even where the declaration was correct.

A folder per shipment, opened at purchase order stage and named with the lot identifier, answers the question six months later without an email search. The [sourcing logistics guide](/blog/peptide-sourcing-logistics-a-professional-guide-to-direct-to-manufacturer-procurement/) covers how that timing meets production; the [manufacturer verification protocol](/blog/china-peptide-manufacturer-verification-a-2026-b2b-sourcing-protocol/) covers the supplier checks that make documents issuable at all.

Two behaviors turn a documentation problem into an enforcement problem: instructing a supplier to re-label goods or misdescribe them, and splitting a shipment to fall under a threshold. A neutral outer carton is normal practice; a false description inside it is not. The importer signs the declaration, and the exposure follows the signature.

## The Author's Take

**Position:** Documentation is not paperwork that follows a sale. It determines whether the goods arrive, and it belongs at the quotation stage, not the border.

**Reasoning:** The failures are rarely exotic. An invoice calls the goods a peptide sample. A certificate covers a lot that is not on the label. A packing list totals ten cartons while the air waybill says eleven. Each is fixable in an afternoon before dispatch and unfixable after arrival, because the fix needs a party in another country to issue a corrected original. Treating documentation as the forwarder's job is the root of most of it. The forwarder transmits; the exporter issues; the importer is liable. When a buyer asks for the invoice draft and packing list before booking, three-quarters of these defects never happen. The rest trace to a certificate that was never batch-specific, a supplier-selection problem rather than a shipping problem.
