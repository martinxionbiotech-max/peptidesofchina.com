## Why mechanism and outcome must stay separate

Receptor activity can provide a hypothesis for observed appetite, gastrointestinal, or metabolic signals. A mechanistic explanation is not a diagnosis and does not show that every reported symptom is caused by the same pathway. Trial results should be read by population, endpoint, exposure, and comparator rather than converted into a general personal-risk prediction.

## What research reports can establish

- A clinical or preclinical study can report adverse events observed under its own conditions.
- A finding in an investigational retatrutide study does not establish the safety profile of an unapproved research material.
- Results from retatrutide should not be transferred automatically to tirzepatide, other incretin agents, or a different formulation.
- An adverse-event list is not a human-use protocol, dose-escalation plan, or treatment recommendation.

## How to read a side-effect claim

1. Identify the study design, population, comparator, and exposure.
2. Check whether the report gives event definitions, severity, timing, and discontinuation information.
3. Separate an observed association from a proposed mechanism.
4. Keep investigational material distinct from an approved finished drug product and its labeling.
