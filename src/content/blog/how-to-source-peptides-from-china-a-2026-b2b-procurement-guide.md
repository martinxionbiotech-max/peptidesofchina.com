Source peptides from China by running the purchase as a documented material transfer, not a price comparison. Define the sequence, salt form, presentation, and test methods; establish which legal entity makes and releases the batch; agree acceptance criteria and the document pack before payment; and fix the Incoterm, HS classification, and import route before dispatch. A quotation becomes a sourcing decision only when the batch record, the analytical evidence, and the landed cost describe the same material.

This guide is for laboratory procurement teams buying research peptides, not clinical supply or personal use. Peptides of China coordinates sourcing, orders, supplier communication, and delivery support; it does not manufacture peptides or operate an independent analytical testing laboratory.

## 1. Define the material before you ask for a price

A catalog name is not a specification. "BPC-157 10 mg" leaves the counterion, purity method, peptide content basis, and storage conditions unresolved. Two suppliers can quote the same three words and ship materials that behave differently in an assay.

Write a purchase brief with ten fields:

1. Compound name and amino acid sequence.
2. Modifications: acetylation, amidation, cyclization, pegylation, or none.
3. Salt or counterion form: acetate, trifluoroacetate, hydrochloride, or another declared form.
4. Presentation: milligrams per vial, number of vials per pack, fill volume or fill mass.
5. Purity basis: the chromatographic method, wavelength, gradient, and integration rule used to state a percentage.
6. Peptide content basis: amino acid analysis, nitrogen determination, or another stated method.
7. Appearance and solubility requirements that matter to the assay.
8. Water content and residual solvent limits, where relevant.
9. Storage and shipping condition: dry ice, gel pack, or ambient, with a temperature range.
10. Documentation required at delivery and the acceptance tests the buyer will run.

Separate requirements from preferences. If the project cannot start without a batch-specific record, a chromatogram, or a stated counterion, say so before the quote. A requirement raised after payment is a request, not a condition.

The specification should carry a version number and a date. When a supplier changes a method or a limit, the old version stays in the procurement file so the change can be seen. For the document side of this step, the [peptide documentation package](/blog/peptide-documentation-package/) checklist maps each record to the question it answers.

## 2. Choose a channel and name the responsible party

Four supply models appear in the China peptide market, and they carry different evidence trails:

- **Synthesis site.** The entity that runs the synthesis, purification, and lyophilization, and issues the batch record.
- **Contract manufacturer or toll synthesizer.** Makes material for another company's label; the brand owner may hold the specification.
- **Trading company.** Buys and resells; may not control the production site or the batch record.
- **Sourcing coordinator or platform.** Places orders with suppliers and manages communication, without manufacturing.

None of these roles is disqualifying. An intermediary that states its role and can obtain batch-linked records is easier to work with than a manufacturer that cannot answer a technical question. The failure mode is an unclear role.

Ask five questions before the first order:

1. What is the legal name and registered address of the entity that will invoice us?
2. Which site synthesized, purified, and filled this batch?
3. Which organization issued the certificate of analysis, and who signed it?
4. Can you provide a batch record extract and a deviation summary for the lot?
5. If you are not the manufacturer, what written authorization do you have to share the manufacturer's records?

Record the answers in the supplier file. A verbal assurance that "we are the factory" is not evidence of which site produced a vial.

A requirement outside the catalog is not an accepted custom-sourcing commitment until both parties confirm it in writing.

## 3. Build the document pack before the quote

Documents serve different purposes: a specification defines the material, a certificate reports what a sample was found to be, a batch record shows how the lot was made, and an invoice declares what crosses a border. Treating all four as paperwork loses the distinctions that make them useful.

| Document | Issuer | What it establishes | Frequent gap |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Specification | Buyer and supplier, agreed in writing | Material, methods, and numeric limits | Links to a catalog page that can change without notice |
| Certificate of analysis | Manufacturer or contract laboratory | Results attributed to a stated batch | No sample identity, method detail, or acceptance limit |
| Batch production record extract | Manufacturer | How the batch was made, tested, and released | Summary only; no equipment, yield, or deviation entries |
| Label proof | Manufacturer or filler | Presentation, lot code, storage, and caution statement | Lot code differs from the COA |
| Packing list | Supplier | Vials per carton, net and gross weight, dimensions | Quantity does not match the invoice |
| Commercial invoice | Seller | Seller, buyer, value, Incoterm, and origin | Generic description such as "peptide, 10 mg" |
| Origin statement | Manufacturer or chamber of commerce | Country of origin for duty and marking | Unsigned, or inconsistent with the invoice |
| Import permit or licence | Buyer, where the destination requires one | Authority to import the material | Assumed to be the seller's responsibility |

A certificate of analysis should carry at least these fields: product name, sequence, batch or lot number, manufacturing date, retest or expiry date, presentation and quantity, each test with its method, the acceptance limit, the result and unit, a release statement, the issuer's name and address, and the signature or electronic approval with a date. A certificate missing the batch number, the method, or the acceptance limit cannot support an acceptance decision.

Keep manufacturer-issued results and buyer-commissioned results in separate files. A laboratory report belongs to your sample, not to the supplier's batch, and that distinction matters if a dispute turns on which vial was tested.

## 4. Write acceptance criteria a laboratory can test

An acceptance criterion needs three parts: the attribute, the method, and the decision rule. "High purity" is not a criterion. "At least 98.0% area by the stated RP-HPLC method, with identity confirmed by LC-MS within the validated mass tolerance" is a criterion a laboratory can accept or reject.

| Attribute | Method to name in the specification | Example release rule | What the result does not prove |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Identity | LC-MS intact mass, or MALDI-TOF with stated mass accuracy | Observed mass within the validated tolerance of theoretical mass | Sequence order or purity |
| Purity | RP-HPLC with stated column, gradient, wavelength, and integration threshold | At least 98.0% area at the specified wavelength | Peptide mass fraction; water and counterion are outside the UV response |
| Peptide content | Amino acid analysis or nitrogen determination | Within the range written into the specification | Identity |
| Counterion | Ion chromatography for acetate or trifluoroacetate | Matches the declared salt form | Absence of residual solvents |
| Water | Karl Fischer titration | Within the specification limit | Residual solvent content |
| Endotoxin | LAL method with inhibition and enhancement controls | Below the limit set for the research use | Sterility |
| Sterility | Membrane filtration with 14-day incubation | No growth under the stated method | Endotoxin content |

The numbers are project decisions. A reference standard may need a tighter purity limit and a content assay; a screening compound may need identity and a broader limit. The method and the number belong in the purchase order.

Sampling deserves the same treatment. Record how many vials were drawn, which vial numbers were tested, and which were retained. A workable default is to test one vial per lot and retain two: one unopened for confirmatory testing, one for a working aliquot. If the lot is large or the supplier is new, increase the sample and state the sampling plan in the order.

Where possible, send a coded sample with the specification sheet so the laboratory does not know the supplier. The [verification protocol](/blog/china-peptide-manufacturer-verification-a-2026-b2b-sourcing-protocol/) covers how to keep the analytical record tied to the batch without revealing the commercial context.

## 5. Calculate landed cost per supplier order

A per-vial price is not a cost. Build the comparison by supplier order, because minimums, discounts, and shipping are usually assessed at that level.

| Cost line | Include in the comparison | Frequent omission |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Goods | Unit price multiplied by vials, normalized to milligrams | Comparing pack prices without normalizing to mg |
| International freight | Carrier charge, fuel surcharge, remote-area fee | Assuming one parcel covers a multi-supplier order |
| Insurance | Cargo cover at the level the Incoterm requires | Assuming the seller's cover matches your risk |
| Duty and tax | HS-based duty, VAT or GST, excise where applicable | Using the seller's declared value as the duty base |
| Clearance | Broker fee, storage, examination, and demurrage | Free-time limits at the destination |
| Payment and FX | Provider fee, conversion spread, chargeback risk | Treating a token transfer as settled payment |
| Testing | Sampling, courier to the laboratory, assays | Testing only after the lot has been released |
| Rejection | Return freight, replacement lead time, study delay | Assuming a refund covers the schedule loss |

On Peptides of China's public catalog, the minimum is $600 in goods per supplier order, and shipping does not count toward it. A qualifying new buyer may use one below-minimum supplier order as a first-order trial; every other supplier order in the same request must reach its own minimum or be removed. A 5% discount applies when a supplier order's gross goods subtotal reaches $3,500. Shipping is excluded, and spending across suppliers is not combined. Two supplier subtotals of $200 do not become a qualifying $400 order.

The $10 processing deposit starts manual handling and stock verification and is credited toward the final invoice. It is not a payment for analytical documentation and does not establish that a lot passed testing. Express shipping is quoted at $97–127 per parcel, with transit estimated at 7–12 days from carrier handover. Confirm the parcel count before payment: a request spanning several suppliers can produce several shipments, each with its own tracking record.

Add the cost of a rejected lot: assay time, repeated study, and a delayed milestone usually exceed the goods price.

## 6. Fix Incoterms and the customs entry before dispatch

Incoterms allocate cost, risk, and clearance duties; they do not transfer title or override the sale contract. Use the 2020 rules and name the place precisely. "DDP China" or "FOB Shanghai" without a named port or premises is incomplete.

- **EXW** places the goods at the seller's premises. The buyer handles export clearance, main carriage, and import.
- **FCA** has the seller deliver to the carrier at the named place. The buyer controls the main carriage and can use its own freight account.
- **CPT** has the seller pay carriage to the destination, but risk passes when the goods are handed to the first carrier. Insurance is the buyer's decision.
- **CIP** adds seller-arranged insurance at Institute Cargo Clauses A, the widest of the standard clauses.
- **CIF** is limited to sea and inland waterway transport and requires only Institute Cargo Clauses C, a narrower cover. Do not use CIF for an air shipment.
- **DAP** has the seller deliver to the named destination; the buyer clears import and pays duty.
- **DDP** has the seller clear import and pay duty. Confirm that the seller can legally act as importer in the destination country before relying on it.

For a first shipment, FCA or CIP with a named airport gives the buyer more control than DDP, where the seller's broker may classify the goods without consulting the importer.

Classification is a separate decision. Peptides do not share one HS code. Heading 2937 covers hormones and chain-modified polypeptides used primarily as hormones. Structure-specific peptides may fall in Chapter 29 subheadings such as 2930, 2933, or 2934, with 2942 as a basket for organic compounds not covered elsewhere. A lyophilized compound sold as a bare material often sits in Chapter 29; the same compound packaged as a prepared laboratory reagent may move to 3822, and a product put up in measured doses as a medicament may move to 3004.

Apply the General Rules of Interpretation in order, starting with GRI 1: the heading text and chapter notes. "Research use only" is a marketing and handling statement, not a classification. For a new SKU at volume, a binding ruling from the destination customs authority removes ambiguity and gives the broker a document to cite. The [HS code guide](/blog/hs-code-for-peptides/) covers the heading logic and ruling process in more detail.

The entry pack should contain the commercial invoice, packing list, air waybill, batch certificate, origin statement, import permit where required, insurance certificate, and proof of payment. The goods description on the invoice should include the sequence or a chemical descriptor, rather than a trade name alone. Mismatched descriptions across the invoice, packing list, and certificate are a common cause of holds; the [customs clearance guide](/blog/research-peptide-customs-clearance-a-professional-b2b-sourcing-guide-2026/) sets out a document-by-document check.

## 7. Receive, reconcile, and retain

Receipt is a reconciliation event, not a release decision. Check the parcel against the packing list, count the vials, photograph the labels and any damage, and record the temperature indicator or logger reading before opening the cartons. Weigh the shipment if the packing list gives a net weight; a large difference is a reason to stop and document.

Reconcile four identifiers: the lot code on the vial label, the lot code on the certificate, the lot code on the invoice or packing list, and the lot code in the batch record extract. If the label uses a different internal code, ask for a written cross-reference. Do not edit the label or the certificate to make the numbers agree.

Record the receipt in a single file: purchase order, accepted revision, invoice, payment confirmation, shipping record, certificate, label photographs, sample log, and the laboratory acceptance report. Keep the receipt record separate from the analytical release. A parcel can arrive complete and still contain material that fails identity or purity.

Retention is part of the record. Keep at least two vials per lot: one unopened, stored at the labeled condition, typically -20 °C and protected from light, and one working aliquot. Retain them for the study duration plus 12 months, or longer under the sponsor's quality system. Label each vial with the lot, the date received, and the study code.

If the shipment is short, damaged, or mislabeled, open a discrepancy record the same day. State the affected item, the expected and received quantity, the identifiers, and the supporting photographs. Keep the affected material identifiable and do not release it to the bench while the issue is open. Shipping errors, order errors, and analytical nonconformities follow different resolution paths; the [FAQ](/faq/) describes the commercial process, not a universal replacement promise.

## 8. Re-qualify the source for repeat orders

A successful first order qualifies a product, a site, a batch, and a specification version. It does not qualify every item from that supplier forever. Carry the successful requirements forward, then recheck the facts that can change: site, synthesis route, presentation, documentation, commercial terms, and shipping route.

Set re-verification triggers in the supplier file:

- A new production site or a new fill site.
- A change in synthesis route, resin, cleavage method, or purification method.
- A new presentation, vial size, or counterion.
- A specification revision or a new analytical method.
- Twelve months since the last document review or audit.
- Two consecutive lots with an out-of-trend assay, a reconciliation gap, or an unexplained certificate change.

Keep a simple scorecard by supplier: on-time dispatch, document completeness at first request, certificate-to-label concordance, discrepancy closure time, assay pass rate, and repeat-order lead time. The [bulk order minimum guide](/blog/bulk-peptide-order-minimums-2026-research-lab-guide/) explains how order size interacts with these checks, and the [logistics guide](/blog/peptide-sourcing-logistics-a-professional-guide-to-direct-to-manufacturer-procurement/) covers the transport side.

For current catalog availability, order handling, and the terms that apply to a specific request, use the [ordering process](/how-it-works/) rather than an old quotation. Commercial terms change; the accepted revision on the order is the version that controls.

## The Author's Take

**Position.** The buyer's advantage in China peptide sourcing sits in the specification and the acceptance test, not in the negotiation email.

**Reasoning.** A discount on an undocumented lot moves risk to the laboratory. The cost of a failed assay, a repeated study, and a missed milestone usually exceeds the per-vial difference that prompted the switch. A supplier that can produce a batch-linked record, a method, and a numeric limit is often cheaper on a landed, accepted-material basis, even when the quote is higher.
