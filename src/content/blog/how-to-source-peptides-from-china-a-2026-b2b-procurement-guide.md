Sourcing peptides from China requires decisions that extend beyond selecting a compound and comparing prices. The buyer needs a defined material, an appropriate supply channel, clear acceptance criteria, a workable delivery route, and a record connecting the request to the goods received. This guide follows that process from project requirements to receipt and review. It is written for laboratory research procurement, not clinical supply or personal use. Peptides of China (PFC) coordinates sourcing, orders, supplier communication, and delivery support. PFC does not manufacture the products or independently test them. Step 1: Write a purchase brief the supplier can answer Start with the research requirement rather than a headline discount. Identify the catalog product, required quantity, supplied presentation, and the characteristics your laboratory needs to evaluate. Where relevant, clarify the exact material identity and how stated content is expressed. Separate essential requirements from preferences. If the project cannot proceed without a particular record, analytical result, or traceability detail, make that a requirement before submitting the request. Do not assume it will become available after payment. A concise purchase brief should cover: The specific catalog item and quantity. The applicable specification and any unresolved technical questions. Documentation needed for the laboratory's acceptance decision. The destination and any receiving constraints. The required arrival window, including time for receipt checks and testing. PFC's public ordering process concerns available catalog products. A requirement outside the catalog should not be treated as an accepted custom-sourcing commitment. Step 2: Review the source and the evidence Establish whether the commercial counterparty is the manufacturer, a sourcing coordinator, or a reseller, and what is known about the production source. Compare offers on the evidence available for the actual material, not on an assumption that one geographic origin or business label guarantees quality. Keep three records distinct: the product specification, any manufacturer-issued analytical information, and independent testing commissioned by the buyer. The specification defines the stated acceptance criteria. A report records findings attributed to a sample or batch. Neither a generic certificate nor a report from a different batch establishes the properties of the shipment you will receive. PFC's documentation approach centers on applicable product specifications. Manufacturer-side information may vary by item and batch; do not assume a universal seller-provided third-party COA package. Decide whether the available information meets your project requirements and whether independent testing is needed. For the detailed document review, use our manufacturer-verification protocol . Step 3: Build the budget by supplier Compare the full procurement cost: goods, shipping, payment-provider or network charges where applicable, and any buyer-arranged testing. Include destination charges if they apply to the shipment. A lower catalog price does not necessarily mean a lower cost for material accepted into your project. Under PFC's current public-catalog terms, the minimum is $300 in goods per supplier order; shipping does not count. A qualifying new buyer may use one below-minimum supplier order as a first-order trial. Other supplier orders in the same request must meet their own minimum or be removed. The full eligibility and trial rules are in the FAQ . A 5% discount applies when a supplier order's gross goods subtotal reaches $3,500. Shipping is excluded, and spending across suppliers is not combined. Existing orders retain their original terms; Pharma and pre-orders are outside these public-catalog rules. For example, two supplier subtotals of $200 do not become a qualifying $400 supplier order. Budgeting by supplier prevents a combined request total from obscuring a shortfall. Confirm actual product quantities and package formats in the catalog rather than assuming every item follows one universal configuration. Step 4: Submit the request and allow for availability checks Submit the order through the website so the product selection, destination, and payment records stay connected. A submitted request is not the same as confirmed stock or a dispatched shipment. The $10 processing deposit initiates manual request handling and stock verification and is credited toward the final invoice. Follow the current instructions attached to the order. The deposit is not a purchase of analytical documentation or proof that the goods have passed independent testing. If availability changes, review the revised selection before proceeding. Check whether removing an item affects the project, the supplier subtotal, or the shipping arrangement. Do not assume that an alternative compound, presentation, or supplier is interchangeable with the original requirement. Keep the accepted revision with the order record. This gives both parties a clear reference if the final invoice or received goods differ from the initial request. Step 5: Review the invoice and payment instructions together Before final payment, check products, quantities, supplier allocation, goods subtotals, shipping, discount treatment, and credits already applied. Resolve discrepancies before paying rather than relying on an informal expectation of a later adjustment. Use the order's current payment section for the payment method, asset, network, receiving address, and amount due. A network name and an asset name are different fields: native SOL is not USDC on Solana. Do not reuse an address or payment amount from another order. Consult the current FAQ payment guidance for supported routes and provider-specific instructions. A provider's availability or fees may differ by account and location. Retain the transaction reference, but distinguish a transfer initiated at a provider from a payment credited to the order. Review the applicable cancellation terms before final payment. Do not assume that a confirmed order can later be cancelled merely because the research schedule changes. Step 6: Confirm the delivery plan before dispatch Shipping options, charges, and estimates depend on the supplier, destination, and parcel. A request spanning several suppliers may involve separate shipments. Ask which items belong to each shipment and keep their tracking records separate. Confirm the receiving address, any delivery restrictions, and the documentation required for the destination. Where import requirements are unclear, obtain appropriate advice from the destination authority or a qualified customs professional before proceeding. A research-use label is not a substitute for determining the applicable requirements. Treat dispatch, carrier handover, customs processing, and delivery as different milestones. Build the project schedule around the confirmed estimate and an appropriate contingency, not the date the request was submitted. For temperature or handling requirements, use product-specific information rather than assuming one shipping condition suits every peptide. Step 7: Receive, reconcile, and assess the material At receipt, compare the contents against the accepted order and shipping record. Record counts, visible condition, labels, and available batch identifiers. Preserve packaging and useful photographs if there is damage or a discrepancy. Keep receipt separate from laboratory acceptance. Where the project requires analytical verification, follow the laboratory's sampling and testing plan before releasing the material for that work. Buyer-directed testing should address the relevant specification and record the identity of the sample submitted. PFC does not perform that independent testing. If something differs, describe the issue precisely: the affected item, expected and received quantity, identifiers, and supporting observations or results. Keep affected material identifiable while the issue is investigated. Shipping problems, order errors, and analytical nonconformities may require different review processes; none should be collapsed into an assumed universal refund or replacement promise. Step 8: Close the order and improve the next one Retain the purchase brief, accepted revision, invoice, payment confirmation, shipping record, received identifiers, available documents, and acceptance decision in one procurement file. Note what caused delay or required clarification. For repeat purchases, carry forward the successful requirements but recheck changing facts: current availability, specifications, documentation, commercial terms, and delivery options. Familiarity with a supplier should make the process more efficient without replacing verification of the next order.
