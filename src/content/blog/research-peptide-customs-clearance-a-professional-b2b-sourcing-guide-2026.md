Research-peptide customs clearance is destination-specific. The safest general rule is to use accurate descriptions and current documentation rather than relying on concealment, a generic label, or an assumed tariff classification. This article is a procurement checklist, not legal or customs advice. Start with an accurate document set Use a commercial invoice and packing information that match the material, quantity, sender, recipient, and declared value. Include a safety data sheet or other document when required for the material and destination. Keep the batch or lot identifier consistent across the label, order record, and any analytical document supplied for review. State the research-only purpose accurately and do not describe a research material as an approved medicine or consumer product. Classification and destination review Chemical classification, import forms, duties, and exemptions depend on the destination, material, and current rules. Do not assume that a single HS code, TSCA statement, FDA position, or EU rule applies to every peptide or jurisdiction. Before dispatch, the buyer should confirm the current requirements with the relevant customs authority or a qualified customs broker. Avoid concealment and documentation mismatch “Stealth shipping,” mis-manifesting, undervaluing, and labeling a material as something else create avoidable compliance and traceability risks. Neutral outer packaging, where permitted, does not replace accurate declarations or required documents. A direct sourcing route cannot guarantee customs clearance. PFC role and order details PFC coordinates manufacturer-side sourcing and international logistics; it is not a manufacturer, customs authority, legal adviser, or independent testing laboratory. Manufacturer-side analytical documentation may be available depending on the product and batch. The current PFC product minimum is USD 600, excluding shipping. Multiple catalog products may be combined to reach the minimum, with standard ordering beginning at one same-product kit or box of 10 vials. The USD 10 processing deposit begins manual availability verification and is credited toward the final invoice. Shipping options, pricing, and estimated transit times vary by destination and are confirmed during order processing. Questions to resolve before dispatch Which authority and customs broker govern the destination? Which identity, end-use, safety, and import documents are currently required? Do the invoice, packing information, labels, and batch records describe the same material? Who is responsible for responding if customs requests additional information?
