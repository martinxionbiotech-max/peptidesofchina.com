## Why This Compound

Native relaxin-2 is a heterodimer. An A chain and a B chain are held together by two interchain disulfides, with a third disulfide inside the A chain. The B chain carries the receptor-binding surface; the A chain mostly provides structural stability. Producing that architecture recombinantly or synthetically means folding two chains and hoping the disulfides pair correctly. The failure mode is a mixture of scrambled isomers that can be chromatographically close to the product and biologically inert.

B7-33 removes that problem by design. The single chain carries the receptor-facing sequence and an internal connection that substitutes for the interchain bridge. The result is easier to synthesise, easier to purify, and easier to characterise by mass spectrometry, because there is only one chain to ionise. The trade-off is that the linker becomes part of the pharmacophore: change its length or composition and the peptide may still bind, but the signalling profile can move.

Relaxin signalling is pleiotropic. In recombinant cell systems, RXFP1 activation raises cAMP, recruits β-arrestin, and activates ERK and Akt depending on cell background and ligand dwell time. In fibrotic tissue models, the downstream readouts that matter are collagen synthesis, myofibroblast differentiation marked by α-smooth muscle actin, matrix metalloproteinase induction, and the balance between TIMPs and active MMPs. A peptide that produces the cAMP signal does not automatically produce the matrix effect, which is why the B7-33 literature tends to report several endpoints rather than one.

The published work on B7-33 spans cardiac ischaemia-reperfusion, renal fibrosis, and dermal scarring models, mostly in rodents, with supporting cell work in cardiac fibroblasts and proximal tubule cells. Some comparisons describe the mimetic as retaining antifibrotic activity while producing a smaller vasodilator response than two-chain relaxin. That is an interesting claim for study design, because it suggests the antifibrotic and vascular arms of RXFP1 signalling can be separated. It is also a claim that should be read in the primary papers, not in a supplier's bullet list, because the magnitude of the separation varies with model, route, and the relaxin comparator used.

For a laboratory, the practical consequence is that B7-33 perturbs one receptor axis with fewer confounding haemodynamic effects than the native hormone. That makes it useful in isolated fibroblast assays, ex vivo tissue baths, and rodent models where blood pressure is a readout. It does not make it interchangeable with relaxin-2 in every system; where the study depends on the two-chain conformation, the mimetic is a different ligand.

There is a nomenclature problem worth flagging early. "Relaxin" on a Chinese supplier's list can mean recombinant H2 relaxin, a B-chain fragment, a crude extract, or a synthetic mimetic. The names are not protected. A buyer who specifies "B7-33" without a sequence is relying on the supplier's internal definition, and the definition may change between batches. The sections below describe how to close that gap.

## What the Batch Record Should Show

Identity starts with mass, and for B7-33 the certificate has to supply the expected value because the catalogue does not. Ask for the construct description in writing: chain length, linker composition, N- and C-terminal groups, and any non-natural residues. Then ask for the observed mass from a deconvoluted LC-MS spectrum. The two numbers should agree within the method's tolerance, typically a few hundred parts per million for a peptide of this size.

The next check is chain integrity. A single-chain construct should show one main mass. A batch that shows a second species roughly 4 kDa lower, or a cluster consistent with two chains, is not the same molecule. Reduction with dithiothreitol followed by mass spectrometry will collapse any disulfide-linked species into linear chains and make the difference obvious. A certificate that reports only "conforms" without a spectrum does not let you run that check.

Purity by reversed-phase HPLC belongs on the record with its method. For a peptide of this size, a C18 column with a water/acetonitrile gradient and trifluoroacetic acid is standard, with detection at 214 nm. Column dimensions, particle size, flow rate, gradient slope, and integration threshold should be stated. Area normalisation at 214 nm counts peptide bonds, so it reports peptide mass rather than molar purity; that is acceptable for a catalogue specification but it will not reveal a co-eluting oxidised or deamidated form. Ask whether the impurity profile was examined by mass spectrometry as well.

Aggregation is the quiet failure mode. Relaxin-family peptides are amphipathic and can form soluble oligomers at higher concentrations, particularly after freeze-thaw or during concentration in a centrifugal filter. Size-exclusion chromatography or dynamic light scattering on a reconstituted sample gives a direct answer. If the supplier cannot provide either, a laboratory can run its own SEC before committing an experiment, and should do so for any study that depends on monomeric ligand.

Counterion and peptide content turn fill mass into usable mass. Acetate is the usual salt for basic peptides; trifluoroacetate can remain from purification. Water content by Karl Fischer and a peptide-content assay by amino acid analysis or UV absorbance belong on the certificate. On a 6 mg fill, a few percent water and a counterion can shift the amount of peptide in the vial enough to matter for a concentration-response curve.

Endotoxin and sterility are separate claims. A lyophilized powder is not sterile unless the process and container closure support it, and "research grade" does not imply either. For primary cells or in vivo work, request limulus amebocyte lysate data and a sterility statement. The [endotoxin primer](/blog/what-are-endotoxins-why-they-matter/) explains why a low endotoxin figure can matter more than an extra point of HPLC purity.

Finally, batch linkage. The number printed on the vial has to appear on the certificate, and both should carry a synthesis date, a purification route, a fill date, and a retest date. A document that could describe any lot is a template. The [CoA reading guide](/blog/how-to-read-a-peptide-coa-a-researchers-guide-to-hplc-and-ms-reports/) takes each field in turn.

## Sourcing & Verification

The first question is not price. It is "what is the sequence, and is it the published B7-33 construct?" A manufacturer who has made the peptide can answer with a sequence and a mass. A trader will answer with a catalogue number and a purity figure. The difference shows up in the first email.

Ask for the certificate before the quotation, and read it against the construct description. Three fields do most of the work: observed mass, HPLC method, and peptide content. If the mass is absent, the rest of the document cannot identify the material. If the HPLC method is absent, the purity number is a claim without a measurement.

Ask where the batch was made. "Factory-direct" is a claim about the supply chain, not a document. The [manufacturer verification protocol](/blog/china-peptide-manufacturer-verification-a-2026-b2b-sourcing-protocol/) sets out the entity checks: business licence, production address, reactor scale, and whether the company that issued the certificate is the company that synthesised the peptide. For a peptide sold in 6 mg vials at a specialist price, the batch size is small enough that a single synthesis can serve many customers, which makes lot traceability more important rather than less.

Ask about the counterion and the fill. A 6 mg vial is a small fill, and small fills are less forgiving of weighing error. A certificate that states peptide content per vial, not just fill mass, tells you the supplier has thought about this. The [counterion note](/blog/role-of-counterions-in-peptide-stability/) explains the effect on mass and on solubility.

Ask about stability data. Relaxin-family peptides are prone to deamidation at asparagine residues and to oxidation if methionine is present. A supplier who has run a short accelerated study can say which degradants appeared. A supplier who has not will say "stable at −20 °C", which is a storage instruction rather than a stability result.

Finally, ask for a pilot vial. A single vial from the lot you will receive is the cheapest identity test available. Reconstitute it, run HPLC and MS in-house or through a contract lab, and compare the result with the certificate. The [quality-testing overview](/peptide-quality-testing/) sets out the test sequence and the limits of each method.

## Handling & Storage

Keep the powder at −20 °C, in a desiccator and away from light. Let the vial reach room temperature before opening; condensation on a cold cake is a direct route to hydrolysis. Wipe the outside of the vial, wait thirty minutes, and open in a dry space.

Reconstitute with water or a buffer that matches the assay, following the [reconstitution guide](/blog/peptide-lyophilization-reconstitution-guide-for-researchers/). Relaxin-family peptides are soluble at neutral pH, but the single-chain construct may aggregate at high concentration. Add the solvent gently down the wall, swirl, and stop when the solution is clear. Vortexing creates foam and exposes the peptide to the air-liquid interface, which is where aggregation starts. If the solution is cloudy, do not assume it is contaminated; check concentration and pH first, and consult the [solubility troubleshooting note](/blog/peptide-solubility-troubleshooting/).

Use low-binding polypropylene tubes for dilute stocks. Relaxin peptides adsorb to polystyrene and to some filter membranes, and the loss is proportionally larger at low concentration. For cell work, filter through a 0.22 µm polyethersulfone membrane and measure recovery with a UV reading at 280 nm if the construct contains tryptophan or tyrosine; if it does not, use a peptide assay rather than assuming full recovery.

Aliquot before freezing. A single freeze-thaw cycle is usually tolerable; repeated cycles are not. Reconstituted solutions should be kept at 2–8 °C and used within the working window established for the assay, not one copied from another peptide. If the study runs for weeks, prepare fresh aliquots from the lyophilized stock. The [handling hub](/peptide-handling-protocols/) covers buffer selection and freeze-thaw design.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is B7-33 the same as relaxin-2?**

No. Relaxin-2 is a two-chain hormone held together by interchain disulfides. B7-33 is a single-chain mimetic built from the relaxin-2 B-chain with an engineered connection that replaces the two-chain architecture. The pharmacology overlaps at RXFP1, but the molecules are different and their masses are different.

**Does the catalogue list a sequence or molecular weight?**

Not on the product page. That is why the certificate has to supply both. Ask for the construct description and the observed mass before ordering, and compare the mass with the published construct rather than with a generic "relaxin" value.

**Which receptor does B7-33 act on?**

The published work describes it as an RXFP1 agonist. RXFP1 signals through cAMP and, depending on cell background, through β-arrestin, ERK, and Akt. The downstream matrix effects are what most fibrosis studies measure, and they do not follow automatically from a cAMP readout.

**How should the lyophilized powder be stored?**

−20 °C, in a desiccator, away from light. Let the vial warm before opening to avoid condensation. Working solutions belong at 2–8 °C and should be used inside the window the assay establishes.

**Why does the certificate need a mass spectrum?**

Because the name does not define the molecule. A single-chain construct can differ from a two-chain contaminant or a truncated sequence by several kilodaltons, and a purity percentage will not distinguish them. A deconvoluted mass, with and without reduction, is the fastest identity check.

**Is the powder sterile?**

Not by default. Lyophilized research peptides are not sterile unless the process and container closure support a sterility claim. For cell work, request endotoxin data and a sterility statement, or filter the reconstituted solution through a 0.22 µm membrane.

**Can B7-33 be used in cell culture?**

Yes, in principle, with an endotoxin-tested preparation and a vehicle control. Relaxin-family peptides can adsorb to plastic and to filter membranes, so run a recovery check and use low-binding tubes. The vehicle matters: a buffer that changes pH or ionic strength can shift the aggregation state.

**Does B7-33 have a CAS number?**

The catalogue page does not state one. A CAS number would not, by itself, establish identity or purity; the sequence and the mass do. If a supplier quotes a CAS number, check that it belongs to the construct being sold rather than to relaxin-2 or a fragment.

**Position.** B7-33 is one of the few peptides in this catalogue where I would treat the sequence, not the purity number, as the primary specification. A 98% batch of the wrong construct is worse than a 95% batch of the right one, because the impurity profile can be cleaned up but the pharmacology cannot.
