## Why This Compound

GHRH is a 44-residue hypothalamic peptide that activates the class B G protein-coupled receptor GHRHR on anterior pituitary somatotrophs. Truncation studies through the 1980s and 1990s established that the first 29 residues carry the full receptor-binding and activation function; the remaining 15 residues contribute to stability and plasma binding rather than receptor contacts. Sermorelin is that minimal sequence, amidated at the C-terminus, with no substitutions. The practical consequence is a peptide that behaves like the native ligand at the receptor and like a short-lived substrate in plasma.

That combination is why the compound still appears in research catalogues. When a study needs a GHRH receptor agonist with a known, unmodified sequence, sermorelin is the cleanest control. When the question is how a modification changes duration or clearance, sermorelin is the baseline. Its susceptibility to DPP-IV is not a defect in that framing; it is the variable being measured. The cleavage site is the His²-Ala³ bond, and published plasma stability work puts the half-life in the range of roughly ten to twelve minutes. Analogues with substitutions at or near that bond — CJC-1295 without DAC being the common example — extend the window without changing the receptor.

The main laboratory readouts are cAMP accumulation in GHRHR-transfected cells and GH release from primary anterior pituitary cultures, usually measured by ELISA or radioimmunoassay. Because sermorelin is a weak antigen and a small peptide, immunoassay standardisation is a real issue: a kit calibrated against full-length GHRH may not quantify the 29-mer accurately. Researchers running quantitative work should confirm standard curve linearity against the actual peptide lot, not against the kit insert.

The acetate counterion matters more than buyers expect. Sermorelin is manufactured as the acetate salt because the free base is less soluble and less stable through lyophilization. Acetate content is therefore a release parameter, not an irrelevant detail. A batch with the right mass by LC-MS but an acetate content outside the specification can behave differently in reconstitution and in cell culture, where the counterion contributes to pH and ionic strength.

## What the Batch Record Should Show

A sermorelin batch record worth accepting has more than a purity number. The first item is the lot identity: batch number, synthesis date, scale, resin lot, and the purification method. Without those, a chromatogram cannot be tied to the vial in front of you.

The analytical package should include a reversed-phase HPLC method with column dimensions, mobile phase, gradient, flow rate, detection wavelength, and injection volume. Purity should be reported as area percent at the specified wavelength, with the integration method disclosed. A single "≥98%" line without a chromatogram is a marketing claim, not a release result. The impurity table matters more than the headline purity: closely eluting deletion sequences, truncated 28-mers, and oxidised species can sit under a broad peak and still pass an area threshold.

Identity should be confirmed by electrospray or LC-MS with observed and theoretical mass for the free base, plus a statement of the charge state used. For a 29-mer of this size, a low-resolution single quadrupole mass is adequate for identity but not for sequence confirmation. If the supplier claims sequence verification, ask for MS/MS fragmentation coverage or a peptide map with the expected fragments.

Three release tests are specific to this salt. Acetate content by ion chromatography should be stated as a range, not a vague "acetate salt." Water content by Karl Fischer should be below the lyophilization specification, because residual moisture drives hydrolysis during storage. Residual trifluoroacetic acid from HPLC purification should be reported if the material is intended for cell culture; TFA is cytotoxic at sufficient concentration and is a common hidden variable in poorly documented batches.

For cell-based work, add endotoxin and bioburden results. A peptide can be 99% pure by HPLC and still ruin a primary pituitary culture if it carries endotoxin. Endotoxin testing by kinetic chromogenic LAL, with the method detection limit stated, is the minimum. Sterility is a separate question and should not be inferred from a 0.22 µm filtration step during fill.

Finally, a mass balance calculation is a useful sanity check: peptide content by amino acid analysis or nitrogen determination, plus water, acetate, and residual solvents, should account for most of the vial mass. Suppliers rarely volunteer this, but it exposes under-filled vials and over-stated peptide content faster than a purity chromatogram does.

## Sourcing & Verification

Sermorelin is a 29-mer made by solid-phase peptide synthesis, purified by preparative HPLC, exchanged into acetate, and lyophilized. That is a mature process, and a competent Chinese peptide manufacturer can run it at gram to kilogram scale. The variation is not in whether the sequence can be made; it is in how much purification is done and how honestly the batch is characterized.

Buyers comparing Chinese suppliers should ask three questions before price. First, who owns the production site and what is the scope of its licence? A trading company with a catalog page is not a manufacturer. Second, can the supplier provide a batch-specific certificate rather than a template? Third, will they release the chromatogram and mass spectrum, or only a summary? The answers separate a factory from a reseller more reliably than a factory tour video.

The common failure modes for this compound are predictable. Incomplete deprotection during synthesis leaves deletion sequences that co-elute on a shallow gradient. Oxidation of methionine, if present in the sequence, adds a +16 Da species that a low-resolution mass may not resolve. Acetate exchange can be incomplete, leaving residual TFA or a mixed salt. Lyophilization of a dilute solution can produce a cake that collapses, which is cosmetic until it affects reconstitution and stability.

Verification should be independent of the supplier's own release testing. A small aliquot sent to a third-party laboratory for RP-HPLC and MS costs less than repeating a failed study. If the compound is destined for a regulated preclinical package, ask for the analytical method validation summary and the column lot; those details tell you whether the method is transferred or improvised. The [quality testing guide](/peptide-quality-testing/) sets out the full document set, and the [COA reading guide](/blog/how-to-read-a-peptide-coa-a-researchers-guide-to-hplc-and-ms-reports/) explains how to spot a report that has been assembled from a template.

One sourcing detail is specific to sermorelin: the 5 mg and 10 mg fill sizes are small, and fill accuracy matters. A 5 mg vial nominally contains 5 mg of peptide, but the label may refer to total peptide mass including counterion and water. Ask whether the fill is expressed as peptide content or as salt weight. The difference can be ten percent or more, which is enough to shift a standard curve.

## Handling & Storage

Lyophilized sermorelin should be stored at −20°C, dry and protected from light. Before opening, let the vial equilibrate to room temperature to prevent condensation on the cake. Condensation is not just a handling nuisance; it creates a local aqueous environment that accelerates hydrolysis every time the vial is reopened.

For reconstitution, use a buffer that matches the assay. Phosphate-buffered saline at pH 7.2–7.4 is standard for cell work. Sterile water is acceptable for stock solutions that will be diluted into buffered medium, but the unbuffered stock is less stable to pH drift. Avoid alkaline buffers above pH 8; the peptide is not uniquely labile there, but acetate exchange and deamidation rates increase. Add the buffer down the vial wall rather than onto the cake, and swirl gently. Vortexing creates foam and surface denaturation, and the loss is irreversible.

Stock concentration should be chosen so that the final dilution into medium is at least 1:100. That keeps the buffer contribution small and reduces adsorption losses. For dilute stocks, use low-binding polypropylene tubes and consider adding a carrier protein such as 0.1% bovine serum albumin for cell assays, provided the assay tolerates it. Sermorelin adsorbs to polystyrene and to some filter membranes; filtering a dilute stock through a 0.22 µm PVDF syringe filter can remove a meaningful fraction of the peptide. Filter the buffer first, then add the peptide.

Aliquot before freezing. A single freeze-thaw cycle is usually tolerable; five are not. Store aliquots at −20°C for working stocks and −80°C for longer periods, and discard any aliquot that has been thawed and refrozen. The catalogue's reconstituted stability figure of up to 14 days at 4°C is a reasonable planning limit for a preserved, sterile stock, not a guarantee for an unbuffered solution in a bench rack.

Sermorelin sits at one end of the GHRH analogue spectrum: native sequence, no albumin binding, rapid DPP-IV cleavage. The ipamorelin row is the one researchers sometimes miss. Ipamorelin is not a GHRH analogue; it acts at the ghrelin receptor and releases GH through a different hypothalamic pathway. Using it as a "GHRH comparator" is a category error even though both compounds appear in growth-hormone research catalogues. If the experiment is designed to isolate GHRHR signalling, sermorelin and CJC-1295 without DAC are the relevant pair. The [DAC versus no-DAC comparison](/blog/dac-vs-no-dac/) explains why the albumin-binding modification changes the pharmacokinetic question rather than the receptor question.

Tesamorelin is the other useful comparator, because it retains the full 44-residue sequence and adds an N-terminal fatty acid that slows DPP-IV cleavage without albumin binding. For a study on receptor occupancy, the difference between sermorelin and tesamorelin is largely a difference in exposure duration. For a study on sequence-specific antibody binding, the 15 extra residues matter and the two are not interchangeable.

## The Author's Take

**Position.** Sermorelin is a comparator, not a workhorse. If a laboratory needs a sustained GHRHR signal across a multi-hour assay, this is the wrong peptide, and no amount of careful handling will fix the pharmacokinetics.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the difference between sermorelin and CJC-1295 Without DAC?**

Both are 29-amino-acid GHRH analogues that activate GHRHR. Sermorelin is the unmodified native GHRH(1-29) sequence and is cleaved rapidly by DPP-IV at the His²-Ala³ bond. CJC-1295 without DAC carries four substitutions that slow that cleavage, so it produces a longer exposure window at the same receptor. The practical difference is duration, not receptor selectivity. A study that needs a native-sequence reference uses sermorelin; a study that needs a longer GHRH signal uses the modified analogue.

**Why is sermorelin supplied as the acetate salt?**

The acetate salt is the standard pharmaceutical and research form because it is more soluble and more stable through lyophilization than the free base. The counterion also affects pH and ionic strength after reconstitution, which is why acetate content belongs in the release specification. A batch that is chemically identical by mass but has a different acetate content is not automatically interchangeable in a cell-based assay.

**How is sermorelin verified for purity and identity?**

Purity is measured by reversed-phase HPLC with a disclosed method and an impurity table, not just a single area-percent figure. Identity is confirmed by mass spectrometry against the theoretical mass of the free base, and sequence confirmation requires MS/MS coverage or a peptide map. For cell work, endotoxin and bioburden results should be requested separately. The [quality testing pillar](/peptide-quality-testing/) explains the document hierarchy.

**Is sermorelin stable after reconstitution?**

The catalogue figure is up to 14 days at 4°C for a reconstituted stock, which is a planning limit rather than a universal guarantee. Stability depends on buffer, pH, concentration, and microbial control. Unbuffered solutions and dilute stocks degrade faster. Aliquot and freeze what will not be used within the working window, and avoid repeated freeze-thaw cycles.

**Can sermorelin be used in cell culture?**

Yes, in GHRHR-expressing cell models and primary pituitary cultures. The practical constraints are endotoxin, acetate load, and adsorption to plastic. Use a buffer-matched stock, keep the final medium dilution at 1:100 or greater, and run a vehicle control at the same acetate and buffer concentration. The [handling guide](/peptide-handling-protocols/) covers the general workflow.

**What should I check on a sermorelin certificate of analysis?**

Check the lot number, the HPLC method and chromatogram, the mass spectrum with observed and theoretical mass, acetate content, water content, and endotoxin if the material is for cell work. A certificate that lists only "purity ≥98%" and a retest date is incomplete. The [COA guide](/blog/how-to-read-a-peptide-coa-a-researchers-guide-to-hplc-and-ms-reports/) has a field-by-field checklist.

**Does the counterion affect HPLC purity?**

It can affect retention time and peak shape, but it should not be counted as purity. A salt-form change alters the mass and the chromatographic behaviour, so a method validated for the acetate salt may not be directly transferable to a trifluoroacetate batch. Ask which counterion the release method was validated against.
