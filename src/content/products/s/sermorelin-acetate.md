## Compound Profile

Sermorelin is a synthetic 29-amino acid peptide corresponding to the biologically active N-terminal fragment of endogenous human growth hormone-releasing hormone (GHRH), specifically GHRH(1–29). The full-length native GHRH is 44 amino acids, but the first 29 residues contain the complete receptor-binding domain required for GHRH receptor (GHRHR) activation — a finding established by systematic truncation studies in pituitary cell models. Sermorelin was the first synthetic GHRH analogue to complete clinical development for paediatric GH deficiency — later withdrawn for commercial rather than safety reasons — providing a body of human pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic data uncommon for research peptides. The key pharmacokinetic limitation of sermorelin relative to its structural successors is its short plasma half-life of approximately 10–12 minutes, attributable to rapid cleavage at the His²-Ala³ bond by dipeptidyl aminopeptidase IV (DPP-IV). This sensitivity makes it a useful negative pharmacological control in DPP-IV inhibition studies, and underlies the development of the four-substitution analogue CJC-1295 Without DAC (Modified GRF 1-29), which confers DPP-IV resistance while retaining the same receptor pharmacology. In research comparing GHRH analogue half-life and GH pulse kinetics, sermorelin serves as the short-acting reference compound.
