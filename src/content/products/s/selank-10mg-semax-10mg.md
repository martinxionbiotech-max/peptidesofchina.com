A Selank and Semax blend is two heptapeptides in one vial, and that single fact drives every question worth asking about it. Selank ends in a C-terminal amide and carries a Lys-Arg pair; Semax ends in a free acid and carries an N-terminal methionine that oxidises on the bench. Their masses differ by 62.08 Da, their net charges differ, and their analytical handles differ. A certificate that reports one purity figure for the mixture tells you almost nothing about whether either peptide is present at the stated ratio, whether the methionine survived lyophilisation, or whether the two components were ever mixed at all. This page sets out what a blend record should contain and where a combination product helps a study.

## Why This Compound

The two peptides share an origin and a stabilising tail, and little else. Both came out of the Institute of Molecular Genetics in Moscow, and both were built by attaching Pro-Gly-Pro to a shorter parent: tuftsin for Selank, the ACTH(4-7) melanocortin core for Semax. The tail was a half-life fix, meant to slow cleavage by circulating peptidases, and it is why the two are often discussed together in Russian reviews.

The research programmes diverged after that. Selank's literature sits in peptidase inhibition and GABAergic gene expression. Kost and colleagues reported that Selank and Semax inhibited enkephalin-degrading enzymes in human serum, a cell-free effect that has been proposed as one route to the peptide's behavioural readouts (Kost et al., 2001). Filatova and colleagues exposed IMR-32 neuroblastoma cells to Selank and measured transcript changes across a panel of GABA-transmission genes, finding shifts that differed from those produced by olanzapine (Filatova et al., 2017). An earlier in vivo study by Volkova and colleagues reported changes in GABAergic gene expression in rat brain after exposure (Volkova et al., 2016). Those are three different systems, and none of them establishes a receptor-level mechanism.

Semax's literature is denser and leans on neurotrophins. Shadrina and colleagues found rapid induction of neurotrophin mRNAs in rat glial cultures (Shadrina et al., 2001). Dolotov and colleagues reported changes in BDNF and TrkB expression in rat hippocampus (Dolotov et al., 2006). Dmitrieva and colleagues used a cerebral ischaemia-reperfusion model and found that both Semax and the isolated Pro-Gly-Pro tripeptide altered transcription of neurotrophins and their receptor genes, which is an uncomfortable result for anyone who assumes the whole sequence is required (Dmitrieva et al., 2009). Filippenkov and colleagues later mapped a wider transcriptome response in the same model (Filippenkov et al., 2020).

Put the two literatures side by side and the case for a blend is not pharmacological. No paper shows that Selank and Semax act synergistically, and the proposed mechanisms are different enough that a combined exposure would be hard to interpret. The case is practical: one procurement for a laboratory that wants both tools, or a pilot screen that would rather not run two reconstitutions. That is a legitimate reason to buy a blend, not to expect it to behave like a single compound.

The structural difference matters more than the shared tail. Selank is amidated and carries a free N-terminus plus Lys and Arg, so it is strongly basic. Semax has two carboxylates, one on the Glu side chain and one at the C-terminus, against a single N-terminal amino group, so it is acidic at neutral pH and carries an oxidisable methionine at position one. In a mixture those facts create two analytical problems: proving the amide on one component and the oxidation state on the other. A single "≥98% by HPLC" line cannot do both.

## What the Batch Record Should Show

Start with the format question, because it changes everything downstream. A blend can be sold as one vial containing both lyophilised powders, or as a pack of separate vials. The catalogue lists 10 vials for both the 5 + 5 mg and 10 + 10 mg formats, and the label does not by itself settle whether each vial holds 10 mg total or 10 mg of each peptide. Ask before ordering. If the vial contains 20 mg of solids, a concentration calculated as if it contained 10 mg will be wrong by a factor of two, and no certificate will warn you.

If the material is co-lyophilised, the certificate needs a ratio assay, not just a purity assay. Reversed-phase HPLC at 214 nm will resolve two peaks if the gradient is shallow enough, but the two peptides are both basic heptapeptides with similar hydrophobicity, and a generic 10-minute gradient can merge them into one broad peak. Ask for the chromatogram. Two baseline-resolved peaks with integration events on both are the minimum evidence that the product is a mixture. One peak is evidence of something else.

Ratio by peak area is not ratio by mass. The two molecules have different molar absorptivities at 214 nm, different counterion loads, and different water content. A ratio calculated from raw area percentages without response factors can be off by several percentage points. The certificate should state whether the ratio was determined against reference standards of each peptide, or by area normalisation alone. The second is a rough check, not a specification.

Identity needs two masses, reported separately. Selank free peptide averages 751.86 Da; the singly protonated ion sits near m/z 752.87. Semax averages 813.94 Da; its protonated ion sits near m/z 814.95. A certificate that reports a single "MW 783" has averaged the two and told you nothing. Look for two deconvoluted masses, each matching its peptide within a tight window, and look for the Semax mass to be reported as the free acid rather than a salt.

Oxidation belongs on the Semax side of the record. Methionine sulfoxide adds 15.99 Da, producing a satellite near 829.94 Da. On a shallow gradient the oxidised form often appears as a leading shoulder rather than a resolved peak, and area normalisation may fold it into the main peak. Ask for the oxidised fraction as a number and for the method used to obtain it. A supplier who understands the molecule will answer directly; a reseller will repeat the headline purity figure.

The Selank side needs the amide confirmed. The amidated and free-acid forms differ by about one dalton, and a low-resolution mass readout cannot distinguish them. Ask for the observed mass at high resolution, or for MS/MS coverage of the C-terminal residues. Without that, "Selank" on the label is a name, not a structure.

Counterion and content close the record. Acetate is the usual salt for both peptides, but residual trifluoroacetate from purification is common and contributes mass without contributing peptide. Karl Fischer water content matters twice over here: the powder is hygroscopic, and water promotes both hydrolysis and methionine oxidation. A peptide-content assay, by nitrogen analysis or amino-acid analysis, is what converts fill mass into molar amount. For a blend, that assay should be run on the mixture and, ideally, on each component before mixing.

Blend uniformity is the field most certificates omit. A co-lyophilised cake can be homogeneous at the top and enriched at the bottom if the fill was a suspension rather than a solution. Ask whether the two peptides were co-dissolved before lyophilisation, and whether homogeneity was checked across vials. If the answer is a blank look, the blend is probably a dry powder mix, which will separate further during shipping. ## Sourcing & Verification

Blends are the easiest product to fake and the hardest to audit, because a buyer cannot weigh the components. These questions are ordered by how quickly they separate a manufacturer from a repackager.

- Does the certificate show two distinct masses, or one averaged value?
- Is the ratio stated by mass or by peak area, and were response factors used?
- Is the Semax component the free acid, and is its methionine oxidation reported?
- Is the Selank component amidated, and is the C-terminal amide confirmed by mass?
- Does each vial contain the stated amount of each peptide, or the stated amount in total?
- Were the peptides co-dissolved before lyophilisation, and was uniformity checked?
- What is the counterion, and is residual TFA measured?
- What is the peptide content per vial, and by which method?
- Can the supplier release one vial as a pilot before a ten-vial pack?
- Is the batch number on the certificate the same as the batch number on the vial?

The format question catches most buyers. A listing that says "10 mg + 10 mg" and "10 vials" can mean ten vials of 20 mg total, ten vials of 10 mg total split evenly, or ten vials of 10 mg of each. Each reading implies a different concentration series, and the difference is a factor of two. Ask for the fill mass per vial and the declared composition as a table. If the supplier cannot produce that table, the blend is not specified well enough for a quantitative assay.

The second filter is the ratio method. A manufacturer who co-lyophilises a blend usually has an in-process check: an HPLC run of the bulk solution before filling, or a gravimetric check of the two weighed components. Ask which was used and what the acceptance window was. A ±10% window on a 1:1 blend is loose but honest; "exactly 1:1" with no method behind it is marketing.

The third filter is component documentation. Each peptide should have its own identity test, because the mixture assay cannot prove that the peak labelled Selank is Selank rather than a deletion sequence. Ask for the component certificates or the internal release data. A supplier who cannot name the synthesis site cannot answer that question.

Finally, check the pack logic against your study design. Ten vials of a blend is convenient for a repeated-measures design with ten subjects or ten time points, and awkward for a concentration-ranging study where each concentration needs a fresh vial. If the study needs single compounds, buy [Selank](/catalog/selank/) and [Semax](/catalog/semax/) separately; the blend will save handling time and cost you attribution. ## Handling & Storage

Store the lyophilised blend at −20°C, desiccated, and protected from light. Let a cold vial reach room temperature before opening. Condensation on a cold cake is a direct route to hydrolysis, and the Semax component starts oxidising at the surface once moisture and oxygen are present.

Reconstitution is usually water or a buffer matched to the assay. Add the solvent down the wall of the vial and swirl gently. Vortexing creates foam, and foam is an air-liquid interface where methionine oxidises faster. If the solution will sit for more than a few hours, purge the headspace with argon and keep it at 2-8°C. Avoid peroxide-containing buffers and trace metals; both accelerate the conversion to sulfoxide.

The components behave differently in solution, which is a handling problem as much as an analytical one. Selank is the more basic of the two and adsorbs to glass and some plastics at low concentration; use polypropylene tubes for dilute stocks and check recovery after filtration. Semax is less sticky but more fragile at its N-terminus. Neither benefits from repeated freeze-thaw, and a blend gives two degradation pathways in the same aliquot.

Aliquot before freezing. One freeze-thaw cycle is usually tolerable for either peptide; five are not. For powder storage, use a desiccator and a freezer without a frost-free defrost cycle. The catalogue storage line is a planning figure, not a stability study. A laboratory that needs a working window should establish it with a fresh preparation and an analytical check rather than assume the label applies to a vial opened six months ago.

For cell work, filter the reconstituted stock through a 0.22 µm membrane and check recovery. Endotoxin data is separate from purity and should be requested if the preparation will contact primary cells. Sterility is not automatic for a lyophilised powder. ## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does the 10 + 10 mg pack contain 20 mg per vial?**

That is the intended reading, but confirm it before ordering. Some listings describe the total fill, some describe each component, and some describe the sum across the pack. Ask for the fill mass per vial and the declared composition as a table. The difference is a factor of two in any concentration calculation.

**Why report two separate masses instead of one molecular weight?**

Because the product is two molecules. Selank averages 751.86 Da and Semax 813.94 Da, a difference of 62.08 Da. A single averaged value cannot confirm either component and cannot reveal an oxidised Semax fraction. Two deconvoluted masses, each matched to its peptide, are the minimum identity evidence for a blend.

**Is the Semax in this product the free acid or the amide?**

The catalogue Semax component is the free acid, sequence Met-Glu-His-Phe-Pro-Gly-Pro. The amidated version is a different product, NA Semax Amidate, and it also carries an N-terminal acetyl group. Confirm the form on the certificate before comparing the blend with a single-compound purchase.

**How much methionine oxidation is acceptable?**

There is no universal threshold. What matters is that the value is reported and that the method could have detected it. A certificate that shows a clean main peak with no leading shoulder and states the oxidised fraction is more useful than one that reports 99% purity with no oxidation field at all.

**Can the blend be used for cell-culture work?**

Only with a sterile, endotoxin-tested preparation. Filter the reconstituted stock through a 0.22 µm membrane and request LAL data if the cells are primary or the readout is sensitive to endotoxin. A lyophilised powder is not sterile by default, and a blend gives two opportunities for contamination.

**Does the blend have a published synergistic effect?**

No peer-reviewed study demonstrates synergy between Selank and Semax. The published work treats them as separate tools with different proposed mechanisms. A blend is a procurement convenience, not a pharmacological claim, and the study design should not assume otherwise.

## The Author's Take

**Position.** I would not buy a Selank and Semax blend for a mechanism study. I would buy it for a screening panel where both peptides are controls rather than the main variable.

**Reasoning.** A blend destroys attribution. If a neurotrophin readout moves, you cannot tell whether the Selank component, the Semax component, or the combination did it, and the literature gives you no basis for separating the two. The analytical record is harder as well: two masses, two counterions, one oxidation pathway, and a ratio that most certificates describe by peak area rather than by mass. The format ambiguity alone, whether 10 + 10 mg means 20 mg per vial or 10 mg total, can derail a concentration series. For a pilot screen that ranks a panel before a focused study, the blend saves handling time and attribution does not matter yet. That is a narrow use case, and I would rather state it plainly than pretend the combination is more than a convenience.
