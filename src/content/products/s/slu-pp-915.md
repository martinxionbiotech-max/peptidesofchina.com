The research interest is in transcriptional control, not in a consumer outcome. ERRα, ERRβ, and ERRγ are nuclear receptors that share the PGC-1α coactivator network and regulate genes involved in oxidative phosphorylation, fatty acid oxidation, and mitochondrial biogenesis. A small molecule that engages those receptors gives researchers a way to ask whether an ERR-dependent transcriptional shift is sufficient to change mitochondrial function in a defined model. That is a mechanistic question, and the answer depends on the quality of the tool as much as on the design of the experiment.

## Why This Compound

The estrogen-related receptors are orphan nuclear receptors: no endogenous ligand has been confirmed, and their activity is regulated largely by coactivator availability and post-translational modification. ERRα is expressed at high levels in oxidative tissues — skeletal muscle, heart, brown adipose tissue, and liver — and its target gene set overlaps heavily with the PGC-1α programme. When PGC-1α is induced by exercise or cold exposure, ERRα is one of the transcription factors that executes the downstream response. ERRγ has a similar role in the heart, where it controls fatty acid oxidation and mitochondrial content. ERRβ is less well characterised and appears to have developmental functions.

That biology made the ERR receptors attractive targets for chemical biology, but the field spent years without a selective, drug-like agonist. The SLU-PP series changed that. SLU-PP-332 was the first widely used pan-ERR agonist, and it required relatively high concentrations in cells and had limited oral exposure. SLU-PP-915 was developed as a follow-on with improved oral pharmacokinetics. The published characterisation describes it as an orally active ERR agonist that engages all three receptor subtypes and modulates the expression of ERR target genes in mouse tissues.

For a research buyer, the value of SLU-PP-915 is that it separates two variables that are usually tangled. Exercise, cold, and caloric restriction all induce PGC-1α and change mitochondrial function, but they also change dozens of other pathways. An ERR agonist lets a researcher ask what happens when the ERR node alone is engaged. The answer is model-dependent, and the literature is still small. That is precisely why batch quality matters: a null result from a poorly characterised tablet is uninterpretable.

The oral route is the other reason the compound appears in catalogues. Most peptide tools require cold chain and reconstitution; a tablet is stable at room temperature and easy to administer in rodent studies. That convenience is also a risk. A tablet is a manufactured dosage form with excipients, a dissolution profile, and a polymorph, and those variables can change exposure without changing the label.

## What the Batch Record Should Show

A tablet batch record is a manufacturing document, not a certificate of analysis with a purity number. It should begin with the batch formula: the API quantity per tablet, the identity and grade of every excipient, the binder, the disintegrant, the lubricant, and the coating system if the tablet is coated. For a 10 mg tablet, the API is a small fraction of the total mass, so blend uniformity is the first critical control point. A poorly blended batch can pass the average assay and fail content uniformity at individual tablet level.

The release tests should include assay by a validated HPLC method, content uniformity across a sample of tablets, and dissolution at a stated apparatus, medium, volume, and time point. Dissolution is the test that connects the tablet to the biology: two batches with the same assay can release the API at different rates if the particle size, polymorph, or excipient grade changes. Ask for the dissolution profile, not a single pass/fail result. A profile with a plateau at 85% in 30 minutes is more informative than a line that says "complies."

Related substances should be reported with the method and the limits. For a small molecule, the main degradation pathways are oxidation and hydrolysis, and the impurity profile can change with storage humidity. Residual solvents should be reported if the API is manufactured in a solvent-heavy process. Polymorph identity by X-ray powder diffraction is worth requesting for a compound with known crystalline forms, because a form change can alter solubility and dissolution.

The physical tests are part of the specification, not a quality afterthought. Hardness, friability, thickness, and disintegration time should be recorded. A tablet that is too soft will chip in the bottle and fail content uniformity at the end of the fill; a tablet that is too hard may not disintegrate within the dissolution window. Moisture content by Karl Fischer or loss on drying belongs in the record, especially if the bottle is marketed without a desiccant.

Microbial limits should be stated. A tablet is not sterile, and it does not need to be, but it should meet a defined microbial limit specification. For a research compound used in cell culture, that is not enough: the tablet excipients may be cytotoxic or may interfere with the assay. Researchers who need solution-phase exposure should prepare a filtered stock and run an excipient-only control.

## Sourcing & Verification

SLU-PP-915 is a small molecule, and small molecules have a different counterfeit profile from peptides. The API can be synthesized by many contract manufacturers, the tablet can be compressed by a separate facility, and the finished bottle can be labelled by a third party. The chain is longer than it looks, and each handoff is a chance for the API source to change without the label changing.

Start with the API. Ask who synthesized it, at what scale, and under what quality system. Ask for the certificate of analysis for the API lot, not just the finished tablet. The API certificate should include assay, related substances, residual solvents, water content, and a polymorph identification. If the tablet manufacturer buys API on the open market, the API lot may change between tablet batches, and the dissolution profile may drift.

Then ask about the tablet process. Was the blend prepared by high-shear granulation or direct compression? What is the tablet press, and what is the compression force range? Is the tablet coated, and if so, with what? These details determine whether the batch is reproducible. A supplier who cannot answer them is a packager, not a manufacturer.

Dissolution method transfer is the next question. The method should specify the apparatus, paddle speed, medium, pH, surfactant if any, sampling times, and detection. A method that uses a sinker or a non-standard medium may not be comparable to published work. If the supplier cannot provide a dissolution profile, ask for a disintegration test at minimum. A tablet that disintegrates in 15 minutes is not necessarily bioavailable, but one that does not disintegrate at all is a failed formulation.

Stability data should cover the shipping lane. A 24-month room-temperature stability study is ideal; a six-month accelerated study at 40°C/75% RH is a reasonable substitute for a research reagent. The study should measure assay, related substances, dissolution, and moisture at each time point. If the tablet is hygroscopic, the bottle and the desiccant are part of the stability story.

## Handling & Storage

Store the bottle at 20–25°C, dry, and protected from light. Do not refrigerate unless the label specifically says so; condensation on a cold tablet is a moisture source, and moisture drives hydrolysis and polymorph changes. Keep the desiccant in the bottle and reseal it immediately after removing a tablet. Do not store loose tablets in a bench vial.

Do not split tablets to adjust the amount. A scored tablet is not necessarily uniform across the halves, and an unscored tablet can crumble. If a study requires a smaller quantity, weigh whole tablets and prepare a suspension or a solution, then verify the concentration analytically. For cell work, crush and dissolve a known number of tablets in a defined volume of buffer or medium, filter through a 0.22 µm membrane, and assay the filtrate. The excipients may not fully dissolve; that is expected, and the filter removes them.

For rodent diet admixture or oral gavage studies, prepare the suspension fresh and record the vehicle, the mixing time, and the particle size. A suspension that settles between doses will deliver a different amount at the end of the session. Use a homogeniser or a mortar and pestle, and keep the suspension stirred during use. The [handling guide](/peptide-handling-protocols/) covers general laboratory preparation, but the specific vehicle compatibility should be checked in a pilot batch.

Light protection matters for many small molecules, and the catalogue does not specify the photostability of this one. Keep the bottle in its original carton or in an amber container. If a solution is prepared for an assay, protect it from direct light and use it within the same day unless a stability study supports a longer hold.

**What is SLU-PP-915?**

SLU-PP-915 is a synthetic small-molecule agonist of the estrogen-related receptors ERRα, ERRβ, and ERRγ. It is used as a laboratory tool to study ERR-dependent transcriptional programmes, particularly in mitochondrial and oxidative metabolism research. It is not a therapeutic product.

**How does SLU-PP-915 differ from SLU-PP-332?**

Both are pan-ERR agonists from the same chemical series. SLU-PP-332 is the earlier tool compound and is widely used in cell work.
