Semax is a seven-residue analogue of the ACTH(4-7) core, extended at the C-terminus with Pro-Gly-Pro and supplied as a lyophilized powder. Its sequence is Met-Glu-His-Phe-Pro-Gly-Pro, and the N-terminal methionine is the analytical weak point: it oxidises readily, adding 16 Da and creating a satellite peak that a purity figure alone will not explain. ## Why This Compound

Semax comes from the same Moscow institute as Selank, but its parent sequence is different. ACTH(4-7) is the short melanocortin core of adrenocorticotropic hormone, a fragment that retains neurotrophic activity without the steroidogenic action of the full hormone. Adding Pro-Gly-Pro at the C-terminus produced a molecule that survives long enough in plasma and tissue to be studied in vivo. The design logic is similar to Selank's tail, though the two peptides otherwise share little beyond their origin.

The research literature has three centres of gravity. The first is neurotrophin transcription. Semax has been reported to raise BDNF and TrkB expression in rat hippocampus, and to induce neurotrophin mRNAs in glial cell cultures within hours of exposure (Shadrina et al., 2001; Dolotov et al., 2006). The second is cerebral ischemia. After experimental ischemia-reperfusion, Semax and the Pro-Gly-Pro fragment changed the transcription of neurotrophins and their receptor genes, and later transcriptome work mapped a broader set of affected pathways (Dmitrieva et al., 2010; Filippenkov et al., 2020). The third is monoamine and behavioural pharmacology, where microdialysis and rodent learning paradigms are used as readouts.

Those are preclinical findings. The compound is registered as a medicinal product in Russia, which gives it a clinical literature that most research peptides lack, but that literature sits outside the scope of a catalogue page. What matters for a laboratory buyer is narrower: the peptide is used as a tool to perturb neurotrophin signalling, and the quality of the tool determines whether a small effect is measurable. A batch with a large oxidised fraction is not the same reagent as a batch with a small one, even when both are labelled 98% pure.

There is a practical reason the oxidation issue deserves attention. Methionine is one of the most easily oxidised residues in a peptide, and the conversion to methionine sulfoxide is accelerated by light, peroxide, metal ions, and repeated freeze-thaw. Semax has a single Met at the N-terminus, so the modification is stoichiometric and easy to detect by mass. A supplier that does not report it either has not measured it or is relying on a purity method that cannot see it. Neither is reassuring for a study that depends on a subtle transcriptional effect.

## What the Batch Record Should Show

Identity starts with the mass. The average mass of the free peptide is 813.94 Da; the singly protonated ion appears near m/z 814.95, and the isotope envelope should look like a real measurement rather than a single copied number. The certificate should state whether the value refers to the free acid or a salt, because the counterion changes the mass of the powder without changing the sequence.

The next field is oxidation. A methionine sulfoxide adds 15.99 Da, producing a peak at about 829.94 Da in the same spectrum. Good certificates report the oxidised fraction as a separate value or show the satellite peak in the deconvoluted spectrum. A certificate that reports "98%" without mentioning oxidation is not necessarily wrong, but it is incomplete: the oxidised form can co-elute under some gradients, and area normalisation may count it as product if the resolution is poor. Ask for the oxidation percentage and the method used to calculate it.

Purity by reversed-phase HPLC should come with method detail. Semax is detected at 214 nm, where the peptide bond absorbs, and the gradient is usually water/acetonitrile with TFA. Column dimensions, particle size, flow, gradient, wavelength, and integration threshold all belong on the certificate. A chromatogram without those parameters is a picture, not a method. If the certificate reports purity by area normalisation only, ask whether any correction was made for water, counterion, or residual solvent.

The free-acid form matters. Semax is not C-terminally amidated; the sequence ends in Pro-OH. The catalogue's NA Semax Amidate is a different molecule with an N-acetyl group and a C-terminal amide, and its mass is about 41 Da higher. A supplier that uses the two names interchangeably is not describing a defined product. Confirm which form is in the vial before comparing quotes.

Counterion and peptide content follow. Acetate is the usual catalogue salt, but TFA can remain from purification. Water content by Karl Fischer and a peptide-content assay are what turn fill mass into peptide mass. On an 814 Da peptide, a few percent water and a counterion can shift the amount of peptide in a 5 mg vial enough to matter for a concentration series. Endotoxin testing is relevant for glial or primary cell work and should be requested rather than assumed.

Traceability closes the record. The batch number on the certificate should match the vial, and the document should carry synthesis and retest dates plus the manufacturing site. A certificate that could apply to any Semax lot, or a chromatogram reused across pack sizes, is a template. ## Sourcing & Verification

Semax is widely listed, and the differences between listings are mostly documentary. The questions below are the ones that change the answer.

- Does the certificate state the free-acid form explicitly, or does it leave the C-terminus unstated?
- Is methionine oxidation reported as a percentage, and is the oxidised peak visible in the mass spectrum?
- What is the observed mass, and does it match 813.94 Da for the free peptide rather than a salt?
- Which counterion is present, and is residual TFA measured?
- What is the peptide content per vial, and by which method?
- Can the supplier provide the raw chromatogram and the deconvoluted spectrum, rather than a PDF summary?
- Who synthesised and who lyophilised the batch, and is a retain sample kept?
- Was the batch stored under inert gas or in a freezer with a defrost cycle?
- Can the supplier release one vial as a pilot before a ten-vial pack?
- Is endotoxin data available for cell-based work?

The oxidation question is the fastest filter. A manufacturer who understands the molecule will answer it directly and may volunteer the sulfoxide value. A reseller will often reply with a purity percentage that does not address the question. ## Handling & Storage

Lyophilised Semax should be stored at −20°C, desiccated, and protected from light. The powder is hygroscopic enough that a cold vial should be allowed to reach room temperature before opening; condensation on the cake is a direct route to hydrolysis and oxidation. Wipe the vial, wait, and open in a dry environment.

Reconstitution is usually water or a buffer compatible with the assay. Add the solvent gently and swirl; avoid vortexing, which creates foam and exposes the methionine to air at the liquid interface. The [reconstitution materials note](/blog/peptide-reconstitution-materials/) covers solvent and container choices in more detail. If the solution will be stored for more than a few hours, purge the headspace with argon or nitrogen and keep it at 2-8°C. Avoid buffers containing peroxide, and avoid trace metal contamination from rusty needles or impure salts, because both accelerate Met oxidation.

For cell work, filter through a 0.22 µm membrane and check recovery. Semax is not particularly sticky compared with cationic peptides, but any filtration step can remove material, and a recovery check is cheaper than repeating an experiment. Use polypropylene tubes for dilute stocks; polystyrene can adsorb small amounts of peptide, and the loss is proportionally larger at low concentrations.

Aliquot before freezing. A single freeze-thaw cycle is usually tolerable; repeated cycles are not. For long-term storage of the powder, use a desiccator and a freezer without a frost-free defrost cycle, which exposes contents to temperature swings and moisture. The catalogue's reconstituted shelf life is a planning figure, not a stability study; a laboratory should establish its own working window with a fresh preparation and an appropriate analytical check.

The steps above are bench procedures. Semax is a research reagent, not a preparation for human or animal use.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is Semax C-terminally amidated?**

No. The catalogue sequence ends in Pro-OH, the free acid. The amidated version is a separate product, NA Semax Amidate, which also carries an N-terminal acetyl group. The two differ in mass by about 41 Da and should not be substituted for one another without rechecking the assay.

**Why does methionine oxidation matter?**

The N-terminal methionine is easily oxidised to methionine sulfoxide, which adds 15.99 Da. The oxidised peptide may have different receptor or enzyme behaviour and can co-elute with the parent under some HPLC conditions. A certificate that reports only a purity percentage may not reveal how much oxidised form is present.

**What mass should the certificate show?**

For the free peptide, the average mass is 813.94 Da and the singly protonated ion is near m/z 814.95. A salt form will shift the observed mass by the counterion. The certificate should state which form was measured and show the isotope envelope, not just a rounded number.

**How should I store the lyophilised powder?**

At −20°C, desiccated, protected from light. Allow the vial to come to ambient temperature before opening; condensation is the enemy here. If the powder will be kept for years rather than months, −80°C with an inert headspace is a more conservative choice, but the batch retest date should come from the supplier's stability data.

**Can Semax be used in cell culture?**

Yes, in principle, but the assay needs a sterile, endotoxin-tested preparation. Filter the reconstituted stock through a 0.22 µm membrane and check recovery. If the cells are primary glia or neurons, endotoxin contamination can dominate the readout, so LAL data is worth requesting before the experiment.

**What is the difference between Semax and ACTH?**

ACTH is a 39-residue hormone that stimulates adrenal steroid production through MC2R. Semax is a seven-residue fragment with a Pro-Gly-Pro tail and does not carry the full steroidogenic sequence. The research literature treats them as different tools, and a supplier should not use the names interchangeably.

**Which purity method is appropriate?**

Reversed-phase HPLC at 214 nm with a stated gradient and column is the standard. Mass spectrometry confirms identity and reveals oxidation. Purity by area normalisation alone does not account for water, counterion, or co-eluting modified forms, so pair it with a peptide-content assay and a mass spectrum.

**Does the colour of the lyophilised cake indicate quality?**

Not reliably. A white to off-white cake is typical, but colour can vary with fill mass, residual moisture, and lighting during handling. A yellow or brown tint may suggest oxidation or contamination, but the certificate and the chromatogram are stronger evidence than appearance alone.

## The Author's Take

**Position.** I would treat the methionine sulfoxide value as the first quality metric on a Semax certificate, ahead of the headline purity number.

**Reasoning.** Semax is a small peptide with one oxidisable residue, and the oxidised form is a plausible confounder in neurotrophin assays.
