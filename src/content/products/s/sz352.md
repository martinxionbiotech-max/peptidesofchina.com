## Why This Compound

GABA is the principal inhibitory neurotransmitter in the mammalian central nervous system. It acts at ionotropic GABA-A receptors, which are ligand-gated chloride channels, and at metabotropic GABA-B receptors, which couple to G proteins. In a cell model, GABA application changes chloride conductance and membrane potential; in a tissue slice, it changes network excitability. The GABA in a research formulation is not the same as synaptic GABA — it has to cross a membrane or activate a receptor on the extracellular face — but it is still a useful tool for probing GABA receptor pharmacology in vitro.

L-theanine is a non-protein amino acid found in tea. It is structurally related to glutamate and glutamine, and it has been studied as a modulator of glutamatergic and GABAergic signalling in cell and slice models. Its exact receptor target is not settled; the literature includes effects on glutamate uptake, on GABA receptor expression, and on monoamine turnover. That uncertainty is a reason to use it as a defined chemical stimulus with a clear readout, not as a stand-in for a mechanism.

L-histidine is the precursor to histamine and a component of the imidazole buffer system. In a research context, it is used to probe histidine decarboxylase activity, histamine synthesis, and the effect of histidine availability on mast cell and neuronal models. It also has buffering capacity near pH 6, which is relevant to formulation.

Taurine is a sulfonic acid that is not incorporated into proteins. It acts as an osmolyte, a chloride channel modulator, and an antioxidant. In the brain, it is one of the most abundant free amino acids, and it has been studied in the context of inhibitory neurotransmission and cell volume regulation.

Melatonin is an indoleamine produced by the pineal gland. It acts at MT1 and MT2 receptors, both G protein-coupled, and its production is entrained to the light–dark cycle. In a research context, melatonin is used to probe circadian clock biology, receptor signalling, and oxidative stress. The concentration in a formulation is usually low, and that is the analytical challenge.

## What the Batch Record Should Show

The certificate should begin with a component table: the declared amount of each analyte, the salt or free form, and the assay result as a percentage of label claim. A total concentration of 352 mg/mL is not a substitute for that table. The sum of the five components may account for only part of the total; the remainder is water, buffer, or excipient, and it should be identified.

GABA and taurine are small, polar molecules that are poorly retained on standard C18 columns without ion-pairing or derivatization. The method should state whether it uses HILIC, ion-exchange, or derivatization with a reagent such as OPA or dansyl chloride. If the method is not selective, GABA and taurine can co-elute with other amino acids in the blend.

L-theanine is an analogue of glutamine and glutamate, and it can interfere with amino acid analysis if the chromatographic separation is insufficient. The method should demonstrate resolution from glutamine and glutamate, or use LC-MS/MS with a specific transition. L-histidine is oxidation-prone and can be lost during sample preparation; the certificate should state whether the sample was protected from light and air.

Melatonin is the component most likely to be understated. It has limited aqueous solubility, it adsorbs to plastic, and it is photolabile. The assay should use a method with a low limit of quantification, such as LC-MS/MS in positive mode, and the sample preparation should avoid filtration steps that remove the analyte. If the certificate reports melatonin as "present" without a number, the product is not fully characterised.

Physical tests for a sterile liquid include pH, osmolality, fill volume, colour and clarity, particulate matter, sterility, and endotoxin. The pH is relevant to melatonin solubility and to the stability of histidine. A batch at the wrong pH can precipitate melatonin or accelerate oxidation. The container should be amber glass or the product should be stored in the dark; melatonin and histidine are both light-sensitive.

A stability-indicating assay is worth requesting. A short accelerated study at 25°C and 40°C, with assay at each time point, will show whether the low-concentration components survive the shipping lane. If the supplier has no stability data, buy a small quantity and test it on arrival.

## Sourcing & Verification

The first sourcing question is the formulation basis. A five-component solution at 352 mg/mL is concentrated, and the solubility of melatonin in water is low. A formulator can increase solubility with a co-solvent, a cyclodextrin, or a pH adjustment, but the certificate should state which. If the melatonin is suspended rather than dissolved, the ampoule is not a solution, and the content uniformity is not guaranteed. Ask for a clarity test and a melatonin assay from the supernatant and from the whole vial.

The second question is the source of the melatonin. Melatonin is a regulated substance in many jurisdictions: it is a prescription medicine in some countries, a dietary supplement in others, and a controlled substance in a few. The buyer is usually the importer of record, and a "research use only" label does not override customs law. Check the destination's rules before ordering. The [compliance pillar](/peptide-compliance-risk/) explains the intended-use boundary, and the [shipping policy](/shipping-and-reshipment-policy/) explains what happens if a parcel is held.

The third question is the analytical method. A supplier that measures only total nitrogen or total amino acids cannot confirm the blend. Ask for the method and the result for each component, especially melatonin and theanine. Ask whether the results are measured or calculated. A calculated result is a formulation record, not a release test.

The fourth question is the water and the container. A sterile liquid made with water that is out of specification can fail endotoxin even after filtration. A clear glass ampoule can photo-degrade melatonin during storage. Ask for the water system's monitoring record and the container specification.

Independent verification should focus on melatonin and theanine. Those are the two analytes that a standard lab is least likely to measure correctly, and they are the two that distinguish this blend from a generic amino acid solution. A contract lab with LC-MS/MS can quantify both from a single aliquot. The [quality testing pillar](/peptide-quality-testing/) sets out the chain-of-custody steps for that request.

## Handling & Storage

Store at 2–8°C, protected from light. Do not freeze. Melatonin and histidine are both light-sensitive, and a freeze-thaw cycle can precipitate the melatonin or crack the ampoule. Keep the ampoules upright and in the original carton.

Let the ampoule stand until it reaches room temperature, then disinfect the neck with 70% isopropanol. Use a fresh needle or a filter needle. For cell work, pass the solution through a 0.22 µm PVDF or PES disc into a sterile tube. If the solution is cloudy, do not filter and proceed; a cloudy solution may indicate undissolved melatonin or precipitation, and the concentration in the filtrate will not match the label.

Dilute into the assay buffer at the final ratio and record the dilution. At this strength, a microlitre-level pipetting error propagates into a visible concentration error. Add the stock slowly with mixing; a concentrated bolus can change the pH or precipitate a component. Run a vehicle control at the same dilution.

Light protection should continue after dilution. Wrap the tube in foil if the assay requires a long equilibration. Do not store a diluted working solution overnight unless a stability study supports it. Most formats are preservative-free, and a dilute solution will support environmental organisms.

For assays that measure GABA or taurine, check the background of the medium. Many cell-culture media contain amino acids that interfere with the assay, and the blend adds to that background. A blank medium control and a spiked recovery control are useful.
