## Compound Profile

Kisspeptin-10 is the C-terminal decapeptide of kisspeptin (also known as metastin), the endogenous neuropeptide product of the KISS1 gene. Kisspeptins are processed from a 145-amino acid precursor into fragments of varying lengths (kisspeptin-54, -14, -13, -10), all of which share the C-terminal decapeptide sequence that is required for GPR54/KISS1R receptor binding. Kisspeptin-10 — the minimally active binding fragment — is the most widely used research form due to its small size, ease of synthesis, and equipotent activity to longer kisspeptin isoforms at GPR54. Kisspeptin and GPR54 are essential regulators of the hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal (HPG) axis. Kisspeptin neurons in the arcuate nucleus and anteroventral periventricular nucleus (AVPV) integrate sex steroid feedback signals and project directly onto GnRH (gonadotropin-releasing hormone) neurons, stimulating GnRH pulse release. Loss-of-function mutations in KISS1 or GPR54 result in hypogonadotropic hypogonadism in rodents and humans — establishing kisspeptin/GPR54 as the upstream activator of reproductive endocrine function. Research applications include HPG axis physiology, puberty onset mechanisms, seasonal reproduction biology, and fertility research.
