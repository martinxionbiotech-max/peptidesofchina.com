## Why This Compound

Kisspeptins were discovered through two lines of work that converged in 2001. One group identified KISS1 as a metastasis suppressor gene and found that its product acted at an orphan G-protein-coupled receptor, GPR54. Another group isolated the same peptide from placental tissue and named it metastin. The gene encodes a 145-residue precursor that is cleaved into fragments of 54, 14, 13, and 10 amino acids, all sharing the C-terminal decapeptide. That shared fragment is kisspeptin-10.

The receptor is now called KISS1R. In 2003, two groups reported that loss-of-function mutations in KISS1R cause hypogonadotropic hypogonadism, establishing the pathway as a required upstream regulator of the hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal axis. The finding was significant because it placed a specific peptide-receptor pair above GnRH in the control hierarchy, rather than alongside it.

The research literature since then has focused on three areas. The first is GnRH pulse generation. Kisspeptin neurons in the arcuate nucleus co-express neurokinin B and dynorphin, forming the KNDy population that has been proposed as the pulse generator. The second is the preovulatory LH surge, where kisspeptin neurons in the anteroventral periventricular nucleus respond to rising oestradiol and activate GnRH neurons. The third is developmental timing, including puberty onset and seasonal reproduction in photoperiod-dependent mammals. These are distinct research questions, and a study designed around one does not automatically speak to the others. The [categories and mechanisms hub](/peptide-categories-mechanisms/) places kisspeptin among the neuroendocrine signalling peptides.

For a buyer, the relevant fact is that the decapeptide is a pharmacological tool, not a hormone replacement. It acts at KISS1R on GnRH neurons; it does not act at the pituitary directly in the same way as a GnRH agonist. That distinction matters when comparing catalogue listings, because kisspeptin-10 and gonadorelin are sometimes presented as alternatives when they act at different levels of the same axis.

Three sequence features drive the analytical behaviour. The C-terminal amide is essential for receptor activity and changes the mass by about one dalton relative to the free acid. There are two asparagine residues, one of them in an Asn-Gly motif, which is a classical deamidation hotspot. And there is a single tryptophan, which is the UV chromophore and the residue most likely to oxidise. A certificate that does not address amidation, deamidation, and oxidation is not describing this peptide in enough detail.

## What the Batch Record Should Show

**Identity and C-terminal amidation.** The monoisotopic mass of the amidated decapeptide is near 1301.6 Da, and the singly protonated ion appears near m/z 1302.6. The free-acid form is approximately 1 Da lower. A low-resolution mass spectrum can distinguish them if the calibration is good; a certificate that reports only "MW 1302.6" without a spectrum has not shown which form was made. Ask for the deconvoluted mass and, ideally, a fragment that covers the C-terminal residue. The amide is not a purification artefact; it is part of the intended structure.

**Deamidation and iso-aspartate.** The Asn-Gly sequence at positions four and five is prone to succinimide-mediated deamidation. The first product is aspartate, which adds about 1 Da; the succinimide intermediate can also hydrolyse to iso-aspartate, which has the same mass as aspartate but a different retention time. On a shallow gradient, these species often appear as a partially resolved leading shoulder. A purity figure of 98% can therefore hide a meaningful fraction of deamidated peptide if the method integrates them into the main peak.

**Oxidation.** Tryptophan oxidation adds 16 Da and is promoted by light, oxygen, and trace metals. The certificate should show whether the +16 species was resolved. For a peptide used in cell-based assays, an oxidised fraction can change potency without changing the headline purity.

**Purity by HPLC, with the method disclosed.** The catalogue floor is 98% by area at 214 nm. Get the column, mobile phase, gradient, and integration settings in writing. The decapeptide is amphipathic: it has hydrophobic residues (Trp, Phe, Leu) and a basic arginine, so retention depends strongly on the ion-pairing agent and the organic modifier. A method transferred from a different peptide may not resolve the deamidated forms.

**Counterion, water, and peptide content.** The counterion is usually acetate or trifluoroacetate, and the certificate should name it. Karl Fischer water content and nitrogen or amino acid analysis convert fill mass into peptide mass. For a 10 mg vial, the difference between fill and peptide content can be several milligrams. The [counterion note](/blog/role-of-counterions-in-peptide-stability/) explains why the salt form affects both mass and solution behaviour.

**Batch linkage and endotoxin.** The certificate should carry a batch number matching the vial label, a synthesis date, and a retest date. For work with primary cells, request LAL endotoxin results; sterility is tested separately.

## Sourcing & Verification

Kisspeptin-10 is sold by suppliers who understand the neuroendocrine literature and by suppliers who copied a catalogue entry. The questions below separate them.

- Does the certificate state that the C-terminus is amidated, or does it show a free-acid sequence? The mass difference is small, but the functional difference is not.
- Is the full sequence printed, including the C-terminal NH₂?
- Was the chromatogram run on a gradient that resolves deamidated and oxidised forms? Ask for the method and the impurity table.
- Is the reported mass the free base or a salt, and does the spectrum show the amidated product?
- How much peptide is in each vial, and how was that measured?
- Is residual TFA reported? It can affect cell-based assays and is a marker of purification.
- Does the lot carry endotoxin data for primary-cell work?
- Will the supplier share a certificate from a lot that has already shipped?

The naming is another trap. Kisspeptin-10, KP-10, metastin(45-54), and KISS1 C-terminal decapeptide all refer to the same sequence. Kisspeptin-54 is a longer isoform with the same C-terminal fragment; it is not the same molecule and has different pharmacokinetics. A listing that uses the names interchangeably is compressing the literature in a way that will confuse an assay.

## Handling & Storage

Lyophilized Kisspeptin-10 is stored at −20°C for long-term use, protected from light and moisture. Aqueous solutions are less stable and should be kept at 2–8°C for short periods, typically within two weeks.

Warm the sealed vial to room temperature before opening it. The powder is hygroscopic, and condensation on a cold vial introduces water that promotes deamidation. Open in a low-humidity space and reseal promptly.

For assay work, dissolve in sterile water or buffer and inspect the solution. The peptide has both hydrophobic and basic character, so solubility depends on pH and ionic strength; a clear solution at one condition may cloud at another. If a concentrated stock is needed, a small volume of DMSO can be used and then diluted below the assay's tolerance, but the final solvent composition should be recorded and matched in the vehicle control. Add solvent gently and swirl rather than vortex.

Avoid alkaline buffers. The Asn-Gly motif deamidates fastest at neutral to alkaline pH, and the resulting iso-aspartate can be difficult to separate from the parent. If the assay requires a pH above 7.5, run a time-course stability check rather than assuming the peptide will hold.

Aliquot before freezing. Single-use portions at −80°C avoid repeated freeze-thaw cycles, which promote aggregation and adsorption. Keep it out of direct light, because the tryptophan side chain oxidises readily. Use low-binding tubes for dilute solutions; peptides with hydrophobic stretches can disappear onto plastic surfaces in a way that looks like a solubility problem.

## How It Compares

**Gonadorelin.** A GnRH analogue that acts directly at the pituitary gonadotroph. Kisspeptin-10 acts upstream at KISS1R on GnRH neurons. The two are used in different experimental designs: gonadorelin tests pituitary responsiveness, while kisspeptin-10 tests the neuronal input to the GnRH system. A researcher who substitutes one for the other is changing the level of the axis being measured. See the [gonadorelin page](/catalog/gonadorelin-acetate/).

**PT-141.** A melanocortin receptor agonist with no activity at KISS1R. It appears in the same neuroendocrine catalogues but belongs to a different receptor family. The comparison is useful only as a reminder that "neuroendocrine research" is a shelf label, not a mechanism. The [PT-141 page](/catalog/pt-141-bremelanotide/) covers its profile.

**HCG.** A glycoprotein hormone that acts at the luteinising hormone receptor. It is not a peptide of the same class and is not a substitute for kisspeptin-10 in a KISS1R assay. The [HCG listing](/catalog/hcg/) sets out its own specification.

**Kisspeptin-54.** The longer isoform. It contains the same C-terminal decapeptide and binds KISS1R, but its size, clearance, and synthesis cost differ. Most pharmacological work uses the decapeptide because it is easier to make and characterise. A supplier who offers "kisspeptin" without specifying the length is not giving you enough information.
