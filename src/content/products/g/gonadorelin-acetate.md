## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the difference between gonadorelin and GnRH analogues like leuprolide?**

Gonadorelin is the unmodified native GnRH sequence with a short ~2-8 minute half-life. Long-acting GnRH analogues (leuprolide, buserelin, histrelin) have modifications at positions 6 and/or 10 that dramatically extend half-life and increase receptor affinity. Paradoxically, sustained GnRH agonist exposure causes receptor desensitization and LH/FSH suppression — the opposite of pulsatile gonadorelin, which stimulates gonadotropin release. This pharmacodynamic difference is fundamental to GnRH research. Why is the pulsatile versus continuous distinction important for GnRH research? The pituitary responds to GnRH pulses with maintained FSH and LH secretion, but desensitizes and suppresses gonadotropin output under continuous GnRH stimulation. This frequency-encoding capacity is a unique feature of GnRHR signaling studied as a model for how the pituitary decodes neuroendocrine pulse information. Research protocols using gonadorelin in pulse generators or intermittent injection schedules are designed specifically to exploit or study this distinction.

## Compound Profile

Gonadorelin acetate is the synthetic form of GnRH (gonadotropin-releasing hormone), the endogenous hypothalamic decapeptide that controls the reproductive axis by stimulating pituitary release of LH (luteinizing hormone) and FSH (follicle-stimulating hormone). The native sequence — pGlu-His-Trp-Ser-Tyr-Gly-Leu-Arg-Pro-Gly-NH₂ — is highly conserved across vertebrates, and gonadorelin acetate reproduces this sequence in synthetic form with acetic acid as the counterion. As the endogenous GnRH agonist (unmodified), gonadorelin has a very short plasma half-life (~2-8 minutes), which is clinically and experimentally exploited: pulsatile administration mimics endogenous GnRH physiology, while continuous infusion produces pituitary GnRH receptor desensitization and LH/FSH suppression. Research applications for gonadorelin acetate span reproductive endocrinology, pituitary pharmacology, and HPG axis physiology. It is used as the reference GnRH agonist in pituitary cell culture studies of LH and FSH secretion, in GnRH receptor binding assays, and in in vivo rodent models examining pulsatile versus continuous GnRH stimulation on pituitary gonadotroph function. The pulsatile dosing paradigm — reproducing the ~1 pulse/hour physiological pattern — is particularly studied in hypogonadotropic hypogonadism models and in research into the entrainment of the pituitary to hypothalamic GnRH pulse frequency.
