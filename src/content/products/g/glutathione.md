## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the difference between reduced and oxidized glutathione in research?**

Reduced glutathione (GSH) is the biologically active antioxidant form with a free thiol. Oxidized glutathione (GSSG) is the disulfide-bonded dimer formed after GSH has donated electrons to neutralize reactive oxygen species. The GSH/GSSG ratio is the primary redox indicator in cellular research. We supply the reduced form (GSH) for antioxidant and redox research protocols. Why must glutathione solutions be prepared fresh? GSH is rapidly oxidized to GSSG in aqueous solution upon exposure to dissolved oxygen, especially at neutral or alkaline pH and in the presence of metal ions. Freshly prepared GSH solutions (preferably in degassed, slightly acidic buffer) are required for accurate research results. Even refrigerated solutions should be used within hours. For longer storage, lyophilized GSH maintained at −20°C in the absence of moisture is stable.

## Compound Profile

Glutathione (GSH) is the most abundant intracellular low-molecular-weight thiol and the primary antioxidant buffer in mammalian cells, present at millimolar concentrations in the cytosol, mitochondria, and nucleus. The tripeptide structure — γ-Glu-Cys-Gly — is synthesized in two ATP-dependent enzymatic steps by glutamate-cysteine ligase (GCL, rate-limiting) and glutathione synthetase (GS). The cysteine thiol (–SH) is the redox-active center, enabling glutathione to reduce disulfide bonds, scavenge free radicals, conjugate electrophilic compounds via glutathione-S-transferases, and serve as the substrate for glutathione peroxidases (GPx) protecting against lipid peroxidation and hydrogen peroxide. Research applications for glutathione span oxidative stress biology, liver research, immune function, and neuroscience. In hepatocyte research, GSH is the primary defense against oxidative hepatotoxicity — liver cells maintain high cytosolic GSH concentrations, and depletion models (using BSO or acetaminophen) followed by GSH repletion experiments are standard in hepatotoxicity research. In neurological research, brain GSH depletion has been studied in models of Parkinson's disease, where substantia nigra GSH deficiency is an early biochemical finding. Immunologically, T cell activation and proliferation are sensitive to intracellular GSH levels, making glutathione a tool in immune cell biology research.
