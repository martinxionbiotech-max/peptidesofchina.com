Pinealon is a three-residue peptide — Glu-Asp-Arg — sold as a pineal-series bioregulator. It is the smallest product on this shelf and the cheapest, which makes it easy to treat as a commodity. That would be a mistake for one analytical reason: the peptide has no aromatic residue, so it has almost no ultraviolet absorbance beyond the peptide bond. A purity claim at 280 nm is meaningless for this molecule, and a chromatogram run at low wavelength on a conventional C18 column can look worse than the peptide actually is.

The catalogue lists 10 mg vials at ≥98%, with a molecular formula and a molecular weight. Those values are checkable, and this page explains how to check them.

## Why This Compound

Pinealon belongs to the Khavinson family of short peptide bioregulators, a research programme that began with tissue-derived peptide fractions and moved to synthetic tri- and tetrapeptides. The pineal member is Glu-Asp-Arg, sometimes written EDR. The research premise is that short peptides can interact with DNA or chromatin-associated proteins and shift gene expression in a tissue-associated pattern. It is a different framework from receptor pharmacology, and the literature reflects that: small animal cohorts, cell-culture endpoints, and a strong presence in gerontology and physiology journals rather than in mainstream drug-discovery journals.

The published record is narrow. A 2011 study in *Rejuvenation Research* reported that Pinealon increased cell viability by suppressing free-radical levels and activating proliferative processes. A 2012 study in the *International Journal of Clinical and Experimental Medicine* examined offspring of rats exposed to prenatal hyperhomocysteinaemia and reported protective effects in that model. A 2019 physical-chemistry paper examined the role of mono- and divalent ions in the Glu-Asp-Arg–DNA interaction, which is relevant to the proposed mechanism but does not establish a cellular outcome. Other papers in *Advances in Gerontology* examined behaviour and neurochemical markers in aged rats under hypoxia and hypothermia.

The mechanistic story is not settled. The DNA-binding work suggests that a tripeptide with two acidic residues and one basic residue can interact with nucleic acid in an ion-dependent way. Whether that interaction produces a specific transcriptional effect in a living cell is a separate question, and the published data do not answer it cleanly. A researcher should treat Pinealon as a tool for exploring the bioregulator hypothesis, not as a compound with a established mechanism.

The analytical profile is more concrete. Glu-Asp-Arg has a molecular formula of C₁₄H₂₅N₅O₉ and a molecular weight of 432.4 Da. It is highly polar, with two carboxylates and one guanidino group, and it is freely soluble in water. It has no tryptophan, tyrosine or phenylalanine, so it does not absorb at 280 nm. These are facts a certificate can confirm, and they are the basis for the batch checks below.

## What the Batch Record Should Show

Identity comes first. The certificate should print the sequence, the calculated mass and the observed mass. For a tripeptide, the intact mass is a strong identity check: there are few ways to arrange three residues into a molecule of 432.4 Da, and a one-residue substitution changes the mass enough to be visible. The certificate should also state whether the peptide is the free acid or the C-terminal amide. The catalogue does not specify, and the difference is about 1 Da — small in absolute terms but enough to shift the charge state in LC-MS.

Purity by HPLC needs the right wavelength. At 214 nm, the peptide bond absorbs weakly, and a tripeptide gives a small signal compared with a larger peptide. The method may need a higher injection volume or a more sensitive detector. At 280 nm, there is nothing to see. A certificate that reports a 280 nm chromatogram for Pinealon is either using a different molecule or a different detection method than the one described. Ask for the chromatogram with the wavelength in the method header.

The chromatography itself is worth a second look. Glu-Asp-Arg is very polar and may not retain well on a standard C18 column without ion-pairing or a shallow gradient. If the peptide elutes near the solvent front, the main peak can co-elute with salts and injection artefacts, and the reported purity may be optimistic. A method that uses a polar-embedded or mixed-mode column, or an ion-pairing reagent, is more informative. The [HPLC versus LC-MS note](/blog/hplc-vs-lc-ms-peptides/) explains why retention behaviour matters for short polar peptides.

Counterion and water content are the mass-balance fields. Pinealon is commonly supplied as the acetate salt; residual trifluoroacetate from purification is also possible. The certificate should name the counterion and, where measured, report residual TFA. Karl Fischer water content and a peptide-content assay convert the 10 mg fill into peptide mass. Without them, a nominal concentration is a fill-weight calculation, not a peptide concentration.

Endotoxin testing matters if the material will contact primary cells. Ask whether LAL was run and what limit applied. Sterility is a separate test; a lyophilized powder is not sterile by default. The [quality pillar](/peptide-quality-testing/) sets out the difference between a release test and a catalogue specification.

Batch traceability is the last field. The lot number on the certificate should match the vial, and the certificate should name the synthesis site and the date. For a cheap peptide, the temptation to skip this check is high; it is also the check that distinguishes a manufacturer from a reseller.

## Handling & Storage

Store the dry powder at −20°C, desiccated, protected from light. Bring the vial to room temperature before opening. A cold vial in a warm room condenses water on the glass, and a highly polar tripeptide will take up that water quickly once the cap is removed.

Pinealon dissolves readily in water and in phosphate-buffered saline. Add the solvent down the wall of the vial and mix by gentle inversion. Avoid vortexing; even a small peptide can adsorb to the glass at a foam interface, and the loss is proportionally larger when the total mass is small. If the solution is cloudy, let it stand for a few minutes before adding more solvent.

For stock solutions, use water or the buffer your assay requires. The peptide has two carboxylates and one guanidino group, so its charge state changes with pH. Record the pH and the buffer; a shift of one pH unit can change the peptide's interaction with a surface or a binding partner. Do not assume that a solution made in water behaves the same as one made in PBS.

The catalogue specifies that aqueous solutions should be kept at 2–8°C and used within two weeks. That is a reasonable short-term window for a sterile-filtered stock. For longer storage, aliquot and freeze at −20°C or below, and do not refreeze. ## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is a peptide bioregulator?**

A short peptide, usually two to four residues, studied for tissue-associated effects on gene expression and cell differentiation. The framework comes from Russian gerontology research and is distinct from receptor agonism or enzyme inhibition. The evidence base is mostly preclinical and concentrated in a small number of laboratories.

**What is the sequence of Pinealon?**

Glu-Asp-Arg, often abbreviated EDR. The catalogue gives the molecular formula C₁₄H₂₅N₅O₉ and a molecular weight of 432.4 Da. A batch certificate should print the sequence and an observed mass that matches the calculated value.

**Why can't I measure Pinealon at 280 nm?**

Because the peptide has no tryptophan, tyrosine or phenylalanine. It has no aromatic chromophore, so it does not absorb at 280 nm. Purity should be measured at 214 nm or by a method that derivatises or detects the peptide backbone. A 280 nm purity claim for this molecule is a red flag.

**How should I store the powder?**

At −20°C, desiccated and protected from light. Bring the vial to room temperature before opening. Reconstituted solutions should be kept at 2–8°C and used within two weeks, or aliquoted and frozen for longer storage.

**What solvent should I use?**

Water or phosphate-buffered saline. The peptide is highly polar and dissolves readily. Add the solvent gently, mix by inversion, and avoid vortexing. Record the pH, because the charge state changes with pH.

**Is the product sterile?**

No. Lyophilized research peptides are not sterile by default. If sterile material is required, filter the reconstituted solution through a 0.22 µm membrane and verify that the peptide does not adsorb to the filter. The [sterility guide](/blog/sterility-testing-for-research-peptides-a-protocol-guide/) covers the options.

**Can I use Pinealon in cell culture?**

The published studies used cell cultures and animal models. For a new system, run a vehicle control and a concentration-response pilot before committing to a full design. The catalogue purity specification is not a cell-culture qualification.

**Why is Pinealon cheaper than the other bioregulators?**

It is a tripeptide made by routine solid-phase synthesis, and the raw-material cost is low. The price difference reflects the number of synthesis steps and the batch size, not a difference in biological activity.
