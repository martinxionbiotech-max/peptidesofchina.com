## Why This Compound

The p53 tumour suppressor is a transcription factor that responds to DNA damage and cellular stress. Its activity is restrained by MDM2, which binds the p53 N-terminal transactivation domain and targets it for degradation. The first 26 residues of p53 contain the MDM2-binding motif, and peptides from that region have been used for decades to study the interaction and to compete with full-length p53 for MDM2 binding.

PNC27 corresponds to residues 12–26 of p53. That fragment retains the HDM2-binding surface and is short enough to synthesise and purify. In a 2017 study in *Nanomedicine*, PNC27 was conjugated to liposomal doxorubicin as a targeting ligand. The conjugate increased cellular uptake and cytotoxicity in HDM2-positive C26 colon carcinoma cells but not in HDM2-negative B16F0 melanoma cells, and increased the effect of the liposomal formulation in a mouse tumour model without changing its biodistribution. That is a targeting result: the peptide directed the carrier to cells with high HDM2 expression.

A separate line of work has examined p53-derived peptides as membrane-active agents. A 2024 paper in *Biomedicines* discussed peptide-induced transmembrane pore formation as a mechanism of tumour cell killing that can spare normal cells. The catalogue description for PNC27 refers to pore-forming mechanisms, and that is the context in which the claim should be read. The two mechanisms — HDM2 targeting and membrane disruption — are different, and a batch certificate does not tell you which one your preparation will engage.

The catalogue also describes PNC27 as a chimeric peptide with a membrane-penetrating domain. The primary paper describes the p53 12–26 sequence without specifying a cell-penetrating fusion. It is possible that the catalogue product is a chimeric version and the literature is about the bare peptide, or that the description has been generalised. A buyer should ask for the full sequence and compare it with the published peptide before assuming the two are the same molecule.

## What the Batch Record Should Show

The certificate should print the full sequence and the calculated mass. For a 15-residue p53 fragment, the mass is large enough that a one-residue deletion is visible, but a terminal modification or a missed deprotection can shift the mass by a similar amount. Tandem MS or a peptide map is more informative than a single intact mass, especially if the peptide is chimeric and contains a cell-penetrating domain.

Purity by HPLC should be reported with method detail. p53(12–26) contains several hydrophobic residues and is likely to retain well on a C18 column, which makes reversed-phase purity meaningful. The certificate should show the gradient, the wavelength and the integration parameters. If the chromatogram is a single peak without a scale, it is an illustration rather than evidence.

Counterion and peptide content are the mass-balance fields. The peptide is commonly supplied as the acetate salt or the trifluoroacetate salt, and the counterion mass is a meaningful fraction of a small fill. Karl Fischer water content and a peptide-content assay convert fill weight into peptide mass. The [purity versus content note](/blog/why-purity-and-concentration-tests-are-not-enough/) explains why a 98% purity number can coexist with a much lower peptide content.

Endotoxin is a priority for this product. Cell-culture studies with p53-derived peptides can be sensitive to lipopolysaccharide, and the readouts — viability, apoptosis, reporter expression — are exactly the kind that LPS can confound. Ask whether the lot was tested by LAL, what the limit was, and whether the result is lot-specific. Sterility is a separate test; a lyophilized powder is not sterile by default. The [sterility guide](/blog/sterility-testing-for-research-peptides-a-protocol-guide/) covers the options.

Identity of the chimeric domain deserves its own check. If the catalogue product contains a cell-penetrating peptide fused to the p53 fragment, the certificate should state the fusion sequence and the linker. A mass that matches the bare p53 fragment and a description that claims a fusion are incompatible. Ask the supplier to reconcile them before ordering.

Batch traceability closes the record. The lot number on the certificate should match the vial, and the certificate should name the synthesis site and the date. For a peptide used in a tumour model, the batch record is also the only way to compare a repeat study with the original.

## Sourcing & Verification

Ask for the sequence in writing, including any fusion domain and linker. The published PNC27 literature describes p53 residues 12–26; the catalogue describes a chimeric peptide. Those may be the same product with a generalised description, or two different products. The sequence resolves the question.

Ask which HDM2 assay the supplier used, if any. A binding assay is not a certificate of analysis, but a supplier who claims HDM2 targeting should be able to describe a competition assay or a surface plasmon resonance experiment. A supplier who cannot has a catalogue description, not a characterisation.

Ask about endotoxin and sterility before cell work. This is not a formality for a peptide that will be added to tumour cells. Ask about the counterion and the peptide content. A 5 mg fill of a 15-residue peptide may contain 3–4 mg of peptide once counterion and water are counted. That difference matters when you calculate a molar concentration for a cell-culture experiment.

## Handling & Storage

Keep the dry powder at −20°C, desiccated and protected from light. Bring the vial to room temperature before opening. A cold vial in a warm room condenses water on the glass, and the peptide cake can take up moisture quickly.

Reconstitute in water or buffer. p53(12–26) has hydrophobic residues and may not dissolve readily in pure water; if it does not, add a small volume of DMSO or dilute acetic acid and then dilute into buffer. Keep the final organic or acid concentration as low as the assay tolerates, and run a vehicle control at the same concentration.

Avoid vortexing and heating. A peptide with a hydrophobic stretch can aggregate at an air-liquid interface, and heat can promote deamidation of asparagine and glutamine residues. Mix by inversion or gentle rotation. If the solution is cloudy, centrifuge at low speed and inspect the pellet before adding more solvent.

For cell culture, prepare a concentrated stock, filter it through a 0.22 µm membrane if sterility is required, and dilute into medium. Check that the peptide does not adsorb to the filter; a small loss is acceptable for a pilot, but a large loss will change the effective concentration. Reconstitution mechanics are covered in the [reconstitution guide](/blog/peptide-lyophilization-reconstitution-guide-for-researchers/), and the [handling pillar](/peptide-handling-protocols/) covers the wider workflow.

Aliquot before freezing. Aqueous solutions are short-term stocks at 2–8°C; repeated freeze-thaw cycles promote aggregation. Split into single-use aliquots, freeze at −20°C or colder, and never refreeze.

## How It Compares

**PNC-28.** A related p53-derived peptide that contains the MDM2-binding domain plus a membrane-translocation sequence. It has been studied in pancreatic cancer models and is often confused with PNC27. The two are different molecules with different sequences and different literatures. If your study design cites PNC-28, do not substitute PNC27 without checking the sequence.

**PTD-DBM.** Another fusion peptide in this catalogue, but with a completely different target: it disrupts the CXXC5–Dishevelled interaction in Wnt signalling. It is not a p53 peptide and not a substitute. The [PTD-DBM page](/catalog/ptd-dbm/) lists the format.

**Bare p53 fragments.** Short p53 peptides without a cell-penetrating domain are useful for cell-free binding assays but may not enter cells efficiently. The choice between a bare fragment and a chimeric peptide depends on the assay, not on potency claims.

**Small-molecule MDM2 inhibitors.** Compounds such as nutlin analogues have published binding data and selectivity profiles. They are not peptides and not catalogue products here, but they are the appropriate benchmark for an HDM2-targeting reagent. A peptide that claims the same target should be compared on affinity and assay conditions, not on price.

**What is PNC27?**

A synthetic peptide corresponding to residues 12–26 of p53, the region that binds HDM2. It is used in oncology research as a tool for studying the p53–HDM2 interaction and, in some studies, as a targeting ligand for drug carriers.
