## Why This Compound

P21 belongs to a family of small neurotrophic peptide mimetics developed in Khalid Iqbal's laboratory at the New York State Institute for Basic Research. The lead compound, P021, was designed as a short sequence from the biologically active region of human ciliary neurotrophic factor (CNTF). The idea behind a mimetic is straightforward: whole neurotrophic factor proteins are large, poorly brain-penetrant, and short-lived in circulation, so a small peptide that reproduces part of their signalling could be a more practical research tool.

The published preclinical work is concentrated in a few models. In the 3xTg-AD mouse, P021 given from birth to postnatal day 120 was reported to prevent the development of the behavioural deficits and to increase pCREB and BDNF expression, with changes in PLC/PKC, MEK/ERK and PI3K/Akt signalling. A 2020 study using a prenatal-to-early-postnatal protocol reported fewer amyloid and tau pathologies in the same model. In a CDKL5 deficiency model, P021 restored neuronal proliferation and maturation in vitro, but chronic treatment in vivo did not raise BDNF or correct neuroanatomical defects. The mixed result is useful: it shows the peptide is not a guaranteed brain-active agent, and it makes the route of administration and exposure a legitimate part of the research question.

There is a naming problem inside the literature. One methods section prints P021 as Ac-DGGLAG-NH2, a six-residue chain, while other papers describe it as a tetrapeptide. The catalogue does not print a sequence at all. A buyer comparing two suppliers may therefore be comparing two different molecules sold under one name. The [molecular weight verification note](/blog/peptide-molecular-weight-verification-steps/) explains why this matters more for a short peptide than for a protein: a two-residue difference is a large fraction of the total mass and a large change in receptor-facing geometry.

The catalogue's older description also called P21 a peptide derived from BDNF loop domains. The primary papers place P021 in the CNTF mimetic family, and its reported effect on BDNF expression is downstream, not a sequence relationship. That distinction is not pedantry. If you are selecting a reagent because you want a BDNF-loop peptide, P21 may not be the molecule you think it is.

## What the Batch Record Should Show

For an acetylated, amidated peptide, identity is not one number. It is a set of four: the amino acid composition or sequence, the N-terminal acetyl group, the C-terminal amide, and the intact mass. A certificate that reports only the intact mass can be consistent with several sequences of similar composition, especially when the peptide is short.

The mass spectrum should show the singly and doubly charged ions with the charge states labelled, and the deconvoluted mass should match the calculated mass for the declared sequence plus acetyl (+42.01 Da) and amide (−0.98 Da relative to the free acid). If the certificate prints a nominal molecular weight to one decimal place, it has not confirmed the terminal groups. Ask for the calculated value and the observed value in the same row.

Purity by HPLC needs method detail. For a peptide this short, the main peak can hide truncation products that differ by one or two residues and elute close to the parent. The certificate should name the column, mobile phase, gradient, wavelength and integration method. Area normalisation at 214 nm is the usual approach; it measures UV-absorbing material and says nothing about water, counterion or residual solvents. A separate peptide-content assay is what converts fill weight into peptide mass.

Counterion matters here because the peptide is small. If the vial contains the acetate salt, the acetate mass is a meaningful fraction of the total. If it contains residual trifluoroacetate from purification, the same is true. The certificate should name the counterion and, where available, report residual TFA. Karl Fischer water content is the other half of the mass balance.

Endotoxin is worth asking about even for a catalogue peptide. Short synthetic peptides are not automatically endotoxin-free, and primary neuronal cultures are sensitive to lipopolysaccharide. A certificate that omits endotoxin is not necessarily deficient, but the omission should be a known gap rather than a surprise discovered after the cells are plated. The [quality pillar](/peptide-quality-testing/) sets out the difference between release testing and a research-grade specification.

Batch traceability closes the loop. The lot number on the certificate should match the vial label, and the certificate should carry a synthesis date, a retest date, and the manufacturing site. A document that could apply to any P21 batch is a template, not a record.

## Sourcing & Verification

Begin with the sequence. Ask the supplier to state it in writing, including the N-terminal acetyl and the C-terminal amide. If the answer is "P21, 10 mg, 98%," you have learned that the supplier does not control the molecule's definition. The [manufacturer verification protocol](/blog/china-peptide-manufacturer-verification-a-2026-b2b-sourcing-protocol/) covers the entity checks that should come before a first order.

Ask whether the batch is the four-residue or six-residue version. The literature uses both descriptions, and a supplier may not know that the distinction exists. A manufacturer that synthesises the peptide will have a sequence file and a batch record; a reseller will have a product name. The difference in answer quality is usually obvious within one email.

Ask for the chromatogram and mass spectrum as separate files. Combined PDFs are easy to assemble and hard to audit. A raw instrument file is better, though many suppliers will not release it. Even a separate PDF with the method header visible is more useful than a certificate that pastes a peak table without a method.

Ask about the counterion and the peptide content. For a 10 mg vial at this price point, the difference between 10 mg of peptide and 10 mg of peptide plus salt plus water can be 20–40% of the active mass. That difference propagates directly into every concentration you calculate. Ask about storage and shipping. Lyophilized P21 should travel at ambient temperature for short periods without harm, but a cold-chain option reduces the risk of moisture ingress during a long international leg. ## Handling & Storage

Hold the powder at −20°C, desiccated and protected from light. Bring the vial to room temperature before opening. A cold vial in a warm room condenses water on the glass and, once the cap is removed, on the powder. Wipe the outside, wait, then open.

P21 is a short, polar peptide and usually dissolves in water or phosphate-buffered saline. Add the solvent down the wall of the vial rather than onto the cake, and mix by gentle inversion. Vortexing creates foam, and a short peptide at a foam interface can adsorb to the glass or aggregate. If the solution is cloudy, let it stand for a few minutes before adding more solvent; sometimes the haze is a transient aggregate that disperses, and sometimes it is undissolved material that will not.

For stock solutions, use the buffer your assay needs rather than a generic one. PBS, Tris and HEPES have different ionic strengths and can change peptide behaviour in cell culture. Record the buffer, the pH, and the nominal concentration in the batch notebook. A concentration calculated from fill weight rather than peptide content is a systematic error that is easy to avoid and hard to detect later.

Aliquot before freezing. Aqueous P21 is a short-term stock at 2–8°C; repeated freeze-thaw cycles promote aggregation and adsorption. Divide into single-use portions, freeze at −20°C or below, and discard rather than refreeze. For step-by-step reconstitution, see the [reconstitution guide](/blog/peptide-lyophilization-reconstitution-guide-for-researchers/); the [handling pillar](/peptide-handling-protocols/) covers storage and aliquoting.

**P21 Adamantane.** The same parent sequence with a lipophilic cap. The cap changes solubility, mass and probably metabolic stability, but no published study has isolated its effect. If your question is about the P021 sequence itself, the unmodified peptide is the cleaner reagent. The [P21 Adamantane page](/catalog/p21-adamantane/) lists the modified format.

**Semax and Selank.** Short neuropeptides with large, mostly Russian-language literatures and different sequences. They are often grouped with P21 in catalogues because they share a research category, not because they share a mechanism. A result obtained with Semax does not validate a P021 hypothesis. ## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is P21 the same as P021?**

P21 is the catalogue name for the P021 family. The published literature uses P021 for the CNTF-derived peptide mimetic. The catalogue does not print a sequence, so the only way to confirm that a specific vial is the same molecule as the published P021 is to compare the batch certificate with the sequence in the paper.

**What is the correct sequence?**

One primary paper reports Ac-DGGLAG-NH2; other papers describe P021 as a tetrapeptide without printing the sequence. The discrepancy has not been resolved in the literature. Ask the supplier which sequence the batch was made to, and ask for the mass spectrum that matches it. Do not assume that two products sold as P21 are the same molecule.

**Why does the catalogue description mention BDNF?**

The older catalogue copy described P21 as a BDNF loop-domain peptide. The primary literature places P021 in the CNTF mimetic family; BDNF appears in the studies as a downstream marker whose expression changes after treatment. The description has been corrected here, but buyers may still encounter the older wording on reseller sites.

**How should I store the powder?**

At −20°C, desiccated and protected from light. Let the vial equilibrate to room temperature before opening. Under those conditions the dry powder is stable for extended periods; the retest date should come from the batch record, not from a generic web page.

**What solvent should I use for reconstitution?**

Water or phosphate-buffered saline for most assays. Add the solvent gently, mix by inversion, and avoid vortexing. If you need a stock in DMSO, keep the final organic concentration as low as the assay tolerates and run a vehicle control.

**Is the product sterile or endotoxin-free?**

No. Lyophilized research peptides are not sterile by default, and endotoxin testing is a separate assay. If your cultures are sensitive to LPS, request the LAL result for the lot or plan a filtration step and a limulus control.

**Can I use the catalogue purity as a concentration reference?**

No. Purity by HPLC area normalisation is not peptide content. Use the certificate's peptide-content value, or measure it, before calculating molar concentrations. The [purity versus content note](/blog/why-purity-and-concentration-tests-are-not-enough/) explains the arithmetic.

**Why is P21 cheaper than the adamantane version?**

The unmodified peptide is a standard synthesis; the adamantane version requires a conjugation step, a purification that separates modified from unmodified material, and a smaller batch. The price difference reflects process, not necessarily a difference in biological activity.
