PEG-MGF is the polyethylene-glycol conjugate of mechano growth factor, the IGF-1 splice variant also called IGF-1Ec. The catalogue sells it as a 2 mg lyophilized vial in a 10-vial pack, which is a smaller fill than most peptides here for a simple reason: the conjugate is bulky, and a 2 mg fill of a PEGylated peptide can contain less peptide mass than a 2 mg fill of the parent. The label weight is not the peptide weight. That is the first thing to establish before calculating anything.

The second thing is what "PEGylated" means for this batch. PEG is a polymer with a distribution of chain lengths, not a single molecular weight. A conjugate can be mono-PEGylated at one site, multi-PEGylated at several sites, or a mixture, and those forms behave differently in solution. A certificate that says "PEG-MGF, ≥98%" without describing the PEG distribution has described a family, not a molecule.

## Why This Compound

Mechano growth factor is an alternative splice variant of the IGF-1 gene. In humans, the IGF-1Ec transcript contains a 49-base insertion that shifts the reading frame and produces a distinct E domain. A synthetic peptide corresponding to the last 24 amino acids of that E domain — often written MGF-24aa-E — has been used to study the variant's biology without the mature IGF-1 sequence.

The most cited cell study used primary human muscle cultures from donors of different ages. MGF-E peptide increased proliferative lifespan and delayed senescence in satellite cells from neonatal and young adult donors, but not from old adult donors. Treated cultures showed hypertrophy and a lower percentage of reserve cells. The authors concluded that the E peptide alone could activate satellite cells and promote fusion, and proposed it as a research route for muscle maintenance that avoids the oncogenic concerns associated with IGF-1. That is a cell-culture result in a specific donor population, not a whole-organism outcome, and the age dependence is part of the finding.

A 2014 review of the E-domain literature noted that IGF-1Ec expression rises after injury in skeletal muscle, during post-infarct myocardial remodelling, and in some cancers. That last point is not a footnote. Any research programme using an IGF-1 pathway peptide should know that the same pathway appears in tumour biology, and should design controls accordingly.

PEGylation is a formulation strategy, not a biological one. Attaching PEG chains increases hydrodynamic radius, reduces renal filtration and can shield the peptide from proteases. A clinical PEGylated IGF-1 analogue achieved a half-life of 140–200 hours in a first-in-human study, which illustrates how large the effect can be. It does not tell you the half-life of this product, because the PEG size, the conjugation site and the peptide are different.

## What the Batch Record Should Show

Start with the conjugation distribution. MALDI-TOF or SEC-MALS can show whether the batch is predominantly mono-PEGylated or a mixture of mono-, di- and unreacted species. A single peak on RP-HPLC is not enough: PEG chains are invisible at 214 nm, and the peptide chromatogram may look clean while the PEG distribution is broad. The certificate should report the distribution, not just the peptide purity.

Ask for the free peptide content. Unreacted MGF E-peptide is the most likely impurity in a conjugation reaction, and it is biologically active in its own right. If the batch contains 10% free peptide, a study using the conjugate is also testing the parent. The certificate should quantify free peptide by a method that can separate it from the conjugate, such as SEC or ion-exchange chromatography.

Ask for the PEG reagent residuals. Activation chemistry can leave reactive groups or breakdown products, and some PEG reagents carry UV-absorbing linkers. The certificate should name the linker chemistry if it is known, and report residual free PEG where measured. This is a documentation ask that many catalogue suppliers cannot meet; the answer itself is informative.

Peptide content is the next field. A 2 mg fill of a PEGylated peptide may contain 0.5–1.5 mg of peptide depending on the PEG mass and the number of chains. Karl Fischer water content and an amino-acid-analysis-based peptide content assay are the two measurements that convert fill into peptide mass. Without them, a nominal concentration can be wrong by a factor of two or more.

Purity by HPLC needs method detail, and the method should be chosen for the conjugate rather than inherited from the parent. PEGylated peptides often elute as broad peaks on reversed-phase columns, and the apparent purity depends on the gradient. The certificate should show the chromatogram with the method header and the integration parameters. A "≥98%" claim from a method that cannot resolve the conjugate from the free peptide is not a purity claim in any useful sense.

Endotoxin and sterility are separate questions. If the material will be used in cell culture, ask for LAL data and a lot-specific result. A lyophilized powder is not sterile by default. The [quality pillar](/peptide-quality-testing/) explains why these tests are not interchangeable with a purity number.

## Sourcing & Verification

Ask three questions before price. What is the PEG molecular weight? Where is it attached? What fraction of the batch is mono-PEGylated? A supplier who can answer all three has a defined product. A supplier who answers only the first has a catalogue entry.

Ask for the sequence of the peptide portion and the conjugation site. MGF E-peptide has several lysines and an N-terminus, and conjugation at different sites produces different molecules. The [modification overview](/blog/how-peptide-modifications-affect-activity/) explains why site selection matters more than the presence of a PEG chain.

Ask for a batch-specific certificate with the conjugation distribution. If the supplier provides a certificate that reports only "purity 98%" and a molecular weight, the document does not describe the product. The [CoA guide](/blog/how-to-read-a-peptide-coa-a-researchers-guide-to-hplc-and-ms-reports/) lists the fields that should be present.

Ask whether the product is made to order. PEG conjugates are usually custom, and the first lot may be the only lot. That is acceptable for exploratory work, but it means there is no historical batch data and the second order may differ in PEG distribution. The [manufacturer verification protocol](/blog/china-peptide-manufacturer-verification-a-2026-b2b-sourcing-protocol/) covers the site checks that should come first.

Ask about shipping and storage. PEGylated peptides can be hygroscopic, and a lyophilized cake that takes up water may become gummy and hard to dissolve. A desiccant and a cold-chain option are cheap insurance for an international order. The [sourcing pillar](/china-peptide-sourcing/) covers the document set.

## Handling & Storage

Keep the lyophilized powder at −20°C, desiccated and protected from light. Let the vial equilibrate to room temperature before opening. PEGylated peptides can be more hygroscopic than the parent, and a cold vial opened in a warm room may pull moisture into the cake.

Reconstitute in water or buffer. Add the solvent down the wall of the vial and mix by gentle inversion. Do not vortex; PEGylated peptides foam readily, and foam concentrates the material at the air-liquid interface where it can aggregate. If the solution is cloudy, let it stand; if the haze persists, centrifuge at low speed and inspect the pellet before adding more solvent.

For cell culture, prepare a concentrated stock and dilute into medium. Keep the final buffer concentration low and record the PEG concentration, because PEG itself can affect cell behaviour at high concentrations. A vehicle control with the same PEG concentration is not optional if the readout is sensitive.

Aliquot before freezing. Aqueous PEG conjugates are short-term stocks at 2–8°C; repeated freeze-thaw cycles can cause phase separation or aggregation. Divide into single-use portions, freeze at −20°C or colder, and do not refreeze. The [reconstitution guide](/blog/peptide-lyophilization-reconstitution-guide-for-researchers/) covers the mechanics, and the [handling pillar](/peptide-handling-protocols/) covers the storage schedule.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is PEG-MGF?**

A polyethylene-glycol conjugate of the mechano growth factor E-domain peptide, supplied as a lyophilized powder for laboratory research. The PEG chain is intended to increase hydrodynamic radius and extend residence time relative to the unmodified peptide.

**What is the PEG molecular weight in this product?**

The catalogue does not publish it. That is a specification gap, not a minor detail: PEG size determines the conjugate's hydrodynamic radius, its analytical behaviour and its concentration in cell culture. Ask the supplier for the PEG molecular weight and the conjugation site before ordering.

**How is PEGylation confirmed?**

By a combination of mass spectrometry and a separation method that resolves the conjugate from free peptide, such as SEC or ion-exchange chromatography. A single RP-HPLC peak is not sufficient because PEG is UV-transparent and the peptide chromatogram can look clean while the PEG distribution is broad.

**What purity and form are supplied?**

Lyophilized powder, ≥98% by the catalogue specification, 2 mg per vial in 10-vial packs. The certificate should state the conjugation distribution, free peptide content, counterion, water content and peptide content.

**How should I store the powder?**

At −20°C, desiccated and protected from light. Equilibrate the vial to room temperature before opening. Reconstituted solutions are short-term stocks at 2–8°C and should be aliquoted before freezing.

**Can I use it in cell culture?**

Only after confirming identity, free peptide content and endotoxin status. PEG itself can affect cell behaviour, so a vehicle control at the same PEG concentration is needed. The catalogue purity specification is not a cell-culture qualification.

**Is PEG-MGF the same as IGF-1?**

No. MGF is an IGF-1 splice variant with a distinct E domain; PEG-MGF is a conjugate of the E-domain peptide, not the mature IGF-1 sequence. The biological effects reported for MGF-E in cell culture are not interchangeable with those of IGF-1.

**Why is the fill only 2 mg?**

Because the conjugate is bulky. A 2 mg fill contains the peptide plus the PEG chain plus counterion and water, so the peptide mass is a fraction of the label weight. Check the peptide-content value before calculating molar concentrations.

## The Author's Take

**Position.** I would not buy PEG-MGF without the PEG molecular weight and a conjugation distribution. A conjugate with an unknown PEG size is not a defined reagent, and a purity number cannot fix that.

**Reasoning.** The published MGF literature is about the E-domain peptide, not about a PEG conjugate. PEGylation changes the molecule's size, its clearance and its assay behaviour; those changes may be useful, but they are not inherited from the parent's data. The most common failure in catalogue PEG-peptides is not "no peptide" but "a mixture of conjugate forms with an unstated free-peptide fraction." That mixture will behave inconsistently between lots, and the inconsistency will look like biology. A supplier who reports the distribution is selling a characterised product. A supplier who reports only a purity percentage is selling a label.
