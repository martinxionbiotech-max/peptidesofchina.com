The design is unusually specific for a catalogue peptide. It does not activate a receptor or inhibit an enzyme; it interrupts a protein–protein interaction inside the cell, and the published work is largely about what happens to Wnt/β-catenin signalling when that interaction is blocked.

## Why This Compound

CXXC5 is a zinc-finger protein that binds Dishevelled and acts as a negative regulator of the Wnt/β-catenin pathway. In skin, the pathway is involved in hair follicle cycling and wound repair. A 2015 study in the *Journal of Experimental Medicine* showed that CXXC5-deficient mice healed cutaneous wounds faster and that the protein regulates myofibroblast differentiation and collagen production. A 2017 study in the *Journal of Investigative Dermatology* showed that CXXC5 was upregulated in miniaturised hair follicles and arrector pili muscle in balding scalps, and that disrupting the CXXC5–Dishevelled interaction with a competing peptide activated Wnt/β-catenin signalling and accelerated hair regrowth and wound-induced hair follicle neogenesis in mice.

That competing peptide is the DBM sequence. PTD-DBM adds a protein transduction domain so that the peptide can enter cells; without the carrier, a short motif peptide would have limited access to the intracellular target. The construct is therefore a tool for asking a specific question: what happens when the CXXC5–Dishevelled interaction is disrupted in a given cell or tissue model?

The answer is not a simple "more Wnt." A 2023 study in *Cells* linked CXXC5 to DHT-induced androgenetic alopecia through prostaglandin D2, which suggests that the protein sits in a larger regulatory network rather than a linear pathway. A 2025 review in *Cells* surveyed hair-regrowth approaches that target follicle neogenesis and Wnt/β-catenin, and placed CXXC5 among several competing hypotheses. A 2023 paper in *Advanced Healthcare Materials* used a hydrogel patch to deliver combination therapy for wound healing, which is a reminder that delivery format can matter as much as the peptide in skin research.

## What the Batch Record Should Show

The certificate should print the full fusion sequence, including the PTD, the linker and the DBM motif. A fusion peptide has more possible truncation products than a single-domain peptide, and the mass alone may not distinguish a full-length product from a truncated one that lost part of the PTD. Tandem MS or a peptide map after enzymatic digestion is the appropriate identity check.

Purity by HPLC should be reported with method detail. A 20-residue fusion peptide is likely to retain on a C18 column, but the PTD portion is often highly basic and can cause peak tailing. The certificate should show the gradient, the column, the wavelength and the integration parameters, and it should report the significant impurities separately. A single "98%" line is a summary, not a profile.

Counterion and peptide content are the mass-balance fields. The peptide is commonly supplied as the acetate or trifluoroacetate salt, and the counterion mass is a meaningful fraction of a 5 mg fill. Karl Fischer water content and a peptide-content assay convert fill weight into peptide mass. The [purity versus content note](/blog/why-purity-and-concentration-tests-are-not-enough/) explains why a 98% purity number can coexist with a lower peptide content.

Endotoxin is a priority for a peptide used in cell culture. The published work used cells and mouse models, and the readouts — Wnt reporter activity, proliferation, wound closure — are sensitive to contamination. Ask whether the lot was tested by LAL, what the limit was, and whether the result is lot-specific. Sterility is a separate test; a lyophilized powder is not sterile by default. The [sterility guide](/blog/sterility-testing-for-research-peptides-a-protocol-guide/) covers the options.

Functional identity is a separate question from chemical identity. A batch can be the right sequence and still fail to compete with CXXC5 if the DBM motif is misfolded or if the PTD does not deliver it. A supplier who claims activity should be able to describe a competition assay, a Wnt reporter assay, or a binding experiment. A certificate of analysis does not establish function. The [testing guide](/blog/peptide-testing-and-analysis-a-professional-guide-to-laboratory-verification/) explains the difference.

Batch traceability closes the record. The lot number on the certificate should match the vial, and the certificate should name the synthesis site and the date. For a fusion peptide made in small batches, traceability is the only way to compare one lot with the next.

## Handling & Storage

Hold the lyophilized powder at −20°C, desiccated and protected from light. Bring the vial to room temperature before opening. A cold vial in a warm room condenses water on the glass, and a basic fusion peptide can take up moisture quickly.

Reconstitute in water or buffer. The PTD portion is highly charged and usually improves solubility, but the DBM motif may be hydrophobic. If the peptide does not dissolve readily, add a small volume of DMSO and then dilute into buffer, keeping the final organic concentration as low as the assay tolerates. Run a vehicle control at the same concentration.

Avoid vortexing and heating. A fusion peptide with a hydrophobic motif can aggregate at an air-liquid interface, and heat can promote deamidation. Mix by inversion or gentle rotation. If the solution is cloudy, centrifuge at low speed and inspect the pellet before adding more solvent.

For cell culture, prepare a concentrated stock, filter it through a 0.22 µm membrane if sterility is required, and dilute into medium. Check that the peptide does not adsorb to the filter; a basic peptide can bind to some membrane materials. Reconstitution mechanics are covered in the [reconstitution guide](/blog/peptide-lyophilization-reconstitution-guide-for-researchers/), and the [handling pillar](/peptide-handling-protocols/) covers the wider workflow.

Aliquot before freezing. Aqueous solutions are short-term stocks at 2–8°C; repeated freeze-thaw cycles promote aggregation. Split into single-use aliquots, freeze at −20°C or colder, and never refreeze.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is PTD-DBM?**

A chimeric peptide composed of a protein transduction domain and a Dishevelled-binding motif. The DBM portion competes with CXXC5 for binding to Dishevelled, which can activate Wnt/β-catenin signalling in cells. The PTD portion is intended to carry the peptide into cells.

**What is CXXC5?**

A zinc-finger protein that binds Dishevelled and acts as a negative regulator of the Wnt/β-catenin pathway. It has been studied in hair follicle biology, wound healing and androgenetic alopecia. It is a protein target, not a receptor.

**Is PTD-DBM the same as the peptide in the hair-regrowth papers?**

The 2017 *Journal of Investigative Dermatology* paper used a competing peptide to disrupt the CXXC5–Dishevelled interaction. The catalogue product is described as PTD-DBM, but the catalogue does not publish the PTD or DBM sequences. Ask the supplier for the full sequence and compare it with the paper before assuming they are identical.

**What purity and form are supplied?**

Lyophilized powder, ≥98% by the catalogue specification, 5 mg per vial in 10-vial packs. The certificate should state the full sequence, the counterion, the water content and the peptide content.

**Is the product sterile or endotoxin-free?**

No. Lyophilized research peptides are not sterile by default, and endotoxin testing is a separate assay. If the material will be used with cells, request the LAL result for the lot or plan a filtration step and a limulus control.

**How should I store it?**

At −20°C, desiccated and protected from light. Reconstituted solutions are short-term stocks at 2–8°C and should be aliquoted before freezing. Avoid repeated freeze-thaw cycles.

**Can I use PTD-DBM in animal models?**

The published work used mouse models, but any in vivo study requires its own ethics approval, formulation work and range-finding. The catalogue product is supplied for laboratory research only.

**Why is the sequence not published?**

The catalogue treats the construct as proprietary. That is a commercial choice, not a scientific one. For a fusion peptide, the sequence determines uptake, toxicity and target engagement, so a buyer should ask for it in writing before designing a study.
