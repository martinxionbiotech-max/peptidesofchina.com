P21 Adamantane is the modified member of a two-product family. The parent peptide, P21, is a short synthetic sequence modelled on the active region of ciliary neurotrophic factor (CNTF); the adamantane version carries a lipophilic hydrocarbon cage intended to change how the peptide behaves in solution and in tissue. Almost everything published about this family concerns the parent. Almost nothing published concerns the capped analogue. That asymmetry is the first fact a buyer needs, because it determines what can be verified and what has to be taken on the supplier's word.

## Why This Compound

The P21 family descends from work on neurotrophic factor mimetics in Khalid Iqbal's laboratory at the New York State Institute for Basic Research. The best-documented member is P021, a short acetylated, C-terminally amidated peptide derived from the biologically active region of human CNTF. Published descriptions have called it a tetrapeptide, while at least one methods section prints the sequence Ac-DGGLAG-NH2 — a six-residue chain. That inconsistency is worth knowing before you compare suppliers, because two vendors can use the same name for materially different molecules.

The preclinical record for P021 is narrow but specific. In the 3xTg-AD mouse, treatment from birth to postnatal day 120 was reported to prevent the emergence of the behavioural deficits and to raise pCREB and BDNF expression, with downstream changes in PLC/PKC, MEK/ERK and PI3K/Akt signalling. A 2020 study treated 3xTg-AD mice prenatally and into early postnatal life and reported fewer amyloid and tau pathologies. The same peptide restored neuronal proliferation and maturation in a CDKL5-deficient cell model, but chronic treatment in CDKL5 knockout mice did not raise brain BDNF or correct neuroanatomy. That last result matters: it is a reminder that a peptide that works in a dish does not automatically reach or act in the brain.

Adamantane is a tricyclic C10H16 hydrocarbon used in medicinal chemistry as a bulky, lipophilic cap. Attaching it to the N-terminus of a peptide can block aminopeptidase attack and increase passive membrane partitioning. It also changes the molecule's mass by at least 136 Da, usually more once a linker is counted, and it can make the peptide markedly less water-soluble. These are general properties of the chemistry, not measurements made on this specific product. The catalogue's older copy claimed "enhanced CNS penetration relative to unmodified analogs." No published pharmacokinetic or brain-exposure dataset supports that sentence for this analogue, and a buyer should treat it as a hypothesis the batch cannot confirm.

## What the Batch Record Should Show

Start with the mass. An unmodified P21 batch should show the parent mass. An adamantane-conjugated batch should show a single dominant species at a mass consistent with the parent plus the cap and linker. If the certificate lists a molecular weight but no spectrum, it has not demonstrated conjugation. Ask for the deconvoluted mass spectrum with the observed and calculated values printed side by side.

Then ask for the chromatogram at two wavelengths. At 214 nm you see the peptide backbone; at 280 nm you see aromatic residues, if the sequence has any. A conjugation reaction that leaves 15% free parent can still produce a main peak that integrates at 98% of total UV area if the free parent co-elutes or elutes in the solvent front. The certificate should report free parent and di-adduct as separate, quantified impurities, not fold them into a single purity number.

The modification itself should be localised. A single mass shift tells you that something was added; it does not tell you whether the cap sits on the N-terminus, a lysine side chain, or a tyrosine. Tandem MS fragmentation, or a peptide map after enzymatic digestion, can distinguish those outcomes. For a research peptide sold at this price, a one-page structure confirmation is a reasonable request. If the supplier cannot provide it, the product is a custom synthesis with an unverified structure, and it should be priced accordingly.

Counterion and peptide content belong in the same conversation. Adamantane-modified peptides are often purified by reversed-phase HPLC with trifluoroacetic acid, and TFA can remain in the lyophilized powder. The certificate should name the counterion and give residual TFA or acetate where measured. Karl Fischer water content and an amino-acid-analysis or nitrogen-based peptide content assay convert fill weight into peptide mass. Without those values, a 10 mg vial is 10 mg of powder, not 10 mg of peptide.

Endotoxin testing is not universal for catalogue peptides, but it is hard to work without in primary cell culture. Ask whether the batch was tested by LAL, what the limit was, and whether the result applies to the lot on the label. The same applies to sterility: a lyophilized powder is not sterile by default, and "research grade" is not a sterility claim.

Finally, check that the batch record names the manufacturing site and the synthesis date. A custom conjugate can be made in a different facility from the parent peptide, and the certificate should say which site performed the conjugation. A certificate without a site is a document without an owner. ## Sourcing & Verification

The first question is not price. It is whether the supplier can draw the molecule. A trading company that buys "adamantane peptide" from a broker will usually have a catalogue number and a certificate template; it will not have the conjugation route, the linker identity, or the purification method. A manufacturer can answer all three, and the answers will be specific: which resin, which coupling reagent, which position, which cleavage cocktail.

Ask for the parent sequence in writing. The P21 literature uses more than one sequence length, and the catalogue does not print a sequence at all. If the supplier cannot state whether the parent is the four-residue or six-residue version, the adamantane product is built on an undefined starting point. The [modification overview](/blog/how-peptide-modifications-affect-activity/) explains why a single residue change can alter solubility and assay behaviour more than the label suggests.

Ask how the conjugate is purified. Preparative HPLC can separate modified from unmodified peptide if the retention difference is adequate; if it is not, the batch may be enriched rather than purified. A certificate that reports only "≥98%" without an impurity profile is not enough to distinguish those cases.

Ask about batch size and lead time. A 10-vial pack of a custom conjugate is often made to order, which means the lot you receive may be the first lot ever made at that scale. That is acceptable for exploratory work, but it changes the risk profile: there is no historical batch-to-batch dataset, and the first repeat order may differ. The [manufacturer verification protocol](/blog/china-peptide-manufacturer-verification-a-2026-b2b-sourcing-protocol/) sets out the entity checks that should precede a custom order.

Ask about shipping temperature. Lyophilized peptides are stable at ambient temperature for short periods, but a custom conjugate with a hydrophobic cap can be more sensitive to moisture. A cold-chain option and a desiccant pack are cheap insurance. The [sourcing pillar](/china-peptide-sourcing/) covers the document set that should travel with an international shipment.

## Handling & Storage

Store the vial at −20°C, desiccated, protected from light. Let it equilibrate to room temperature before opening; condensation on a cold vial is the most common way a dry peptide acquires water. Wipe the outside, wait thirty minutes, then open in the driest space available.

The adamantane cap makes this peptide more hydrophobic than the parent. If it does not dissolve readily in water or buffer, dissolve the powder in a small volume of DMSO or dimethylformamide first, then dilute into aqueous buffer while stirring gently. Keep the final organic solvent concentration as low as the assay tolerates, and run a vehicle control at the same concentration. Do not heat the solution to force dissolution; heat promotes aggregation and can hydrolyse the C-terminal amide.

Avoid vortexing. A hydrophobic peptide at an air-liquid interface is prone to adsorption and self-assembly, and foaming concentrates it there. Mix by inversion or gentle rotation. If the solution is hazy, centrifuge at low speed and inspect the pellet before adding more solvent; sometimes the haze is undissolved material, sometimes it is aggregate.

Aliquot before freezing. Aqueous solutions of modified peptides are short-lived compared with the dry powder, and repeated freeze-thaw cycles encourage aggregation. Split into single-use aliquots, freeze at −20°C or colder, and never refreeze. For long-term storage, keep the powder dry and cold; the [handling pillar](/peptide-handling-protocols/) covers the general schedule, and the [stability note](/blog/peptide-stability-why-lyophilized-peptides-are-far-more-stable-than-you-think/) explains why the dry state is the better default.

All of this is bench handling. The product is supplied for laboratory research only and is not intended for administration to humans or animals.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is P21 Adamantane the same compound as P021?**

P21 is the catalogue name for the P021 family of CNTF-derived neurotrophic peptide mimetics. The adamantane version is a modified analogue, not the same molecule as unmodified P021. The published P021 studies used the unmodified peptide, so their results cannot be transferred to the capped analogue without new data.

**What is the purpose of the adamantane group?**

Adamantane is a bulky, lipophilic hydrocarbon cage used in medicinal chemistry to block N-terminal metabolism and increase passive membrane partitioning. In a peptide, it can also reduce aqueous solubility and change aggregation behaviour. Those are general chemistry effects; the magnitude for this specific peptide has not been published.

**Has brain penetration been measured for this product?**

No published pharmacokinetic or brain-exposure dataset is available for the adamantyl P21 analogue. The catalogue's older claim of enhanced CNS penetration is a hypothesis based on the lipophilicity of adamantane, not a measurement. Ask for a study report if a supplier repeats the claim.

**How can I confirm that the adamantane is attached?**

Request a batch-specific mass spectrum showing the mass shift relative to the parent, plus tandem MS or a peptide map that locates the modification. A certificate that prints only a molecular weight has not confirmed conjugation. The [molecular weight verification note](/blog/peptide-molecular-weight-verification-steps/) explains the difference between a catalogue value and a measured one.

**What solvent should I use?**

Try water or phosphate-buffered saline first. If the peptide is not soluble, dissolve in a small volume of DMSO and dilute into buffer, keeping the final organic content as low as the assay allows. Run a vehicle control at the same DMSO concentration. Avoid heating and vortexing.

**Why is there no CAS number?**

The product is a custom conjugate whose linker chemistry is not published. CAS numbers are assigned to defined structures; without a defined linker, there is no single structure to register. A supplier who prints a CAS number for this product is probably quoting the parent peptide.

**Is the powder sterile?**

No. Lyophilized research peptides are not sterile by default. If your work requires sterile material, filter the reconstituted solution through a 0.22 µm membrane and verify that the peptide does not adsorb to the filter. The [sterility testing guide](/blog/sterility-testing-for-research-peptides-a-protocol-guide/) covers the alternatives.

**Can I order a custom batch?**

Yes, and for a conjugate this is often the honest route. Specify the parent sequence, the linker, the conjugation position, the counterion, and the tests you require. Expect a longer lead time and a batch-specific certificate rather than a catalogue document.
