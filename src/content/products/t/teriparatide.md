## Why This Compound

Parathyroid hormone is an 84-residue peptide secreted by the parathyroid glands. Its N-terminal 34 residues contain the PTH1 receptor activation domain, and the synthetic fragment PTH(1-34) has been the standard research agonist for that receptor since the 1970s. PTH1R is a class B G protein-coupled receptor expressed on osteoblasts, osteocytes, renal tubular cells, and several other tissues. Activation couples to Gs and raises cAMP; it also engages beta-arrestin and intracellular calcium depending on the ligand and the exposure pattern.

The exposure pattern is the central mechanistic variable in bone research. Continuous PTH exposure and intermittent PTH exposure produce different effects on bone remodelling in animal models, and the difference is thought to reflect the balance between osteoblast and osteoclast activity, the duration of receptor occupancy, and the downstream signalling bias. A researcher who uses PTH(1-34) in a cell model is usually studying one of three things: cAMP/PKA signalling, beta-arrestin recruitment, or the transcriptional response in osteoblast-lineage cells. A researcher who uses it in a mouse model is usually studying the remodelling response to a defined exposure schedule.

Those are preclinical questions. Medical use of teriparatide is outside the scope of this page, and a research vial should not be treated as a substitute for a pharmaceutical product. The purity specification, the counterion, and the aggregation profile of a research batch are not the same as a licensed drug's release specification, even when the sequence is identical.

The other reason the compound matters is analytical. PTH(1-34) is a 34-mer with a hydrophobic N-terminal region and a charged C-terminal region. It adsorbs to surfaces, aggregates at high concentration, and oxidises at methionine residues. A batch that is 98% pure by RP-HPLC can still contain soluble aggregates that are invisible on the chromatogram and active in a cell assay. That is the failure mode this page is written to catch.

## What the Batch Record Should Show

The first item is the expression or synthesis route. If the material is recombinant, the certificate should name the expression host, the fermentation scale, and the purification train. If it is synthetic, the certificate should name the synthesis scale, the resin, and the purification method. The route determines the impurity profile: recombinant material can carry host-cell proteins and DNA, while synthetic material can carry deletion sequences and residual solvents. A certificate that omits the route is not traceable.

The second item is identity. LC-MS should show the intact mass, and for a 34-mer the deconvoluted mass should match the theoretical value within the method's tolerance. Sequence confirmation by MS/MS or peptide mapping is stronger than a single intact mass, especially if the N-terminal region is the part that matters for receptor activation. A supplier that reports only "HPLC purity" has not confirmed identity.

The third item is purity and impurity profile. RP-HPLC should be run with a disclosed method, and the impurity table should list the main related substances: deletion sequences, oxidised methionine, and truncated fragments. The N-terminal methionine is susceptible to oxidation, and an oxidised species can be biologically less active. If the certificate reports a single purity figure without an impurity table, the buyer cannot tell whether the main peak is the intended sequence.

The fourth item is aggregation. Size-exclusion chromatography should be run on the reconstituted solution as well as the lyophilized powder. PTH(1-34) can form dimers and higher-order aggregates at high concentration or at neutral pH, and the aggregate may not be visible on RP-HPLC. A batch with 98% monomer by SEC is a different material from a batch with 90% monomer and 8% aggregate. For cell assays, the aggregate can trigger a different signalling response or a non-specific effect.

The fifth item is counterion and water content. Teriparatide is supplied as the acetate salt in the pharmaceutical format, and research batches may be acetate or trifluoroacetate. The certificate should state the counterion and the acetate content by ion chromatography. Water content by Karl Fischer should be below the lyophilization specification. Residual TFA should be reported if the material is for cell culture.

For cell work, endotoxin and bioburden results are required. A 10 mg vial is a large fill for a cell assay, and a contaminated batch can ruin a primary osteoblast culture before the first readout. Sterility is a separate test and should not be inferred from filtration during fill.

## Sourcing & Verification

Teriparatide is a mature sequence, and many suppliers list it. The variation is in the route, the purification, and the documentation. A buyer who compares only the price per milligram is comparing a recombinant pharmaceutical-grade API with a synthetic research-grade peptide and treating them as the same product. They are not.

The first sourcing question is the route. If the supplier says "recombinant," ask for the expression system and the host-cell protein testing. If the supplier says "synthetic," ask for the synthesis scale and the impurity profile. A supplier that cannot answer is a reseller. The [research-grade versus GMP guide](/blog/research-grade-vs-gmp-peptides/) explains why the two categories have different documentation expectations.

The second question is the fill. A 10 mg vial of a 4.1 kDa peptide is a small amount of powder, and the fill can be expressed as peptide content or as total mass including counterion and water. Ask which basis the label uses. If the certificate says 10 mg by mass and the peptide content is 8.5 mg, the assay will be off before the first dilution.

The third question is aggregation. Ask for SEC data on the reconstituted solution, not just the lyophilized powder. If the supplier cannot provide it, test a small aliquot on arrival. A simple SEC-HPLC method with a phosphate buffer and a size-exclusion column can distinguish monomer from dimer and higher-order aggregate. The [molecular weight verification guide](/blog/peptide-molecular-weight-verification-steps/) covers the identity side.

The fourth question is the cold chain. Teriparatide is stable as a lyophilized powder at −20°C, but the reconstituted solution is less stable and should be handled according to the supplier's instructions. A shipment that arrives warm is not necessarily ruined, but the aggregate profile may have changed. Ask for the shipping conditions and the stability data that support them.

Independent verification should focus on identity and aggregation. Those are the two properties that a standard purity test can miss. A contract lab with LC-MS and SEC can confirm both from a single vial. ## Handling & Storage

Keep the vial at −20°C in a dry, dark place, and let it come to room temperature before breaking the seal. A cold vial opened in a humid room can pull moisture into the powder, and moisture accelerates aggregation and hydrolysis.

Reconstitution is sequence-specific. PTH(1-34) is soluble in water, but the solution is more stable at mildly acidic pH than at neutral pH. A common research buffer is 10–50 mM acetate at pH 4.5–5.5, or dilute hydrochloric acid in water, followed by dilution into the assay buffer. If the assay requires neutral pH, prepare the stock at acidic pH and dilute it into buffered medium immediately before use. Do not adjust the stock to pH 7 and leave it on the bench; that is a recipe for aggregation.

Use low-binding polypropylene tubes and avoid vigorous vortexing. PTH(1-34) adsorbs to glass and to some plastics, especially at low concentration. Add a carrier protein such as 0.1% bovine serum albumin if the assay tolerates it, or use a low-binding tube and a concentrated stock. Filter the buffer before adding it to the peptide; filtering the peptide solution can remove aggregate and change the concentration.

Aliquot before freezing. A freeze-thaw cycle can change the aggregate profile, and a 10 mg vial is too large for a single experiment. Store aliquots at −20°C or −80°C, and discard any aliquot that has been thawed and refrozen. The [handling pillar](/peptide-handling-protocols/) covers general laboratory practice; the [stability note](/blog/peptide-stability-why-lyophilized-peptides-are-far-more-stable-than-you-think/) discusses the dry-form advantage.

For cell work, make the final dilution in medium and bring it to assay temperature before application. Run a vehicle control at the same buffer and salt concentration. If the readout is cAMP, check the assay's dynamic range and the phosphodiesterase inhibitor concentration; those variables can swamp a small difference between batches.

## How It Compares

Teriparatide is one of several PTH-receptor ligands used in bone research. The full-length hormone, PTH(1-84), retains the C-terminal region that interacts with the PTH1R and may have additional signalling properties. Abaloparatide is a PTHrP analogue with a different receptor-binding profile. PTHrP(1-36) is another tool for the same receptor. The sequences are related but not interchangeable.
