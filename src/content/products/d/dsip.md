## Compound Profile

DSIP (Delta Sleep-Inducing Peptide) is a naturally occurring neuropeptide first isolated from rabbit cerebral venous blood during slow-wave sleep in 1977. The 9-amino acid sequence — Trp-Ala-Gly-Gly-Asp-Ala-Ser-Gly-Glu — exhibits amphiphilic properties that allow it to cross the blood-brain barrier, an unusual characteristic for peptides of its size. Research interest in DSIP spans neuroendocrine signaling, stress response modulation, and the neurochemistry of sleep-wake cycles. Preclinical studies in rodent models have examined DSIP in the context of EEG slow-wave activity, hypothalamic-pituitary axis interactions (particularly corticotropin and LH release), and stress-induced behavioral endpoints. Research has also explored DSIP's antioxidant properties — an unexpected finding for a neuropeptide — and its potential role as a modulator of cellular stress responses under conditions of oxidative challenge. The compound's amphiphilicity and CNS penetration make it a valuable pharmacological probe in neuroscience research.
