ARA-290 exists because erythropoietin does two unrelated things. One is to drive red blood cell production through the classical EPO receptor homodimer. The other is to protect tissue under stress through a receptor complex that EPO also activates but that does not require the erythropoietic machinery. Separating those two activities was the point of the molecule, and it is the reason an 11-residue peptide can be studied as a tissue-protective agent without the haematological effects of the parent hormone.

That separation is also the source of most of the confusion around it. ARA-290 is not EPO, does not act through the same receptor complex, and does not produce the same pharmacology. Buyers who treat it as a "small EPO" will misread the literature and, more practically, may accept a vial that is not the right peptide.

## Why This Compound

The innate repair receptor is a heterodimer of the EPO receptor and the beta common receptor, CD131. It is expressed at low levels in healthy tissue and rises in injured or stressed tissue — peripheral neurons, immune cells, pancreatic islets, renal tubules. Brines and colleagues described the heteroreceptor in 2004 and proposed it as the route through which EPO produces tissue protection without erythropoiesis.

ARA-290 was designed to fit that receptor. It is an 11-residue peptide derived from the helix B surface of EPO, the region implicated in tissue protection rather than erythropoiesis. The peptide is small, soluble, and does not contain the glycosylation sites that give EPO its long half-life; it is a research tool, not a hormone replacement.

The preclinical literature clusters around three areas. The first is peripheral neuropathy. Swartjes and colleagues reported that ARA-290 produced long-term relief of neuropathic pain in rats, and that the effect was lost in βcR knockout mice — evidence that the receptor complex, rather than a nonspecific peptide effect, was responsible. Pulman's group later showed reversal of mechanical allodynia in a neuritis model, extending the finding to an inflammatory nerve injury.

The second is tissue protection outside the nervous system. Patel and colleagues tested a pyroglutamate helix B surface peptide in acute kidney injury and reported attenuation of injury in rats. The result is consistent with IRR expression in renal tubules and with the broader hypothesis that the receptor is a general stress-response pathway rather than a neuron-specific one.

The third is immune modulation. Nairz's group showed that cibinetide dampened innate immune cell functions in experimental colitis, reducing inflammatory cytokine production. Yao and colleagues tested cibinetide in isolated human islets under stress and reported improved engraftment in a transplantation model — an in vitro and ex vivo line of work that is mechanistically separate from the neuropathy studies.

What ties these together is the receptor, not the disease. ARA-290 is a probe for IRR signalling, and the endpoints that matter are receptor-dependent: phospho-STAT3, phospho-Akt, NF-κB translocation, and cytokine release. A study that measures only a behavioural or histological outcome cannot tell whether the peptide acted through the IRR or through something else.

## What the Batch Record Should Show

Identity for an 11-residue peptide is straightforward in principle and often sloppy in practice. MS/MS should cover the full sequence, with the N-terminal histidine and the C-terminal glutamine both accounted for. The stated sequence on this page is a modified 11-mer, and the certificate should state the modification explicitly; "ARA-290" alone does not define the molecule.

Mass accuracy matters at this size. The expected mass is near 1096 Da, and a nominal-mass check will not distinguish the parent peptide from a deamidated or oxidized variant. Ask for the observed monoisotopic mass and the calculated mass, with the difference stated in ppm.

Purity by HPLC at 214 nm should come with a method and a chromatogram. The impurity table should list individual peaks, including any that correspond to deletion sequences. For a peptide this short, single-residue deletions are common and can be resolved with a shallow gradient.

Counterion and residual TFA belong on the record. The peptide is cationic and retains TFA from purification; the certificate should state whether an acetate exchange was performed and what the residual TFA level is. Water content by Karl Fischer and net peptide content per vial complete the quantitative fields.

Endotoxin is a specific concern for this compound because the biological readouts are inflammatory. A preparation contaminated with endotoxin will suppress or amplify cytokine signals depending on the assay, and the researcher may attribute the effect to the peptide. Ask for EU/mg and reject anything above 5 EU/mg for cell work.

## Sourcing & Verification

ARA-290 is made by solid-phase synthesis, and the sequence is short enough that many facilities can produce it. The variation is in purification and documentation, not in feasibility.

The first question is whether the supplier distinguishes ARA-290 from pHBSP. The two names refer to related helix B surface peptides with different N-terminal modifications, and they are sometimes used interchangeably in the grey literature. They are not the same molecule, and a certificate that uses both names without specifying the structure has not identified the product.

The second question is sequence confirmation. Ask for the MS/MS spectrum, not just the intact mass. A supplier who can only provide a mass may have bought the peptide from a trading house and never characterised it.

The third is the endotoxin result. This is a peptide used in immune assays; a contaminated lot is worse than a low-purity lot, because the contamination produces a biologically plausible false positive.

Independent verification on arrival should include LC-MS identity, HPLC purity, and endotoxin. A small aliquot is enough. If the peptide is intended for in vivo work, add a sterility check and confirm the counterion, since TFA can produce local irritation that confounds a behavioural endpoint.

## Handling & Storage

Store lyophilized ARA-290 at −20°C, protected from light. The peptide is hygroscopic; let the vial equilibrate before opening.

Reconstitute in sterile water or a neutral buffer. The sequence is hydrophilic and dissolves readily; if the solution is cloudy, the likely cause is a collapsed cake or a counterion effect rather than insolubility. Centrifuge briefly and check the supernatant before discarding the preparation.

Working solutions can be held at 4°C for up to two weeks according to the catalog specification, which is longer than most peptides. That stability is useful for a study that runs across several days, but it assumes a sterile solution and a neutral pH. If the buffer contains a reducing agent or a chelator, expect a shorter working life.

Adsorption is the practical loss mechanism for a small cationic peptide. Use low-binding tubes for dilute solutions, and avoid transferring through multiple containers. If an assay is quantitative at low nanomolar concentrations, a pre-rinse of the tube with a dilute peptide solution can reduce the loss.

A study that uses haematocrit as a control for ARA-290 is checking the wrong thing, and a study that assumes the two are interchangeable is not testing the IRR hypothesis.

Against pHBSP, the difference is the N-terminal modification, which affects stability and receptor binding. The published literature sometimes treats them as a family; for purchasing, they are separate products with separate certificates.

## The Author's Take

My position is that ARA-290 is a receptor-pharmacology tool and should be bought like one: sequence first, endotoxin second, purity third. The intact mass is the least informative field, because several related peptides share a similar mass and only fragmentation sequencing separates them.

I also think the clinical literature around cibinetide should be read separately from the preclinical work. The animal and cell studies support a mechanism; they do not establish a clinical effect, and a supplier who markets the compound on clinical associations is overselling it.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is ARA-290 the same as erythropoietin?**

No. It is an 11-residue peptide derived from the helix B surface of EPO and acts through the innate repair receptor, a heterodimer of EPOR and βcR. It lacks the erythropoietic activity of the full hormone.

**What in vitro assay formats are used for ARA-290?**

IRR activation is typically measured by phospho-STAT3 or phospho-Akt immunoblot, NF-κB reporter assays, and cytokine release from LPS-stimulated macrophages. Receptor dependence should be confirmed with a βcR-blocking antibody or βcR-deficient cells.

**Is ARA-290 available for wholesale research purchase?**

Yes, in 10 mg vials at ≥98% HPLC purity, with larger quantities on request. Wholesale orders should specify the sequence, counterion, and endotoxin limit, since those fields are not standardized across suppliers.

**What purity and form is ARA-290 supplied in?**

Lyophilized powder, ≥98% by HPLC, identity confirmed by LC-MS. The batch record should add the MS/MS spectrum, the observed and calculated masses, and the endotoxin value.

**How should reconstituted ARA-290 be stored?**

At 4°C for up to two weeks in a sterile, neutral buffer, or aliquoted at −20°C for longer storage. Avoid repeated freeze-thaw cycles and use low-binding tubes for dilute solutions.

**Why is endotoxin testing important for this peptide?**

Because the main readouts are inflammatory. A contaminated preparation can change cytokine release or immune cell behaviour and produce a false positive that looks like an IRR effect. Ask for EU/mg, not a generic sterility statement.

**Can ARA-290 be used in animal studies?**

It is supplied for laboratory research use only. Animal work requires institutional approval, a defined formulation, and independent verification of identity, purity, and endotoxin before use.
