## Why This Compound

Growth hormone does two things that are hard to separate: it promotes growth through IGF-1, and it mobilises fat. The C-terminal region of the hormone carries much of the lipolytic activity, and a synthetic fragment of residues 176–191 retains that activity in rodent models without the growth-promoting effects of the full molecule. That separation is the entire research rationale for AOD-9604.

The fragment used in most studies is not a plain 176–191 sequence. It carries an additional tyrosine at the N-terminus, making it a 16-residue peptide with a tyrosine leader, and it has a disulfide bond between the two cysteines at positions 7 and 15. The disulfide is not decorative: it constrains the peptide into a loop that presents the hydrophobic face thought to interact with the receptor. Reduce it, and the peptide changes shape.

Ng and colleagues characterised the metabolic activity of the fragment in the late 1990s and early 2000s. The 2000 paper in Hormone Research described a synthetic lipolytic domain that reduced body weight gain in obese rodents, and a companion study in Zucker fatty rats examined the molecular actions of the same domain. Heffernan's group then tested the fragment in obese mice and in β3-adrenergic receptor knockout mice, finding that the lipid effects depended in part on the β3 receptor — evidence for a mechanism distinct from the IGF-1 axis.

That β3 dependence is the most interesting and least settled part of the story. If AOD-9604 acted only through β3 receptors, its effects should disappear in knockout animals. The published data show a partial dependence rather than an absolute one, which suggests either a second receptor or a downstream interaction. Later reviews of β3-adrenergic physiology place the receptor in brown and white adipose tissue, with effects on lipolysis and energy expenditure that vary by species.

For a laboratory, the practical takeaway is that AOD-9604 is a tool for studying fragment biology, not a validated mechanism. Experiments should measure lipolysis directly — glycerol or free fatty acid release from adipocytes — rather than inferring it from body weight. Body weight integrates food intake, water balance, and lean mass, and a fragment that changes any of those can look like a lipolytic agent.

## What the Batch Record Should Show

Sequence identity is the first requirement, and for AOD-9604 it needs MS/MS coverage of the full 16 residues. A single intact mass near 1815 Da is compatible with several sequences, including the 15-residue fragment without the tyrosine leader. The fragmentation spectrum should show the b and y ion series across the length of the peptide, with the N-terminal tyrosine and the C-terminal phenylalanine both accounted for.

Disulfide status is the second requirement, and it is the one most often missing. The certificate should state whether the peptide is the oxidised (cyclic) form, the reduced dithiol, or a mixture. The mass difference is 2 Da, which is easy to miss in a low-resolution measurement but obvious in a high-resolution spectrum. A peptide supplied as the reduced form will oxidise slowly in solution and behave inconsistently; a peptide supplied as a mixture is not a defined reagent.

Purity by HPLC should be run at 214 nm with a method that resolves the main peak from deletion and truncation variants. AOD-9604 is small enough that single-residue deletions differ by 100–130 Da and can elute close to the parent; a shallow gradient is worth the extra run time. The impurity table should list individual peaks.

Counterion and residual TFA belong on the record. This peptide is cationic (arginine at position 8, histidine absent but lysine absent too), and it retains TFA from purification. Acetate exchange is common for research-grade material; the certificate should say which form was shipped and report residual TFA if the exchange was incomplete.

Water content, appearance, and fill mass complete the document. Endotoxin matters for cell work; a lipolysis assay using primary adipocytes is sensitive to inflammatory contamination, and an endotoxin result above 5 EU/mg is a reason to re-purify.

## Sourcing & Verification

AOD-9604 is a high-volume item, and the market reflects that. Real synthesis at 10 mg scale is inexpensive, so the price floor is low; the temptation to cut corners is correspondingly high.

Three failure modes are common. The first is the wrong fragment: 176–191 without the tyrosine leader, or a shorter C-terminal piece, sold under the AOD name. The masses differ enough that a high-resolution MS check catches it, but a nominal-mass check may not. The second is a broken disulfide: linear peptide is easier to make and purify, and it can be sold as the cyclic form if nobody measures. The third is underfill: a vial labelled 5 mg containing 3 mg of peptide and 2 mg of mannitol.

Ask for the MS/MS spectrum and the high-resolution mass, not a nominal figure. Ask for the disulfide status in writing. Ask for the net peptide content per vial, not the fill weight. A supplier who can answer all three is manufacturing or has a manufacturer's data; one who cannot is reselling.

Independent verification is cheap relative to the experiment. A 1 mg aliquot sent for LC-MS/MS and a second aliquot for amino acid analysis will confirm sequence and composition. If the peptide is intended for cell work, add an endotoxin assay. ## Handling & Storage

Store lyophilized AOD-9604 at −20°C, protected from light. The powder is stable for long periods if kept dry; the solution is not.

Reconstitute in sterile water or a neutral buffer. The peptide dissolves readily; if it does not, the vial may have absorbed moisture and the cake may have collapsed. Avoid vortexing, which foams the solution and can promote aggregation at the air–water interface.

The disulfide is sensitive to reducing agents. Dithiothreitol, β-mercaptoethanol, and high concentrations of glutathione will open the loop and change the peptide's conformation. Keep reducing agents out of the buffer unless the experiment specifically requires the reduced form, and note that cell culture medium contains cysteine and glutathione that can slowly reduce exposed disulfides.

Prepare working solutions fresh. AOD-9604 is small enough to pass through 0.22 µm filters without loss, but it adsorbs to some membranes; PVDF is a safer choice than cellulose acetate. For long-term storage of solutions, aliquot and freeze at −80°C rather than holding at 4°C.

The comparison to full hGH is the one that matters conceptually. The fragment was designed to keep the lipolytic activity and lose the growth-promoting and insulin-antagonising effects. If a study reports IGF-1 elevation after AOD-9604, the preparation may be contaminated with full hormone or the result may reflect an indirect effect; either way, the finding needs checking before it is attributed to the fragment.

Against tesamorelin, the difference is the upstream axis. Tesamorelin stimulates endogenous GH release through the GHRH receptor; AOD-9604 acts downstream as a fragment. They are not substitutes, and a study that swaps one for the other is testing a different pathway.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How does AOD-9604 differ from HGH Fragment 176-191?**

AOD-9604 carries an additional N-terminal tyrosine, making it a 16-residue peptide rather than the 15-residue 176–191 fragment. The extension changes the mass, the solubility, and the handling; the two are not interchangeable in an assay.

**Does AOD-9604 affect IGF-1 levels in research models?**

Preclinical work suggests the fragment does not raise IGF-1 to the extent full growth hormone does, which is central to its research rationale. Results vary by model and preparation, and a preparation contaminated with full hormone would confound the comparison.

**What purity is your AOD-9604?**

The catalog specification is ≥98% by HPLC, with identity confirmed by mass spectrometry. The batch record should add the MS/MS sequence coverage, the disulfide status, and the counterion.

**What are the storage requirements?**

Store the lyophilized powder at −20°C, protected from light and moisture. Reconstitute in sterile water or neutral buffer, aliquot, and freeze at −80°C for longer storage. Avoid reducing agents that would open the disulfide.

**Why does the disulfide bond matter for this peptide?**

The Cys7–Cys15 disulfide constrains the peptide into a loop that presents its hydrophobic face. The reduced form has a different conformation and behaves differently in binding and cell assays, so the oxidation state must be specified.

**Can I filter-sterilize AOD-9604 solutions?**

Yes, through a 0.22 µm PVDF or PES membrane. Avoid cellulose acetate, which can adsorb the peptide, and pre-rinse the filter with buffer to reduce losses on small volumes.

**Is AOD-9604 supplied with a certificate of analysis?**

The manufacturer's batch specification accompanies the order. For this peptide, ask for lot-specific MS/MS, HPLC, disulfide status, and net peptide content rather than a generic certificate.
