## Why This Compound

AMP-activated protein kinase is the cell's energy gauge. When ATP falls and AMP rises, AMP binds the γ subunit, allosteric activation follows, and the kinase switches the cell toward catabolism: fatty acid oxidation, glucose uptake, mitochondrial biogenesis, and inhibition of anabolic processes that consume ATP. The 2012 and 2018 reviews by Hardie and by Herzig and Shaw remain the clearest accounts of how that switch is wired.

AICAR activates AMPK indirectly. The compound enters cells through nucleoside transporters, is phosphorylated by adenosine kinase to ZMP, and ZMP mimics AMP at the γ subunit. The result is AMPK activation without a change in the cellular adenine nucleotide pool — a pharmacological imitation of energy deficit. Corton and colleagues established this mechanism in 1995, and it is still the reason AICAR is used as a "chemical exercise" tool.

The downstream phenotypes are well documented in rodents. Winder's group showed that chronic AICAR treatment increased mitochondrial enzymes in skeletal muscle, and later work connected the effect to PGC-1α and to changes in fibre-type markers. The compound also increases GLUT4 expression and glucose uptake in muscle, and it has been studied in cardiac ischaemia-reperfusion models and in cancer cell lines, where AMPK activation interacts with mTOR signalling.

Two caveats deserve more attention than they usually get. First, ZMP is not AMP: it activates AMPK but also inhibits other enzymes that recognise the nucleotide, including adenosine kinase and IMP dehydrogenase. Effects attributed to AMPK may therefore be partly off-target. Second, AICAR is a nucleoside, and cells vary in how well they take it up; a concentration that works in hepatocytes may do little in a cell line with low transporter expression. Reviewers increasingly ask for a genetic control — AMPK knockdown or a kinase-dead mutant — before a phenotype is assigned to AMPK.

For buyers, the practical implication is that AICAR is a tool for mechanism, not a compound whose effects can be assumed. The reagent has to be clean enough that the mechanism is the only variable.

One more design point is worth carrying into procurement. Exercise-physiology studies often use AICAR as a stand-in for training, comparing treated sedentary animals with exercised controls. That comparison only works if the compound's purity and the amount delivered are consistent across the study, because the exercised arm cannot be adjusted after the fact. A batch that is 8% underfilled turns a planned comparison into a confounded one, and the shortfall will not be visible in the phenotype until the data are analysed.

## What the Batch Record Should Show

Identity for a nucleoside is best established by NMR, not by mass alone. The ¹H spectrum in DMSO-d₆ should show the ribose anomeric proton near 5.9 ppm, the sugar hydroxyls, the aromatic proton on the imidazole ring, and the amide and amine signals. A spectrum that lacks the anomeric proton is not AICAR, whatever the mass says.

Mass spectrometry should confirm the expected molecular ion at m/z 339 [M+H]⁺ and the fragmentation pattern of the riboside. LC-MS in positive mode is standard; a run in negative mode adds confidence for the carboxamide.

Purity by HPLC is usually measured at 260 nm, where the purine-like chromophore absorbs. The method should be stated, and the chromatogram should show the main peak plus any nucleoside impurities. This is where dilution shows up: inosine, adenosine, and ribose are cheap and UV-active, and a lot cut with inosine will show an extra peak that a careless integrator can round into "98%".

Water content by Karl Fischer matters because the powder is hygroscopic and the molecular weight includes no water. A lot that reads 6% water is 6% less AICAR than the label implies. Residual solvents from synthesis — methanol, ethanol, acetonitrile — should be below ICH limits, and heavy metals should be reported if the route uses catalytic hydrogenation.

Appearance should be a white to off-white crystalline powder. A yellow or brown tint suggests oxidation or residual imidazole, and it is worth a query even if the HPLC passes.

Endotoxin applies for cell-culture use. AICAR itself is not cytotoxic at moderate concentrations, but a contaminated preparation will produce inflammatory effects that mimic metabolic stress. Ask for EU/mg, not a pass/fail sterility statement.

## Sourcing & Verification

AICAR is manufactured by chemical synthesis from a ribose starting material, and the process is well established in China. That means supply is plentiful and quality is variable. The common failure modes are dilution, isomer contamination, and residual solvent.

Dilution is the most common. Because AICAR is a white powder with a slightly bitter taste and no distinctive appearance, it can be cut with mannitol, glucose, or inosine without changing the look. An NMR spectrum catches all three: mannitol gives a distinctive multiplet pattern, glucose gives a different anomeric signal, and inosine lacks the aminoimidazole protons.

Isomer contamination is subtler. The β-anomer is the active form; a synthesis that lacks stereochemical control can produce a mixture with the α-anomer, which has different transporter affinity. HPLC may not resolve the two, but NMR coupling constants will.

Residual solvent is a regulatory issue rather than a biological one at research scale, but it matters for cell work. A lot with 3000 ppm methanol will affect a sensitive assay.

Ask for the NMR spectrum and the HPLC trace for the lot, not a representative batch. If the supplier cannot provide an NMR, the material is probably bought from a trading house and repackaged. A small aliquot sent for independent NMR and LC-MS is cheap insurance; 20 mg is enough for both.

## Handling & Storage

Store the powder at −20°C for long-term holding, with short-term bench work at 2–8°C. Protect it from moisture; the powder is hygroscopic and will cake if left open in a humid room.

AICAR dissolves in water to a modest extent and more readily in DMSO. For cell work, prepare a concentrated DMSO stock, filter it, and dilute into medium. Check the final DMSO concentration against the vehicle control; a 0.5% difference between treated and control wells can shift the result in sensitive assays.

Aqueous solutions are stable for days at 4°C, not weeks. The ribose can hydrolyse slowly at acidic pH, and the compound is susceptible to microbial growth in plain water. Prepare working dilutions fresh, and keep them protected from light.

For animal work, the compound is typically formulated in saline or a buffered vehicle. AICAR is not very soluble at neutral pH; if a protocol calls for a high concentration, check for precipitate before use and choose a vehicle that keeps the compound in solution. This is a formulation detail, not a protocol recommendation.

The comparison to metformin is misleading in one important way. Metformin activates AMPK indirectly by inhibiting mitochondrial respiration, so its effects depend on the metabolic state of the cell and take hours to develop. AICAR's ZMP route is faster and more direct, which makes it better for acute mechanistic experiments and worse for modelling a chronic drug exposure.

Against the direct activators, AICAR's advantage is its literature. A-769662 and compound 991 are more selective, but fewer labs have used them, and the phenotype data are thinner. A study that needs a well-trodden path uses AICAR; a study that needs to isolate AMPK from upstream signals uses a direct activator.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How does AICAR differ from metformin as an AMPK activator?**

Metformin inhibits mitochondrial complex I and activates AMPK indirectly through a rise in AMP, a process that takes time and depends on the cell's metabolic state. AICAR is phosphorylated to ZMP, which mimics AMP at the AMPK γ subunit, producing faster activation. They are different tools for different experimental questions.

**What purity is your AICAR?**

The catalog specification is ≥98% by HPLC, with identity confirmed by mass spectrometry. The batch record should include the NMR spectrum, the HPLC method and chromatogram, water content, and residual solvent data.

**What are the storage conditions?**

Store the lyophilized powder at −20°C, protected from moisture and light. Aqueous solutions can be held at 2–8°C for a few days; prepare working dilutions fresh and avoid repeated freeze-thaw of stocks.

**How can I tell whether my AICAR has been diluted?**

Run ¹H NMR. Mannitol, glucose, and inosine each produce signals that AICAR does not, and the integration ratio gives an estimate of purity. HPLC at 260 nm can miss a non-UV-active diluent, which is why NMR is the better check.

**Is AICAR cell-permeable?**

Yes, it enters cells through nucleoside transporters and is phosphorylated intracellularly. Uptake varies between cell types, so a concentration that works in one line may be insufficient in another with lower transporter expression.

**Does AICAR activate AMPK in all tissues?**

No. Skeletal muscle, liver, and adipose tissue respond well in rodent studies; some cell lines respond poorly. Include a positive control such as a direct AMPK activator, and confirm activation by phospho-ACC or phospho-AMPK immunoblot.

**Can I use AICAR in animals?**

It is supplied for laboratory research use only. Any animal work requires institutional approval, a defined formulation, and independent verification of the compound's identity and purity before use.
