Adipotide is a compound with an unusually direct design story: find a peptide that homes to the blood vessels feeding white fat, attach a sequence that kills the cells it lands on, and watch what happens to the tissue. The first half of that story is well documented. The second half is where the research gets difficult, because a peptide that destroys adipose vasculature is not a subtle tool, and the published animal work shows both the intended effect and a clear off-target signal.

This page covers what the molecule is, what a batch record for it should contain, and why the difference between CKGGRAKDC-GG-D(KLAKLAK)₂ and a generic "adipotide" listing matters more than the purity number on the label.

The architecture is a CKGGRAKDC targeting domain joined through a GG spacer to a D(KLAKLAK)₂ killing domain; the targeting sequence binds prohibitin on white adipose vascular endothelium, and the killing sequence disrupts mitochondrial membranes in the cells it reaches.

## Why This Compound

The targeting domain came out of phage display. Kolonin and colleagues screened peptide libraries against the vasculature of white adipose tissue and identified CKGGRAKDC as a sequence that homed there preferentially. The receptor turned out to be prohibitin, a protein better known as a mitochondrial chaperone but also expressed on the surface of adipose endothelial cells. The 2004 Nature Medicine paper showed that coupling this homing sequence to a proapoptotic peptide ablated white adipose vasculature in mice and reduced fat mass.

The killing domain is D(KLAKLAK)₂, an amphipathic peptide written in D-amino acids so that proteases cannot degrade it quickly. On its own, KLAKLAK disrupts mitochondrial membranes, but poorly and without selectivity — it needs a targeting sequence to concentrate it at the right cells. The D configuration also reduces immunogenicity and extends the peptide's half-life in circulation, which is why the same motif appears in several targeted cytotoxic peptides.

The proposed mechanism runs from endothelial apoptosis to tissue ischaemia. Kill the cells lining the adipose capillaries, and the adipocytes they supply lose oxygen and nutrients. Over days, the fat pad shrinks. Barnhart and colleagues tested the construct in obese rhesus macaques and reported reductions in body weight and improved insulin sensitivity, with the effect concentrated in visceral depots. That study is the strongest preclinical evidence for the approach and also the source of its most important caveat: renal tubular effects appeared alongside the intended adipose effect.

That caveat is not a footnote. Prohibitin is expressed in kidney tissue, and a prohibitin-targeting peptide will find it. The monkey work documented renal tubular dilation and degeneration at effective doses, which is exactly the kind of finding that should shape how a laboratory designs a follow-up study. A researcher using adipotide as a tool needs a kidney endpoint in the protocol, not just a body-composition endpoint.

For all that, adipotide remains a useful probe for adipose vascular biology. It answers questions that genetic models answer slowly: what happens to a fat pad when its blood supply is removed, how quickly do adipocytes die, and which depots recover. The peptide's selectivity is imperfect, but its kinetics are fast and its effect is local enough to study.

## What the Batch Record Should Show

This is a synthetic peptide of roughly 2.5 kDa with two functionally distinct domains, and the analytical record should demonstrate both. Start with the sequence: CKGGRAKDC, a GG spacer, then D(KLAKLAK)₂. The D-amino acids are the detail most likely to be wrong in a cheap batch, because a manufacturer working from the all-L sequence will produce a peptide with the same nominal mass that digests in serum within minutes.

Mass spectrometry should confirm the intact mass and the fragmentation pattern. MS/MS coverage of both domains is the goal; a spectrum that only covers the cationic KLAK region leaves the targeting sequence unverified. Because the molecule contains a cysteine, the certificate should state whether the peptide is supplied as the free thiol, the disulfide-linked dimer, or a capped derivative. Each form has a different mass and different biological behaviour, and "adipotide" alone does not specify which one is in the vial.

Purity by HPLC should be reported with the method and the chromatogram. The amphipathic character of the killing domain makes this peptide prone to forming aggregates and to sticking to column matrices; a method that resolves the main peak from the D-amino acid deletion variants is more informative than a single percentage. Impurity peaks corresponding to truncated KLAK sequences should be listed individually.

Counterion and residual TFA belong on the record. Cationic peptides purified on TFA gradients retain trifluoroacetate, and the amount affects both the mass balance and the tolerability of the preparation in cell culture. Acetate exchange reduces the TFA content but adds a step the supplier may skip. Water content by Karl Fischer, appearance, and fill mass complete the basic document.

Endotoxin deserves particular attention. A peptide that kills endothelial cells will produce a cytotoxic effect whether or not it is contaminated, but endotoxin will confound the interpretation: a preparation with 50 EU/mg can shrink a fat pad through inflammation rather than through prohibitin targeting. Ask for endotoxin in EU/mg, and treat a result above 5 EU/mg as a reason to re-purify rather than a number to note.

## Sourcing & Verification

Adipotide is a made-to-order peptide for most suppliers, and the synthesis is not trivial. The D-amino acid segment requires a different coupling strategy and a different purification gradient from the targeting domain, and the cysteine adds a decision about oxidation state. A facility that has not made it before will learn on the buyer's batch.

Ask three questions before ordering. First, has the facility synthesized this exact sequence before, and can it provide a previous lot's MS/MS spectrum? Second, what oxidation state is being supplied, and is the cysteine free, capped, or dimerized? Third, what is the endotoxin specification, and is it measured per milligram or per vial? The answers separate a specialist from a generalist.

A fourth question concerns scale. The catalog lists 2 mg and 5 mg vials, which are research-scale quantities. If a supplier quotes a price for 100 mg that is only marginally higher per milligram, the batch is probably being made at a scale where purification is compressed, and the impurity profile will show it.

Independent verification on arrival is worthwhile. A 1 mg aliquot sent for LC-MS and endotoxin testing costs less than repeating a failed animal study. For a peptide with a known renal signal, a contaminated batch can produce exactly the toxicity the literature warns about, and the researcher will attribute it to the compound rather than to the vial.

Shipping is straightforward for a lyophilized peptide, but the customs declaration should name the product accurately. A parcel described as a generic "peptide sample" can be held, and a held parcel that sits in a warm depot for two weeks is a stability question. ## Handling & Storage

Store the lyophilized powder at −20°C, protected from light. The peptide is hygroscopic, and a vial opened cold will absorb water; equilibrate it in a sealed bag before weighing or reconstituting.

Reconstitute in sterile water or buffer at neutral pH. The cationic killing domain makes the peptide soluble, but it also makes it prone to binding to glass and to anionic surfaces. Use low-binding tubes, and avoid heparin-coated or highly charged filter membranes. A 0.22 µm PVDF filter is a reasonable choice; a cellulose acetate membrane may adsorb a significant fraction of the peptide.

Working solutions should be prepared fresh. The D-amino acids resist proteolysis, but the peptide still adsorbs, aggregates, and oxidizes at the cysteine. If a protocol requires a stock, prepare it at higher concentration in water, aliquot, and freeze at −80°C; dilute on the day of use.

Handling precautions are those of a cytotoxic research peptide. Gloves, eye protection, and a lab coat are appropriate; weigh the powder in a fume hood or a closed balance enclosure to avoid inhaling fines. Dispose of waste as chemical waste according to institutional rules, not down the sink.

The comparison to unconjugated KLAKLAK is the one that defines the compound. Without the CKGGRAKDC domain, the killing peptide has no reason to concentrate in fat, and any effect it produces is a toxicity result rather than a targeting result. A study that reports adipose effects from KLAKLAK alone has not reproduced adipotide.

Against AOD-9604, the difference is the level of the system being perturbed. AOD-9604 acts on adipocyte metabolism; adipotide acts on the vasculature that supports the adipocyte. The two produce different histology and different time courses, and comparing them in one study is informative only if both endpoints are measured.

## The Author's Take

My position is that adipotide should be sold with the renal finding attached to it. The primate study is part of the compound's identity, not an inconvenient detail, and a buyer who does not know about it will design a study that cannot distinguish target effect from off-target toxicity.

I also think the cysteine oxidation state is the most under-documented field in grey-market listings. Free thiol, disulfide dimer, and capped peptide are three different reagents, and a certificate that does not say which one was shipped has not described the product.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is adipotide's mechanism of action?**

It is a bifunctional peptide: CKGGRAKDC binds prohibitin on white adipose vascular endothelium, and the D(KLAKLAK)₂ domain disrupts mitochondrial membranes in the cells it reaches. The downstream effect is endothelial apoptosis followed by adipose tissue ischaemia.

**What purity is your adipotide?**

The catalog specification is ≥98% by HPLC, with identity confirmed by mass spectrometry. The batch record should include the chromatogram, the mass spectrum, the cysteine oxidation state, and an endotoxin value.

**What are the storage conditions?**

Store lyophilized powder at −20°C, protected from light and moisture. Once dissolved, split the solution into single-use aliquots and hold them at −80°C; a thawed aliquot should not go back into the freezer.

**Why does the cysteine matter?**

The peptide contains one cysteine, and its oxidation state changes the mass and the behaviour of the molecule. A free thiol, a disulfide-linked dimer, and a capped cysteine are different products, and the certificate should state which one the lot contains.

**Is adipotide selective for adipose tissue?**

Not completely. Prohibitin is expressed in other tissues, including kidney, and the primate study reported renal tubular effects alongside the intended adipose effect. Any protocol should include a kidney endpoint and a control for off-target cytotoxicity.

**Can I use adipotide in cell culture?**

Yes, with care. The peptide is cytotoxic by design, so concentrations and exposure times need to be established for each cell type, and a KLAKLAK-only control helps separate targeting from nonspecific membrane damage.

**Does the catalog provide a certificate of analysis?**

The manufacturer's batch specification accompanies the order. For a peptide with this mechanism, request the lot-specific MS/MS, HPLC, and endotoxin data before use rather than relying on a generic certificate.
