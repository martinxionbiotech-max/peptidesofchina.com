FOXO4-DRI is a D-retro-inverso peptide built to interfere with the FOXO4–p53 interaction in senescent cells. It is 18 residues long, carries D-amino acids throughout, and has an approximate mass of 2,448 Da. The catalogue supplies it as a lyophilized powder in 10 mg vials, ten to a pack, with a ≥98% HPLC specification.

The chemistry is the interesting part. A retro-inverso peptide is not simply a peptide made of D-amino acids; it is a sequence whose direction and stereochemistry are both inverted so that the side chains approximate the spatial arrangement of the parent L-peptide. The result is a molecule that proteases do not recognise and that can survive in biological media long enough to reach an intracellular target.

That design also creates an identity problem. The very features that make the peptide stable — D-residues and a reversed backbone — are the features that standard sequencing methods cannot resolve. A certificate for FOXO4-DRI has to do more than report a mass.

## Why This Compound

Cellular senescence is a state in which a cell stops dividing but does not die. Senescent cells accumulate with age and after genotoxic stress, and they secrete a mixture of cytokines, proteases, and growth factors known as the senescence-associated secretory phenotype. That secretion is thought to contribute to tissue dysfunction, which is why senolytics — compounds that selectively eliminate senescent cells — became a research category.

FOXO4-DRI comes from a specific observation about how senescent cells avoid apoptosis. In senescent cells, the transcription factor FOXO4 is reported to bind p53 and retain it in the nucleus. p53 is normally a pro-apoptotic signal; sequestering it allows the senescent cell to persist. The peptide was designed to compete with that interaction, releasing p53 and allowing the cell to enter apoptosis. The anchor paper is Baar and colleagues in *Cell* (2017), which reported that the peptide selectively induced apoptosis in senescent cells and improved several tissue endpoints in aged or chemotherapy-treated mice.

That is a strong preclinical story, and it is also a narrow one. The work is concentrated in a small number of laboratories, the endpoints are model-specific, and the translation to human tissue is unresolved. A 2022 review of the senolytic field described the clinical path as early and mostly focused on other compounds. None of that makes FOXO4-DRI uninteresting; it makes it a tool for a defined question, not a general-purpose "longevity" reagent.

The catalogue files the compound under Anti-Aging & Longevity. That is a shelf label, not a claim. The research question the peptide addresses is whether disrupting a specific protein–protein interaction changes the survival of senescent cells, and the useful evidence is the kind that can be measured: apoptosis markers, senescent-cell burden, and tissue histology.

## What the Batch Record Should Show

FOXO4-DRI is one of the harder peptides in the catalogue to characterise, and the certificate should reflect that.

**Identity by mass spectrometry.** A deconvoluted mass with the observed and calculated values. For a peptide of this size, the charge-state envelope should be visible, and the deconvoluted mass should agree with the stated sequence to within the instrument's calibration error. The [molecular-weight verification note](/blog/peptide-molecular-weight-verification-steps/) explains how to check that the envelope is consistent with the expected number of charges.

**Sequence confirmation.** This is the difficult item. Edman degradation is designed for L-amino acids and does not read a D-retro-inverso backbone in the usual way. Tandem mass spectrometry can fragment the backbone and give a sequence, but it cannot distinguish D from L residues, because the fragments are isobaric. A supplier who claims "sequence confirmed by MS/MS" has confirmed the order of residues, not their stereochemistry. Establishing the D-configuration requires chiral amino acid analysis after hydrolysis, or a comparison with a characterised reference standard. Ask which one was done.

**Purity by HPLC with method detail.** Column, mobile phase, gradient, wavelength, and integration. D-retro-inverso peptides are often hydrophobic and elute late on a C18 column; a gradient that was designed for a small hydrophilic peptide may not resolve the main peak from closely related diastereomers. The [CoA reading guide](/blog/how-to-read-a-peptide-coa-a-researchers-guide-to-hplc-and-ms-reports/) walks through the fields that matter.

**Counterion and residual TFA.** Peptidomimetics are commonly supplied as acetate or trifluoroacetate salts. The counterion changes the mass of the vial contents and can affect cell penetration and assay performance. The certificate should name it and report residual TFA where relevant. The [counterion note](/blog/role-of-counterions-in-peptide-stability/) explains why this is not a housekeeping detail.

**Peptide content and water content.** A 10 mg fill is not 10 mg of peptide. Karl Fischer titration and nitrogen or amino acid analysis convert the fill into a molar amount. For a peptide used at low micromolar concentrations in cell culture, the difference between 8 mg and 10 mg of peptide content is a 20% error in every calculation downstream.

**Aggregation and appearance.** DRI peptides can aggregate, and the lyophilised cake should be inspected for shrinkage, melting, or discoloration. Where available, size-exclusion chromatography or dynamic light scattering adds useful information.

**Endotoxin and bioburden.** For any cell-based senescence assay, endotoxin is a relevant specification. A chemical-purity certificate does not cover it. Request EU/mg and a bioburden count.

**Batch linkage.** The certificate must match the vial label, and it should carry a manufacture date and a retest date. A document that could describe any FOXO4-DRI lot is a template.

## Handling & Storage

Store the lyophilized powder at −20°C, protected from moisture and light. Equilibrate the vial to room temperature before opening; a cold vial condenses water, and the powder is hygroscopic.

Reconstitute in sterile water, phosphate-buffered saline, or the assay medium. DRI peptides are often poorly soluble in pure water at high concentration; if dissolution is slow, try a small amount of DMSO as a pre-dissolution step and then dilute into aqueous buffer, keeping the final DMSO concentration within the assay's tolerance. Add solvent down the wall and swirl; do not vortex.

Expect adsorption. Peptidomimetics with hydrophobic side chains bind to plastic, and the loss is proportionally larger at low concentrations. Use low-binding tubes, avoid transferring through multiple containers, and check recovery if you filter. A 0.22 µm membrane may retain a meaningful fraction of a dilute solution, and the effect depends on the membrane polymer.

Aliquot before freezing. Aqueous stocks at 2–8°C are short-term working solutions; repeated freeze-thaw cycles promote aggregation. Freeze single-use portions at −80°C and do not refreeze. Protect from light.

For cell-based work, prepare the dilution immediately before treatment and include a vehicle control at the same final solvent concentration. DRI peptides can be taken up by cells, but the extent depends on concentration, time, and the cell type; a "cell-penetrating" label is not a guarantee for every system.

All of the above is bench-scale work. The peptide is sold for laboratory research and is not intended for administration to humans or animals. ## Frequently Asked Questions

**What does D-retro-inverso mean?**

It means the sequence is reversed and built from D-amino acids rather than L-amino acids. The inversion is designed to preserve the spatial arrangement of side chains while making the backbone unrecognisable to proteases. The result is a peptidomimetic with greater stability in biological media than the parent L-peptide.

**Is the sequence available?**

The catalogue lists the sequence as available on request. For a research buyer, the batch certificate should at least state the residue count and the measured mass, and a confidentiality agreement can usually cover the full sequence.

**Can mass spectrometry confirm the D-amino acids?**

No. Tandem mass spectrometry can confirm the order of residues, but D- and L-residues are isobaric and fragment the same way. Establishing stereochemistry needs chiral amino acid analysis after hydrolysis or comparison with a characterised reference standard.

**What purity should the batch meet?**

The catalog standard is ≥98% by HPLC. Ask how the method resolves diastereomers and deletion sequences; a high area percentage from a method that cannot separate them is not evidence of a clean batch.

**Where should the powder be kept?**

At −20°C, dry and dark. Let the vial warm to room temperature before opening so that condensation does not form on the powder, then reseal it promptly after use.

**Can it be used with cultured cells?**

It is supplied for laboratory research only. Suitability depends on endotoxin, sterility, solvent tolerance, and the cell model. Request the full certificate before planning a cell-based assay.

**Does the catalogue category imply a health claim?**

No. The category is a navigation label. The product is a research reagent, and the published work is preclinical.
