## Why This Compound

LL-37 sits at the intersection of two research traditions that rarely share standards. Antimicrobial-peptide work cares about minimum inhibitory concentration, membrane permeability and resistance evolution; innate-immunity work cares about receptor occupancy, cytokine output and cell-type specificity. The same peptide can be a credible reagent in both, but the quality attributes that matter are not identical. A batch with a clean HPLC trace and a low endotoxin value is a good starting point for either; a batch with a clean HPLC trace and no endotoxin data is only usable in the first.

The structural reason for the peptide's versatility is its amphipathicity. In aqueous solution LL-37 is largely disordered; in the presence of anionic membranes or detergent micelles it folds into an α-helix with a hydrophobic face and a cationic face. Sancho-Vaello and colleagues showed that the peptide can oligomerise and form channel-like assemblies in membrane mimics, which is one structural explanation for the membrane-permeabilisation phenotypes reported in the literature. The same property makes the peptide sticky, aggregation-prone and difficult to keep at a defined monomeric concentration.

For a laboratory, that means the buffer is part of the experiment. Salt concentration, pH, lipid composition and the presence of serum all change what LL-37 does. A supplier cannot control those variables, but a supplier can provide a peptide that is pure enough, salt-free enough and endotoxin-low enough for the researcher to control them. That is the standard this page is written against.

## What the Batch Record Should Show

Identity should be confirmed by intact mass and by MS/MS or peptide mapping. The intact mass of LL-37 is 4493.3 Da; the observed mass should be within the method's tolerance, and the adduct pattern should be consistent with the expected counterion. A mass match alone does not prove the sequence, because a scrambled isomer has the same mass. Fragmentation coverage should include the N-terminal helical region and the C-terminal tail, where sequence variants are most likely to be missed.

Purity should be measured by reversed-phase HPLC at 214 or 220 nm with a gradient that resolves the main peak from deletion and truncation products. LL-37 is made by solid-phase synthesis; the common impurities are n-1, n-2 and truncated sequences, plus oxidised forms if the batch has been exposed to air. The certificate should state the column, mobile phase, gradient, wavelength and integration threshold. A chromatogram image without method details is not enough to reproduce the result.

Counterion and water content are next. LL-37 is usually supplied as an acetate or trifluoroacetate salt. The counterion affects solubility, the pH of the reconstituted solution and the apparent purity by mass. Karl Fischer water content should be reported; a lyophilized peptide can hold several percent water and still appear dry. If the certificate reports only “peptide purity” and not the salt form, the peptide content per vial is uncertain.

For biological work, endotoxin is the attribute that most often decides whether a batch is usable. LL-37 is cationic and binds lipopolysaccharide; a contaminated batch can produce apparent immune activation that has nothing to do with the peptide's own signalling. Ask for a limulus amebocyte lysate result in endotoxin units per milligram, and ask whether the assay was performed on the lot or on a representative sample. The [endotoxin guide](/blog/what-are-endotoxins-why-they-matter/) explains why this matters more for cationic peptides than for neutral ones.

## Sourcing & Verification

LL-37 is a mature synthetic peptide with many suppliers and a wide price range. The cheap end of the market is dominated by repackaged material with minimal documentation; the expensive end includes custom synthesis with lot-specific release testing. Neither price nor country of origin predicts quality. The useful discriminator is the analytical package and the supplier's willingness to answer follow-up questions.

Start with the sequence and the mass. Ask for the calculated mass, the observed mass and the fragmentation table. Then ask for the purity method and a copy of the chromatogram with the integration parameters. Then ask for counterion, water content and endotoxin. A supplier who can produce all four within a day is probably working from a real batch record; a supplier who sends a one-page certificate with a logo is not.

The second check is the physical form. LL-37 powder should be white to off-white and should dissolve clear at the recommended concentration. A yellow or sticky powder suggests oxidation or moisture ingress. If the peptide is supplied as a solution, check the pH and the presence of particulates. The [COA reading guide](/blog/how-to-read-a-peptide-coa-a-researchers-guide-to-hplc-and-ms-reports/) walks through the fields in order.

The third check is the supplier's handling of the aggregation problem. LL-37 self-associates in salt-containing buffers, and a supplier who claims “no aggregation” without qualification is either unaware of the literature or overselling. Ask what buffer they recommend for reconstitution and whether they have tested recovery after filtration. A supplier who says “use water, avoid salt, and check by absorbance” is more credible than one who promises a universal protocol.

Finally, verify the internal links between the listing and the rest of the catalogue. A supplier who sells LL-37 should also be able to explain the difference between LL-37, KR-12 and a generic cathelicidin fragment. If every antimicrobial peptide on the site has the same description, the catalogue is a template, not a technical resource. ## Handling & Storage

Store the lyophilized powder at −20 °C, protected from light and moisture. Let the vial equilibrate to room temperature before opening. Weigh or transfer in a low-humidity environment; LL-37 is hygroscopic and a damp powder is harder to dissolve and more prone to oxidation.

For reconstitution, start with sterile water or a low-salt buffer. LL-37 is more soluble at acidic pH; if water alone gives a hazy solution, try 0.1% acetic acid or a dilute acetate buffer. Avoid phosphate-buffered saline as the first solvent, because the phosphate and chloride ions screen the cationic charges and promote self-association. Once the peptide is dissolved, you can dialyse or dilute into the assay buffer, but check for precipitation at each step.

Use low-binding polypropylene tubes. LL-37 adsorbs to glass and to some plastics, especially at low concentrations. If the assay requires a concentration below the adsorption-sensitive range, add a carrier such as 0.01% Tween-20 only if the assay tolerates it, and run a carrier-only control. Do not vortex aggressively; shear can promote aggregation.

Aliquot and freeze at −20 °C or −80 °C, and avoid repeated freeze-thaw. Aqueous solutions are less stable than the powder; the catalog note suggests using them within a week at 2-8 °C, but the practical limit depends on concentration, buffer and assay. Record the lot, the reconstitution date and the buffer. ## How It Compares

The closest comparator is KR-12, the 12-residue fragment of LL-37 that retains part of the antimicrobial activity but loses much of the receptor pharmacology. KR-12 is easier to synthesise and less aggregation-prone, which makes it a good control for membrane activity; it is not a substitute when the experiment depends on the full-length peptide's immunomodulatory domains.

Against melittin, LL-37 is less haemolytic and more receptor-active, but the two are often used together as membrane-active controls. Against human β-defensins, LL-37 is larger, more amphipathic and more promiscuous in its receptor interactions. Against a generic cationic antimicrobial peptide, the difference is the sequence-specific immunomodulation, which is exactly the part that is hardest to standardise.

Within the catalogue, [KPV](/catalog/kpv/) is a much smaller anti-inflammatory tripeptide, [Thymosin Alpha-1](/catalog/thymosin-alpha-1/) is an immune-modulating peptide with a different mechanism, and [BPC-157](/catalog/bpc-157/) is a repair-biology peptide with no direct antimicrobial claim. They are not interchangeable; the comparison is useful mainly for designing panels. If you need a membrane-active control, LL-37 and melittin are the usual pair; if you need a receptor-level control, use a selective FPR2 agonist instead.

## The Author's Take

LL-37 is one of the few peptides where I would pay a premium for endotoxin data. The molecule's biology is inseparable from its charge, and charge is exactly what makes it bind lipopolysaccharide. A batch that is 98% pure by HPLC but carries a high endotoxin load can produce a beautiful, reproducible immune-activation phenotype that is an artefact. ## Frequently Asked Questions

**What makes LL-37 unique among antimicrobial peptides?**

It is the only human cathelicidin, and it combines direct membrane activity with receptor-level signalling through formyl-peptide and purinergic receptors. That combination is why it appears in both antimicrobial and innate-immunity literature. The uniqueness is a research property, not a therapeutic claim.

**Why does LL-37 have context-dependent effects in cancer research?**

Published studies report different directions of effect in different tumour models, and the reasons are not fully resolved. Receptor expression, tumour microenvironment, peptide concentration and the presence of serum or lipids can all change the readout. The context dependence is a reason to control the model carefully, not a reason to treat the peptide as a therapy.

**What purity is your LL-37?**

The catalog specification is ≥98% by HPLC. The lot certificate states the measured value and the method. Ask for the chromatogram and the integration parameters if you need to compare batches.

**What are the storage requirements?**

Lyophilized powder at −20 °C, dry and protected from light. Aqueous solutions at 2-8 °C, used within about a week. LL-37 self-associates in salt-containing buffers, so low-salt reconstitution is preferred.

**How do I control for endotoxin in LL-37 assays?**

Ask for a lot-specific LAL result in EU/mg, run a vehicle control at the same counterion concentration, and include a polymyxin B inhibition control if the assay is sensitive to lipopolysaccharide. If the supplier cannot provide endotoxin data, assume the batch is not characterised for cell work.

**Why does LL-37 aggregate in salt buffers?**

The peptide is cationic and amphipathic. Chloride and phosphate ions screen the charges that keep it soluble, and the hydrophobic face drives self-association. Low ionic strength, slightly acidic pH and low-binding tubes reduce the problem.

**What is the difference between LL-37 and KR-12?**

KR-12 is a 12-residue fragment of LL-37 that retains membrane activity but lacks most of the C-terminal receptor-interacting region. It is easier to handle and useful as a control, but it is not a full-length substitute.

**Is LL-37 supplied as acetate or trifluoroacetate salt?**

The salt form depends on the batch and the purification method. Ask for the counterion on the certificate. If the assay is sensitive to trifluoroacetate, request an acetate-exchange batch or perform a counterion exchange before use.
