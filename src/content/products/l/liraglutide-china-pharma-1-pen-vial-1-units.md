## Why This Compound

Most peptide catalogues sell lyophilized powder because powder is forgiving. Liraglutide is different: it is a formulated, acylated peptide that has already been through a manufacturing process designed around solution stability. The C-16 fatty acid attached through a linker lets the peptide bind albumin and extends its residence time in circulation — a pharmacokinetic design feature that also changes how the molecule behaves in a vial. It adsorbs to plastic, it can form oligomers, and its methionine and asparagine residues are oxidation and deamidation hotspots.

For a research lab, those features are the reason to work with liraglutide rather than a generic GLP-1 fragment. Receptor-binding experiments need a ligand with the correct acylation pattern; remove the fatty acid and you no longer have the same molecule. Analytical method development needs a peptide that stresses the column and the mobile phase in realistic ways. Comparability work needs a reference that can be orthogonalised against an rDNA-derived product, which is exactly the problem Tang and colleagues addressed with a higher-order-structure and in-vitro-function framework.

There is also a practical reason this page exists in a China-sourcing catalogue. Liraglutide is one of the most counterfeited and most repackaged peptides in the world. Pen presentations add a second layer of risk: the cartridge, the pen mechanism, the fill volume and the label all become part of the product identity. A buyer who evaluates only the peptide content misses most of what can go wrong.

## What the Batch Record Should Show

A credible liraglutide batch record starts with identity, and identity here means more than a mass match. The record should show the intact mass consistent with the acylated peptide, a peptide map or MS/MS fragmentation that covers the N-terminus, the linker and the C-terminus, and an assessment of the acylation site. A mass that matches the expected value but a peptide map that shows a misplaced acyl chain is a different molecule with different receptor behaviour.

Purity should be reported from a stability-indicating HPLC method, not a generic gradient. Liraglutide has a long hydrophobic tail; related substances elute close to the main peak, and a shallow gradient is needed to resolve deamidated, oxidised and isomerised forms. The record should name the column, mobile phase, wavelength and integration threshold. A single “≥98%” number without those details is not a specification — it is a claim. The Bothe work on oligomerisation memory effects is useful here because it shows that sample history can change the physical state of the peptide before it ever reaches the column.

Assay and peptide content are separate numbers. Assay by UV at 280 nm depends on the aromatic residues and can drift with formulation excipients; nitrogen determination or amino-acid analysis gives an orthogonal check. Water content matters even in a solution presentation because the formulation is aqueous and the concentration is fixed. For a pen, fill volume and deliverable volume are product attributes, and the batch record should state both.

The rest of the record is presentation-specific: container closure integrity, sterility, endotoxin, visible and subvisible particulates, pH, and osmolality. If the material is offered as a research reference rather than a licensed finished product, the supplier should say so plainly and should not imply that a release panel for a medicine has been performed. Our position is that the documentation must match the claim; a research-use listing should carry research-grade analytics, not a counterfeit copy of a pharmaceutical release certificate.

## Sourcing & Verification

Liraglutide sourcing splits into two markets that are often confused. One is the licensed pharmaceutical supply chain, with its serialisation, cold chain and prescription controls. The other is the research-reference market, where material is sold for laboratory work and where the documentation burden falls on the buyer. This listing belongs to the second category. That distinction is the first thing to verify, because a supplier who blurs it is telling you something about how they handle every other claim.

The second verification step is the pen itself. Ask for photographs of the actual lot, not a stock image. Check the cartridge fill, the label text, the lot number and the expiry format against the accompanying documents. Pen mechanisms are mechanical devices; a batch that was assembled from mismatched components can pass a peptide assay and still fail as a product. If the supplier cannot describe the assembly and fill site, the presentation is not verifiable.

Third, ask for the analytical package before price. At minimum: intact mass, peptide map or MS/MS, HPLC purity with method details, assay, water content, counterion or excipient profile, sterility and endotoxin. For a solution presentation, also ask for particulate data and container-closure integrity. A supplier who can provide only a certificate of analysis with no method detail is not providing a batch record; they are providing a summary.

Fourth, check the cold chain. Liraglutide solution should not be frozen, and temperature excursions during international shipping are common. Ask how the shipment is packed, what logger data is included, and what the reshipment policy is if the logger shows a breach. ## Handling & Storage

Store the pen/vial presentation at 2-8 °C, protected from light, and do not freeze it. Freezing a solution formulation can irreversibly change the oligomer distribution and can damage the container closure. Keep the unit upright and in its original carton; labels and cartridges are not designed for loose storage.

Before opening, let the unit equilibrate to room temperature for a short period to reduce condensation on the septum. Wipe the septum with an appropriate laboratory disinfectant and use a fresh needle or transfer device for each aliquot. Do not pool material from multiple units unless the experiment explicitly requires a composite sample, because pooling destroys the lot-level traceability that the batch record is supposed to provide.

For analytical work, dilute in a buffer that matches the assay. Liraglutide is sensitive to adsorption at low concentrations; use low-binding tubes and consider a carrier protein only if the assay tolerates it. Avoid high shear and repeated freeze-thaw. If the assay requires a concentration series, prepare it fresh from a single stock rather than serially diluting in a way that compounds adsorption error.

Record the lot, the opening date, the storage temperature and any visible change. A hazy solution, a colour shift or a particle is a stop signal for that aliquot. Against semaglutide, liraglutide has a shorter acyl chain and a different albumin-binding profile; in the lab, the practical difference is that semaglutide is more hydrophilic and generally easier to keep in solution, while liraglutide is more surface-active. **Can I freeze the pen to extend storage?**

No. Freezing a solution formulation can alter the oligomer distribution and damage the container closure. Keep it at 2-8 °C, upright and protected from light. If you need a frozen archive, use a lyophilized research vial rather than a formulated pen.

**How do I verify the acylation site?**

Use a peptide map or MS/MS method that covers the lysine residue carrying the fatty-acid linker. An intact mass alone will not distinguish a correctly acylated peptide from one with the acyl chain on a different residue. Ask the supplier for the fragmentation coverage, not just the mass.

**What purity method is appropriate?**

A stability-indicating reversed-phase HPLC method with a shallow gradient and a wavelength that captures the peptide backbone, typically 214 or 220 nm. The method should resolve deamidated, oxidised and isomerised forms. A generic gradient run at 280 nm is not adequate for this molecule.

**How does liraglutide compare with semaglutide in the lab?**

Both are acylated GLP-1 receptor agonists, but the acyl chain and linker differ, which changes albumin binding, hydrophobicity and solution behaviour. Semaglutide is generally easier to handle in aqueous buffer; liraglutide is more surface-active. Use the one that matches the receptor construct and the assay matrix.
