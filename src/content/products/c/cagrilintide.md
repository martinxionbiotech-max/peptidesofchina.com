## Compound Profile

Cagrilintide is a long-acting acylated amylin analogue developed for metabolic research. Structurally derived from human amylin (IAPP — islet amyloid polypeptide), it is modified with a C20 fatty diacid chain to extend its plasma half-life to approximately 7 days, enabling once-weekly administration in clinical protocols. Research interest centers on its action at amylin receptors (AMY1, AMY2, AMY3) in the hypothalamus and area postrema, which are distinct from the GLP-1 receptor pathway targeted by semaglutide and related compounds. Preclinical and early clinical studies have examined cagrilintide's role in energy intake regulation, gastric emptying rate, and glucagon suppression. Unlike GLP-1 receptor agonists, amylin analogues appear to act via complementary central pathways, with satiety signals mediated through hindbrain amylin receptor populations. This mechanistic distinction has generated research interest in dual-agonism approaches combining amylin and GLP-1 receptor activity — most notably in the CagriSema combination, currently in phase 3 clinical evaluation.
