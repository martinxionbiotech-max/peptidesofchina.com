## Compound Profile

A blend is defined by three things: the identity of each component, the ratio between them, and the uniformity of that ratio from vial to vial. The components here are well characterized individually. Mod GRF 1-29 is a 29-residue GHRH analogue with four substitutions that improve stability, including a D-Ala2 that blocks DPP-IV cleavage; ipamorelin is a five-residue GHSR-1a agonist with two non-natural amino acids. Their molecular weights differ by a factor of nearly five, which makes them easy to distinguish by mass spectrometry and hard to quantify in a single chromatographic run without a gradient that resolves both.

The blend pairs CJC-1295 without DAC (Modified GRF 1-29) with ipamorelin. The GHRHR arm signals through Gs-cAMP-PKA; the GHSR-1a arm signals through Gq-PLC-PKC. Research use centres on GH-axis pharmacology, pituitary synergy, GH pulse architecture and IGF-1 regulation. ## Why This Compound

The rationale for the pair is receptor crosstalk. GHRHR is a Gs-coupled receptor: activation raises cAMP, activates PKA, and promotes both GH gene transcription and secretory granule release. GHSR-1a is Gq-coupled: activation produces inositol trisphosphate, releases calcium from intracellular stores, and triggers exocytosis through a separate arm. In a somatotroph with both receptors occupied, the two signals act on different nodes of the same machinery. The result in rodent pituitary cultures and intact animals is a GH peak that exceeds the sum of the two individual responses, which is why the combination appears so often in pulse-amplitude studies.

That synergy is also a confound if it is not controlled. A blend fixes the ratio, so a study cannot vary one component without changing the other. For a first characterization of the interaction, separate vials are more informative: the investigator can hold the GHRH concentration constant and titrate the GHSR-1a agonist, or the reverse. A pre-combined vial is better suited to a standardised protocol where the ratio is already established and the goal is reproducibility across batches or sites.

The blend is not a long-acting product. Both components are short-lived: Mod GRF 1-29 clears in roughly 25 to 30 minutes, and ipamorelin has a research half-life of about two hours in rodent studies. The resulting exposure is a discrete window, not a plateau. That aligns with the pulse-design logic described on the CJC-1295 without DAC page. A DAC-containing GHRH component would not match the ipamorelin kinetics, which is why the blend uses the non-DAC form.

There is a practical reason the pair is sold together as well. Two lyophilized powders mean two reconstitutions, two concentration calculations and two chances to introduce an error. A single vial with a fixed ratio removes those steps. The trade-off is analytical: the buyer now depends on the supplier's fill and mixing controls rather than on two independent certificates.

## What the Batch Record Should Show

Identity for a blend requires two separate confirmations. The certificate should show the intact mass of Mod GRF 1-29 at approximately 3368 Da and the intact mass of ipamorelin at 711.85 Da, each supported by a spectrum. A single mass spectrum of the mixture can show both peaks, but it does not prove the ratio; for that, a quantitative method is needed.

The ratio is the central specification. If the vial is labelled 5 + 5 mg, the record should state the measured content of each component and the acceptance range. A ratio expressed only as "5 mg + 5 mg" on the label, with no assay result, is a fill target rather than a measurement. Ask how the ratio was verified: a validated HPLC method with two standards, an LC-MS method with isotope-labelled internal standards, or amino acid analysis after hydrolysis. Each has different accuracy and different failure modes.

Uniformity across vials matters more than the content of any single vial. A fill solution can be homogeneous at the start and separate during a long lyophilisation run, particularly when the two peptides differ in size and hydrophobicity. The record should show content uniformity from multiple vials, not one. If the supplier reports a single assay from a composite sample, the number describes the batch average and says nothing about vial-to-vial variation.

Purity has to be reported per component. A single "≥98%" figure for the blend is ambiguous: it could mean each component is 98% pure, or that the combined chromatogram has 98% of its area in the two main peaks. Those are different claims. Ask for the related-substances profile of each peptide, including the specific degradants that matter: isoaspartate and truncation for the GHRH fragment, hydrolysis and aggregation for ipamorelin.

The rest of the record is conventional but should be complete: water content by Karl Fischer, acetate content, residual TFA, fill mass, cake appearance, pH of the reconstituted solution, endotoxin by LAL, and bioburden or sterility where claimed. For a co-lyophilized blend, the cake appearance can also tell you something: a single homogeneous cake suggests one fill solution, while two distinct layers or a mottled cake suggest separate fills or incomplete mixing.

## Sourcing & Verification

Ask what "pre-combined" means in the supplier's process. There are at least three possibilities. The two peptides can be dissolved together and lyophilized as one cake; they can be filled as two separate cakes in the same vial; or they can be supplied as two separate vials in one box. Each has a different uniformity risk. A single co-lyophilized cake is the most convenient but depends on solution homogeneity and a validated fill. Two cakes in one vial avoid mixing but can be harder to reconstitute evenly. Two vials in one box are the most flexible and the least ambiguous analytically.

Ask for the ratio verification method and the acceptance criteria. A supplier who cannot state a range has not defined the product. Ask whether the ratio is mass-based or molar. A 5 + 5 mg label is a mass ratio; because the molecular weights differ by nearly fivefold, the molar ratio is very different, and a study that reports concentrations in molar terms needs to make the conversion explicitly.

Ask for content uniformity data from at least three vials, preferably from the beginning, middle and end of the fill. Ask whether the batch was tested for both components after lyophilisation or only before. A pre-fill assay confirms the solution; a post-fill assay confirms the product. The difference is the lyophilisation step, which is exactly where segregation can occur.

## Handling & Storage

The vial belongs at -20°C in a desiccator, away from light. Equilibrate to room temperature before opening. A co-lyophilized cake can be hygroscopic, and repeated opening of the same vial introduces moisture that affects both components. If the vial is intended for multiple reconstitutions, split it into single-use aliquots rather than reopening it.

Reconstitute gently. Add solvent down the wall, swirl, and allow the cake to dissolve completely before measuring. A blend can take longer to dissolve than a single peptide because the two components have different solubilities: the GHRH fragment is polar and dissolves readily, while ipamorelin carries a hydrophobic naphthylalanine residue that can slow dissolution at higher concentrations. Do not vortex. If a film or particle remains after gentle swirling, do not assume it is excipient; it may be undissolved peptide, and the ratio in solution will not match the label.

For cell work, prepare a concentrated stock and dilute into the assay buffer. Both components adsorb to plastic and glass at low concentrations, so a carrier protein is advisable for dilute working solutions. If the assay uses serum-containing medium, note that the GHRH component is susceptible to DPP-IV in serum; the D-Ala2 substitution slows but does not eliminate that route. Incubation times and medium composition belong in the methods.

Split the solution into single-use aliquots before the first freeze. Once reconstituted, the blend is specified for 14 days at 2–8°C; for longer campaigns, hold the aliquots at -80°C. **What is in CJC-1295 + Ipamorelin?**

The blend contains CJC-1295 without DAC, also called Mod GRF 1-29, and ipamorelin. The first is a 29-amino-acid GHRHR agonist; the second is a five-amino-acid GHSR-1a agonist. Both are supplied as lyophilized powder for laboratory research.

**Why are these two compounds studied together?**

They activate different receptors on the same somatotroph. GHRHR raises cAMP through Gs, while GHSR-1a raises intracellular calcium through Gq. Activating both pathways at once produces a larger GH response than either alone in rodent models, which is the basis for the combination.

**How should the blend be stored?**

**Does the blend dissolve easily?**
