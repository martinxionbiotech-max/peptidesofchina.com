## Compound Profile

The peptide backbone is the same modified GHRH(1–29) sequence used in the non-DAC product, with a reactive maleimide group attached through the DAC modification. ## Why This Compound

GHRHR signaling is normally episodic. The hypothalamus releases GHRH in bursts, somatotrophs respond, and the hormone clears within minutes. Many research questions are about that rhythm: how pulse amplitude changes with age, how somatotroph sensitivity shifts after repeated exposure, whether continuous occupancy produces a different transcriptional response from intermittent occupancy. A short GHRH analogue is the tool for the pulse questions. The DAC form is the tool for the continuous-occupancy question.

The mechanism is a conjugation reaction. The Drug Affinity Complex is a maleimidopropionyl group; maleimides react with thiols through a Michael addition, and the dominant thiol in serum is the free cysteine at position 34 of albumin. The resulting thioether is stable, and because albumin has a long circulation time, the attached peptide rides along. Published work on CJC-1295 reported a plasma half-life measured in days, with the albumin conjugate acting as a circulating reservoir.

That design has consequences for how the compound is used. In a serum-free buffer, there is no albumin to bind, so the DAC group is intact but inactive as a half-life extender. In serum-containing medium or in vivo, conjugation happens quickly and the free peptide concentration falls. An in vitro assay that adds the peptide to complete medium is therefore not measuring the same exposure as an assay in buffer. This is not a flaw; it is the pharmacology. It does mean that medium composition belongs in the methods section.

The sustained profile also changes the interpretation of a GH-axis readout. A short GHRHR agonist produces a peak and a return to baseline within an hour, so serial sampling can resolve the pulse. A DAC conjugate produces a plateau, and the same sampling schedule will show a different curve. Designs that compare the two forms need to match sampling to the expected kinetics rather than reusing a pulse protocol. The distinction is pharmacological, not a matter of potency.

For work that needs a GHRH input with a defined end, the [CJC-1295 without DAC](/catalog/cjc-1295-without-dac/) product is the appropriate comparator. For work that needs a pre-mixed GHRH plus GHSR-1a combination, the [CJC-1295 + Ipamorelin](/catalog/cjc-1295-ipamorelin/) format exists, though the combination uses the short-acting GHRH component. Mixing a DAC peptide with a short GHSR-1a agonist produces mismatched kinetics, which is one reason that particular pairing is uncommon.

## What the Batch Record Should Show

Intact mass is the first check. The expected value is approximately 3459 Da, and the certificate should show a single dominant species at that mass. Two impurities are specific to this chemistry and should be reported separately. The first is unmodified peptide at about 3368 Da, which represents DAC that failed to couple or was lost. The second is hydrolysed maleimide at roughly 3477 Da: water opens the maleimide ring to a maleamic acid, and the ring-opened product cannot react with albumin. A batch with a large hydrolysed fraction will still pass an intact-mass check at the main peak, and it will still look pure by HPLC if the two species co-elute. It will not behave as a long-acting compound.

This is the analytical point that separates a serious DAC supplier from a catalog entry. Ask for the ratio of intact maleimide to hydrolysed maleimide, measured by mass spectrometry or by a cysteine-trapping assay. In a trapping assay, the batch is reacted with a known thiol and the extent of conjugation is measured; a batch with 80% intact maleimide will conjugate to 80% of the theoretical maximum. A supplier who reports only "purity ≥98%" has not answered the question that matters for this molecule.

HPLC purity should come with the method, wavelength and integration settings, plus a related-substances table. Because the hydrolysed and unhydrolysed forms differ by only 18 Da and can elute close together, the chromatographic separation needs to be described. If the method cannot resolve them, the purity figure is an aggregate and should be labelled as such. Water content by Karl Fischer is especially important here: moisture drives maleimide hydrolysis in the solid state, so a cake with high residual water will lose activity during storage even at -20°C.

Counterion and residuals complete the picture: acetate content, residual TFA, residual solvents, fill mass, cake appearance, pH of the reconstituted solution, endotoxin by LAL, and bioburden or sterility where claimed. The lot number should match the vials, the manufacturer and site should be named, and the document should carry a retest date.

One more check is worth requesting for a DAC peptide: a free-thiol assay. Maleimides can react with cysteine residues on the peptide itself if any are present, but this sequence has none, so the expected free-thiol content is zero. A nonzero result suggests a different molecule or a contaminated lot.

## Sourcing & Verification

The CAS number is not a reliable discriminator in this product family. The same registration, 863288-34-0, appears on certificates for the non-DAC peptide and for DAC-modified material in different catalogs. Ask for the measured mass and the hydrolysis ratio instead. If the certificate shows a main peak at 3368 Da, you are looking at the wrong compound regardless of the label.

Ask how the maleimide is protected during synthesis and purification. Maleimides are not stable at high pH, and a purification step above neutral can hydrolyse a meaningful fraction before the product is ever lyophilized. A manufacturer who understands this will describe a low-pH purification and a dry-down that limits water content. A reseller will repeat the phrase "high purity" and change the subject.

Ask whether the lot was tested for conjugation capacity, not just identity. The functional assay is simple: react the batch with an excess of a thiol such as cysteine or a small thiol-containing peptide, then measure the mass shift. A batch that conjugates stoichiometrically is what the long-residence claim depends on. A batch that does not is an expensive short-acting peptide with an extra mass unit.

The documentation trail should name the manufacturer and the site, not just a trading company. ## Handling & Storage

Keep the lyophilized powder at -20°C, desiccated, protected from light. Let the vial warm to room temperature before opening. The maleimide is the fragile part: it hydrolyses in the presence of water, and the rate rises with pH and temperature. A dry cake at -20°C is stable for its stated retest period; a cake that has absorbed moisture during repeated opening is not.

Reconstitute in a buffer at mildly acidic to neutral pH, and avoid alkaline conditions. Do not add dithiothreitol, β-mercaptoethanol, glutathione or any other thiol-containing reagent to a solution that contains the peptide. Those reagents will consume the maleimide before it can bind albumin, and the resulting preparation will behave like the non-DAC peptide. If a reducing agent is required for a separate step, remove it before the peptide is added, or use a thiol-free alternative and validate that it does not react with the maleimide.

For cell work, consider the medium. Complete medium contains albumin, and the peptide will conjugate to it. That is the intended mechanism, but it means the free concentration is lower than the nominal concentration, and the effect will vary with the serum lot. Serum-free medium gives a different exposure again. Report the medium composition, because the same nominal concentration can produce different results in the two systems.

Aliquot before freezing and avoid repeated freeze-thaw. The catalog allows two weeks at 2–8°C once reconstituted; beyond that, prepare single-use aliquots at -80°C. Do not filter a solution through a membrane that contains residual thiol or a high-binding polymer without checking recovery. ## Frequently Asked Questions

**What does DAC stand for in CJC-1295?**

DAC stands for Drug Affinity Complex. It is a maleimidopropionyl group attached to the peptide that reacts with the free cysteine of serum albumin, forming a covalent thioether. The albumin conjugate circulates longer than the free peptide, which is the basis for the extended research half-life.

**How long does CJC-1295 with DAC remain active in research models?**

Published work reports a half-life in the range of six to ten days for the albumin-bound form. The exact value depends on the model and the assay used to measure it. The non-DAC form, by contrast, clears in roughly 25 to 30 minutes.

**What is the difference between CJC-1295 with DAC and without DAC?**

The DAC version carries a reactive maleimide that binds albumin and extends exposure to days. The non-DAC version lacks that group and produces a short pulse. They share a modified GHRH(1–29) backbone but are different pharmacological tools.

**Why does the certificate need a hydrolysis ratio?**

The maleimide ring opens in water to form maleamic acid, which cannot bind albumin. A batch with a high hydrolysed fraction will pass a mass check at the main peak but will not produce the long-residence behavior. The ratio of intact to hydrolysed maleimide is the functional specification for this product.

**Can I add DTT or β-mercaptoethanol to a solution of this peptide?**

No. Thiol-containing reducing agents react with the maleimide and consume the reactive group before it can bind albumin. If a reducing step is required, remove the reagent before adding the peptide, or use a thiol-free alternative and confirm compatibility.

**How should the lyophilized powder be stored?**

Keep the vial at -20°C under desiccant and away from light; let it equilibrate before opening. After reconstitution, use within two weeks at 2–8°C. Moisture drives maleimide hydrolysis, so the dry state is the one that matters.

**Is the product supplied as a sterile powder?**

It is supplied as a lyophilized powder in sealed vials. Sterility and endotoxin results should be requested for the specific lot if your application requires them; not every catalog lot is tested on those parameters.

**Can it be used in cell culture?**

Yes, with a caveat. Serum-containing medium contains albumin, and the peptide will conjugate to it, lowering the free concentration. Serum-free medium gives a different exposure. Report the medium composition and the serum lot when comparing results across experiments.
