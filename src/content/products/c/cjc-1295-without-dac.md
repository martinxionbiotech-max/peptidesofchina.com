## Compound Profile

The molecule is a modified fragment, not a full-length hormone. Native GHRH is a 44-residue peptide; the 1–29 N-terminal fragment retains receptor binding and is the basis of sermorelin. Mod GRF 1-29 takes that fragment and changes four positions to slow enzymatic clearance. The result is a peptide that still signals through GHRHR but has a pharmacokinetic profile suited to controlled pulse experiments. It carries no albumin-binding modification, so its duration is measured in minutes and its behavior in a vial is that of an ordinary synthetic peptide.

## Why This Compound

GHRH is the hypothalamic signal that sets the amplitude of a GH pulse. It binds GHRHR on anterior pituitary somatotrophs, activates Gs, raises cAMP, and drives both GH synthesis and release. Native GHRH(1–29) does this well in a dish, but in plasma it is cleaved within minutes, largely by dipeptidyl peptidase IV removing the N-terminal Tyr-Ala dipeptide. That short residence is fine for a neuron firing in bursts; it is inconvenient for an experiment that needs a reproducible window of receptor occupancy.

Mod GRF 1-29 answers that problem with four changes: D-Ala at position 2, Gln at position 8, Ala at position 15, and Leu at position 27. The D-Ala2 substitution is the important one for stability, because DPP-IV cannot cleave the modified bond. The other three adjust the peptide's shape and remove the oxidation-prone methionine at position 27. The reported result is a half-life of roughly 25 to 30 minutes in research models, compared with about 7 minutes for the native fragment.

That timescale is the reason to choose this compound over the DAC version. A 30-minute window produces a defined secretory episode: the agonist is present, the somatotroph responds, and the signal clears. If the question is how pulse amplitude, pulse duration or inter-pulse interval change under an experimental condition, a short GHRHR agonist gives a cleaner temporal handle than a compound that remains active for days. The same property makes it useful in studies of somatotroph desensitisation, where repeated discrete exposures are easier to interpret than a continuous plateau.

The standard in vitro systems are primary rat anterior pituitary cells and GHRHR-transfected cell lines. In primary cultures, GH release into the medium is the readout, and a cAMP accumulation assay gives a second, receptor-proximal measure. In vivo work uses intact rodents for pulse profiling and GH-deficient models where the endogenous signal is low enough that an evoked response stands out. Co-administration with a GHSR-1a agonist is common because the two receptors converge on the same secretory machinery from different second messengers: cAMP from GHRHR, calcium from GHSR-1a.

What this compound is not is a long-acting analogue. If a design calls for sustained GHRHR occupancy, the [CJC-1295 with DAC](/catalog/cjc-1295-with-dac/) form is the appropriate tool, and the difference is pharmacological rather than a matter of degree. Confusing the two is the most common specification error in this product family.

## What the Batch Record Should Show

Identity starts with intact mass. The expected value is 3367.9 Da, and a single dominant peak at that mass is consistent with the 29-residue chain. Mass alone, however, cannot prove the D-Ala2 substitution. D-alanine and L-alanine are isobaric; they differ in configuration, not in elemental composition. A certificate that says "mass confirmed" has not demonstrated that the stability-conferring modification is present. To establish that, the supplier needs either chiral amino acid analysis after hydrolysis, NMR, or a digestion experiment in which DPP-IV cleaves the L-Ala variant but not the D-Ala one. Very few certificates include this, and asking for it is a quick way to learn how well the supplier knows the molecule.

Sequence coverage by MS/MS is the next layer. The 29-residue chain should be covered across its length, with the N-terminal and C-terminal fragments both represented. A partial map that covers only the middle of the peptide leaves the terminal modifications unverified. For a fragment peptide, the C-terminal amide is part of identity too; a free acid at the C-terminus is a different molecule with different binding behavior.

Purity by RP-HPLC at 214 nm should come with the method, the integration parameters and the impurity profile. Two degradation products deserve specific attention on this peptide. The Asp-Ala bond near the N-terminus can rearrange to isoaspartate under acidic or thermal stress, and isoAsp-containing variants often elute close to the parent peak, so a generous integration window can hide them. The second is truncation at the N-terminus, which removes the D-Ala2 that protects the molecule from DPP-IV. A batch that is 98% pure by area but contains several percent of des-D-Ala material will behave differently in a stability assay than the number suggests.

Counterion and residuals belong in the record: acetate content by ion chromatography, residual TFA from preparative purification, water content by Karl Fischer, and residual solvents if the synthesis used them. Fill mass per vial, cake appearance, pH of the reconstituted solution, endotoxin by LAL, and bioburden or sterility complete the package. The document should identify the manufacturer and site, carry a lot number that matches the vials, state the test methods and acceptance criteria, and give a retest date.

One structural point simplifies the stability story: replacing Met27 with Leu removes the principal oxidation site. There is no cysteine or tryptophan in the chain either. The degradation routes that matter are hydrolysis, isoaspartate formation and aggregation, not oxidation. A batch record that reports only "purity 98.5%" without a size-exclusion or aggregation check is answering a narrower question than the one a formulation study needs.

## Sourcing & Verification

Start with the name. "CJC-1295" is used loosely in the market to mean either the DAC or the non-DAC compound, and some catalogs list both under the same CAS number. The CAS number 863288-34-0 appears on certificates for material that is sold as Mod GRF 1-29 and on material sold as CJC-1295 with DAC, which means the number alone cannot settle identity. Ask for the measured intact mass and compare it with the certificate value. A difference of roughly ninety daltons between the two forms is easy to see on a mass spectrum and hard to explain away.

Ask who made the lot and whether the sequence was verified after synthesis. A supplier who buys bulk peptide and repackages it may never have seen a mass spectrum. Ask for the chromatogram file, not a transcribed table. Ask for the counterion and the residual TFA figure. Ask whether the lot was tested for isoaspartate content, and if not, whether the supplier understands why the question matters. The answers separate a manufacturer from a reseller more reliably than a factory photo.

The D-Ala2 question is the one worth pressing. If a supplier cannot explain why the modification is there, they are unlikely to have controlled it. A simple test is to ask what DPP-IV would do to the batch. The correct answer is that the L-Ala form is cleaved and the D-Ala form is resistant, which is the whole point of the substitution. A supplier who answers that the peptide is "stable" without engaging with the mechanism is telling you something about the depth of their technical file.

## Handling & Storage

Store the lyophilized powder at -20°C in a desiccated container, protected from light. Bring the vial to room temperature before opening to avoid condensation on the cake. The peptide is polar and carries several basic residues, so it dissolves readily in water and in buffered saline. A clear solution at a reasonable working concentration is normal; cloudiness suggests aggregation, contamination or a badly reconstituted cake, and the vial should not be used for a quantitative assay.

Reconstitute by adding solvent down the vial wall, then swirl rather than shake. Foam is a sign of interfacial stress, and peptide at an air-water interface can aggregate. If the solution needs to be sterile-filtered, filter the buffer first and pass the peptide through a low-protein-binding membrane, or accept the loss and quantify it. For cell work, prepare a concentrated stock and dilute into medium containing serum or a carrier protein; dilute aqueous peptide solutions adsorb to plastic and to glass, and the loss is proportionally larger at low concentrations.

Aliquot before the first freeze. Repeated freeze-thaw cycles change the aggregation profile without any visible cue. Keep working aliquots at -80°C, hold the reconstituted solution at 4°C for no longer than the 14 days in the specification, and discard any aliquot that shows particles or a shift in color. ## How It Compares

Against [CJC-1295 with DAC](/catalog/cjc-1295-with-dac/), the choice is entirely about time. The DAC form carries a maleimidopropionyl group that binds serum albumin covalently, extending its presence from minutes to days. That is useful for sustained-exposure designs and awkward for pulse work. The non-DAC form gives a clean on-off window and is the better instrument when the question is about timing.

Against [sermorelin acetate](/catalog/sermorelin-acetate/), the difference is the four substitutions. Sermorelin is the native GHRH(1–29) sequence and is cleared quickly. Mod GRF 1-29 resists DPP-IV and produces a longer, more reproducible response per unit of peptide in rodent models. Sermorelin remains a reasonable choice for work that wants the unmodified sequence, and it is usually cheaper.

Against [tesamorelin](/catalog/tesamorelin/), the contrast is size and duration. Tesamorelin is a 44-amino-acid GHRH analogue with a modified N-terminus, developed as a longer-acting molecule. It is a different research tool, not a drop-in substitute, and its analytical profile is correspondingly more complex.

The most common combination is with [ipamorelin](/catalog/ipamorelin/), which supplies the GHSR-1a arm. The pre-combined [CJC-1295 + Ipamorelin](/catalog/cjc-1295-ipamorelin/) vials fix the ratio; separate vials let a design vary each component. ## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is CJC-1295 without DAC?**

It is a 29-amino-acid GHRH analogue, also called Mod GRF 1-29, that activates GHRHR on pituitary somatotrophs. It carries four substitutions relative to native GHRH(1–29) that slow enzymatic clearance, giving a research half-life of roughly 25 to 30 minutes. It is supplied as a lyophilized powder for laboratory use only.

**How does it differ from CJC-1295 with DAC?**

The DAC version has a maleimidopropionyl group that binds serum albumin covalently, extending its activity to days. The non-DAC version has no such group and clears in about half an hour. One produces a discrete pulse; the other produces sustained receptor occupancy. They are different experimental tools, not two strengths of the same product.

**Is Mod GRF 1-29 the same as sermorelin?**

No. Sermorelin is the native GHRH(1–29) sequence. Mod GRF 1-29 has four substitutions, including D-Ala at position 2, that make it resistant to DPP-IV cleavage. The two are related but not interchangeable, and a certificate should state which one the lot contains.

**What do the four substitutions do?**

D-Ala2 blocks DPP-IV cleavage at the N-terminus. Gln8 and Ala15 adjust the peptide's conformation and receptor interaction. Leu27 replaces a methionine, removing the main oxidation site. Together they extend the half-life from about 7 minutes to roughly 25 to 30 minutes in research models.

**How can I verify the D-Ala2 modification?**

Intact mass cannot distinguish D-alanine from L-alanine because they are isobaric. Ask for chiral amino acid analysis, NMR, or a DPP-IV digestion experiment. A certificate that reports only molecular weight has confirmed chain length, not stereochemistry.
