## Compound Profile

Melanotan-2 (MT-2) is a synthetic cyclic analogue of alpha-melanocyte-stimulating hormone (α-MSH), a neuropeptide derived from the proopiomelanocortin (POMC) precursor. The compound is a 7-amino acid cyclic peptide — Ac-Nle-cyclo[Asp-His-D-Phe-Arg-Trp-Lys]-NH₂ — with key modifications relative to native α-MSH: norleucine substitutes Met⁴, D-phenylalanine replaces Phe⁷ for improved metabolic stability, and a lactam cyclisation bridges Asp⁵ and Lys¹⁰. These changes confer resistance to serum peptidases and increase receptor binding potency approximately 1000-fold over native α-MSH. MT-2 is a non-selective melanocortin agonist active at MC1R, MC3R, MC4R, and MC5R, with this broad receptor profile underpinning its wide research use across melanogenesis, energy homeostasis, and sexual behaviour models. The majority of preclinical research on MT-2 has addressed three functional areas: melanogenesis via MC1R in melanocyte models, CNS-mediated sexual behaviour via MC4R in hypothalamic circuits, and energy intake/expenditure modulation via MC3R and MC4R in metabolic research. MT-2 was the original compound that prompted the development of more selective melanocortin agonists — PT-141 for arousal research (lower MC1R activity) and afamelanotide for photoprotection (MC1R selective). Understanding MT-2's broad receptor activity remains essential for interpreting the mechanistic literature on melanocortin system biology. ## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the key pharmacological difference between Melanotan-2 and PT-141?**

Melanotan-2 is a non-selective melanocortin agonist with high affinity at MC1R, MC3R, MC4R, and MC5R. MC1R activation drives significant melanogenesis (increased melanin production). PT-141 (bremelanotide) is a cyclic peptide derived from MT-2 with substantially reduced MC1R affinity, essentially eliminating melanogenic activity at research doses while retaining MC3R/MC4R activity. Researchers select between them depending on whether MC1R activity is desired (melanogenesis studies) or should be excluded (arousal/anti-inflammatory studies).
