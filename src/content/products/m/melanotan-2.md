## Compound Profile

Melanotan-2 (MT-2) is a synthetic cyclic analogue of alpha-melanocyte-stimulating hormone (α-MSH), a neuropeptide derived from the proopiomelanocortin (POMC) precursor. The compound is a 7-amino acid cyclic peptide — Ac-Nle-cyclo[Asp-His-D-Phe-Arg-Trp-Lys]-NH₂ — with key modifications relative to native α-MSH: norleucine substitutes Met⁴, D-phenylalanine replaces Phe⁷ for improved metabolic stability, and a lactam cyclisation bridges Asp⁵ and Lys¹⁰. These changes confer resistance to serum peptidases and increase receptor binding potency approximately 1000-fold over native α-MSH. MT-2 is a non-selective melanocortin agonist active at MC1R, MC3R, MC4R, and MC5R, with this broad receptor profile underpinning its wide research use across melanogenesis, energy homeostasis, and sexual behaviour models. The majority of preclinical research on MT-2 has addressed three functional areas: melanogenesis via MC1R in melanocyte models, CNS-mediated sexual behaviour via MC4R in hypothalamic circuits, and energy intake/expenditure modulation via MC3R and MC4R in metabolic research. MT-2 was the original compound that prompted the development of more selective melanocortin agonists — PT-141 for arousal research (lower MC1R activity) and afamelanotide for photoprotection (MC1R selective). Understanding MT-2's broad receptor activity remains essential for interpreting the mechanistic literature on melanocortin system biology. ## Why This Compound

Melanotan-2 was designed to solve a pharmacology problem. Native alpha-MSH is a linear tridecapeptide that is rapidly degraded in serum, so its effects are short-lived and its receptor occupancy is hard to control in an experiment. The MT-2 sequence makes three changes: norleucine at position four, D-phenylalanine at position seven, and a lactam bridge between the side chains of aspartate at position five and lysine at position ten. The bridge closes the peptide into a ring. The D-amino acid and the ring together make the molecule much more resistant to peptidases, and the result is a longer-acting, higher-potency agonist in receptor assays.

The same changes create the analytical problem. A cyclic peptide is not just a sequence; it is a sequence plus a topology. If the lactam bridge opens, the molecule becomes linear. The linear form has the same amino acids in the same order, plus the elements of water, and it may retain some receptor binding. A certificate that reports the correct molecular weight by a method that cannot distinguish cyclic from linear, or that reports a purity percentage without saying what the main peak is, does not prove that the product is MT-2. It proves that the sample contains a peptide with the right amino-acid composition.

The receptor profile is broad. MT-2 activates MC1R, MC3R, MC4R, and MC5R, and the research literature reflects that breadth. In melanocyte models, MC1R activation drives the cAMP-MITF-tyrosinase axis and increases melanin content. In hypothalamic circuits, MC4R activation has been studied in relation to energy balance and sexual behaviour. In immune and gut models, melanocortin signalling has anti-inflammatory effects, though KPV rather than MT-2 is the usual tool for that work. A researcher who wants to isolate one receptor subtype should not use MT-2. The compound is a non-selective tool, and its value is in experiments where a broad melanocortin response is the question.

The published evidence is mostly preclinical. Rodent studies dominate the energy-balance and behavioural literature; cell-culture studies dominate the melanogenesis work. Early human studies with MT-2 exist, including a 1996 phase I evaluation, but they are small and not a basis for claiming that any particular outcome is established. The gap between a receptor-binding assay and a whole-organism effect is large, and the gap between a rodent model and a human is larger. A product page should not paper over that.

There is also a regulatory dimension. Melanotan-2 is not approved as a medicine in the United States or the European Union, and its status varies by jurisdiction. This catalog sells it as a laboratory research material, not as a therapeutic. Researchers should confirm that possession and use are permitted in their own jurisdiction before ordering. That is not a sales disclaimer; it is a practical condition of working with a compound that regulators have looked at closely.

## What the Batch Record Should Show

The first field is identity by mass spectrometry. The calculated molecular weight of MT-2 is 1024.18 Da, so the protonated ion should appear near m/z 1025 in positive-ion mode. The certificate should show the spectrum, not just the number, and the observed mass should be within a stated tolerance of the calculated value. A spectrum with a strong peak at m/z 1042 suggests hydrolysis of the lactam bridge: the linear form is about 18 Da heavier because it has gained the elements of water.

The second field is purity by reverse-phase HPLC. The certificate should give the column, mobile phase, gradient, wavelength, and integration method. It should also state whether the method resolves the cyclic peptide from the linearised form and from diastereomers. Diastereomers are a real risk in a peptide with a D-amino acid: a synthesis that racemises at that position produces a peptide with the same mass and a different shape. Mass spectrometry will not distinguish them, and a shallow HPLC gradient may not either. A method with adequate resolution is the only way to see the difference.

The third field is counterion. MT-2 is commonly supplied as the acetate salt, though trifluoroacetate appears in some research-grade material. The counterion affects solubility and mass balance, and it can affect the apparent pH of a reconstituted solution. The certificate should state which counterion is present and give the residual content if the product is intended for sensitive assays. A peptide content figure that ignores counterion and water overstates the amount of active material.

The fourth field is water content by Karl Fischer titration. A lyophilised powder can hold several percent water, especially if it was exposed to humid air during filling or shipping. The certificate should give the result and the fill mass. A 10 mg vial that contains 8% water and 6% acetate has substantially less peptide than the label implies. This is not a reason to reject a batch automatically, but it is a reason to calculate the actual peptide mass before planning an experiment.

The fifth field is appearance and solubility. MT-2 is typically a white to off-white powder. It should dissolve in water or aqueous buffer, though the acetate salt may dissolve more readily than the free base. The certificate can record appearance, but appearance is not an identity test. A white powder can be the wrong peptide.

Finally, the record should include the batch number, manufacture date, retest date, and site of manufacture. If the certificate does not link to the vial label, the rest of the data is unanchored. For a compound with a known history of grey-market substitution, traceability is not a bureaucratic nicety.

## Sourcing & Verification

The first question is whether the certificate shows a mass spectrum on the finished lot. A synthesis report with a calculated mass is not the same as a measured spectrum. Ask for the observed m/z and the ion mode. If the supplier cannot provide a spectrum, the identity claim is unverified.

The second question is how the method handles the cyclic-versus-linear distinction. Ask whether the HPLC gradient was developed to resolve the lactam-opened form, and whether the certificate reports any hydrolysed peptide as a related substance. A supplier who has never thought about the lactam bridge is unlikely to have a method that detects its loss. A supplier who has thought about it will have an answer about resolution and forced-degradation data.

The third question is the diastereomer profile. MT-2 contains D-phenylalanine, and racemisation during synthesis or storage can produce a diastereomer with the same mass. Ask whether the method reports diastereomeric purity or only peak-area purity. If the answer is "HPLC purity is 98%," ask what the other 2% is. The answer may be innocuous, but the buyer should hear it.

The fourth question is counterion and peptide content. Ask for acetate content, water content, and the calculated peptide mass per vial. A certificate that reports only "10 mg" and "≥98%" is a label, not a mass balance. For a 10 mg vial, the difference between 9.2 mg and 8.1 mg of peptide is material in a small assay.

The fifth question is storage and shipping. MT-2 is light-sensitive, and heat exposure during transit can accelerate hydrolysis. Ask how the product is shipped, whether it is protected from light, and whether the batch has a stability assessment at the recommended storage temperature. A supplier who ships lyophilised powder at ambient temperature without a stability rationale is asking the buyer to absorb the risk.

## Handling & Storage

Store lyophilised MT-2 at −20°C long-term and 2–8°C short-term, protected from light and moisture. The powder is hygroscopic; let the vial equilibrate before opening. Keep it in a desiccator and avoid repeated warming. An amber vial or a foil wrap is appropriate for the stock.

Reconstitution should use a buffer that keeps the peptide soluble and the pH near neutral. The lactam bridge is susceptible to hydrolysis at high pH, so avoid strongly basic conditions. If the assay requires a particular buffer, check compatibility before dissolving the whole vial. Prepare the solution gently; do not vortex vigorously, because foaming and surface adsorption can change the concentration.

Because the molecule is light-sensitive, keep solutions in amber tubes or foil-wrapped containers. Aliquot into single-use volumes and store at −20°C or below. Avoid repeated freeze-thaw cycles. A solution that has been thawed and refrozen several times is not the same sample that was characterised, even if it still looks clear.

Solution stability is assay-dependent. The published storage recommendation is a starting point, not a guarantee for every buffer. If the experiment runs for several days, establish a time course and check for hydrolysis products rather than assuming the peptide is intact. This is especially important for a cyclic peptide, where the loss of the bridge may not produce a visible change.

This is laboratory material, supplied for in vitro and preclinical research. It is not for human or veterinary use, and nothing here is a dosing instruction. Reconstitution volumes and exposure conditions belong in an approved study protocol.

## How It Compares

[Melanotan-1](/catalog/melanotan-1/) is the linear, 13-amino-acid analogue, often called afamelanotide. It is more MC1R-selective than MT-2 and is the better tool when the study is about melanocyte biology without the MC3R/MC4R effects. The two are not interchangeable: MT-1 is linear and longer-acting in some formulations, while MT-2 is cyclic and broader in receptor activity.

[PT-141](/catalog/pt-141-bremelanotide/) is a cyclic melanocortin peptide derived from MT-2 with lower MC1R activity. It is the more selective tool for MC4R-centred research, and it is the right comparator when the question is whether an effect is mediated by MC4R rather than MC1R. [KPV](/catalog/kpv/) is a much smaller α-MSH fragment with an anti-inflammatory research profile; it is not a melanocortin agonist in the same sense and should not be used as a substitute in pigmentation or energy-balance studies.
