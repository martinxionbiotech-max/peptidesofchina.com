## Compound Profile

MOTS-c (Mitochondrial Open Reading Frame of the 12S rRNA type-c) is a 16-amino acid mitochondrial-derived peptide (MDP) — a class of bioactive peptides encoded by short open reading frames within the mitochondrial genome, termed mitokines. MOTS-c is encoded by the 12S ribosomal RNA gene and was first characterized in 2015 by Lee et al. at the University of Southern California. It is notable for being translated within mitochondria and capable of translocating to the nucleus, acting as a regulator of gene expression and energy metabolism. Preclinical research has focused on MOTS-c in the context of metabolic regulation and glucose homeostasis. Rodent studies have shown that MOTS-c administration activates AMPK, inhibits the folate-methionine cycle, and results in improved insulin sensitivity and reduced adiposity in diet-induced obesity models. Age-related decline of MOTS-c in plasma has been observed in both rodent and human observational data, positioning it within longevity and metabolic aging research as an exercise-mimetic compound.
