## Why This Compound

Hexarelin belongs to the growth-hormone secretagogue family, a group of small peptides that activate the ghrelin receptor, GHS-R1a. The receptor is a Gq/Gi-coupled GPCR concentrated in the pituitary and hypothalamus, and its activation triggers a calcium signal that drives GH release from somatotrophs. Hexarelin's sequence is His-D-2-MeTrp-Ala-Trp-D-Phe-Lys-NH₂. The D-2-methyltryptophan at position two is the feature that separates it from GHRP-6, which carries unmodified tryptophan, and from GHRP-2, which carries 2-naphthylalanine. Structure-activity work by Bowers and others established that this substitution raises potency at GHS-R1a, which is why hexarelin is often used as the high-potency reference in secretagogue comparisons.

The second story is the one that makes hexarelin more than a potency benchmark. In the late 1990s, Locatelli and colleagues showed that hexarelin protected the isolated rat heart from ischaemia-reperfusion injury in hypophysectomised animals, where pituitary GH release cannot explain the effect. Bodart and colleagues then identified CD36, a scavenger receptor expressed on cardiomyocytes and other cells, as a binding partner for hexarelin and other GHRPs. That work split the pharmacology into a GH-dependent arm and a GH-independent arm, and it gave cardiac researchers a peptide probe for a receptor that had been studied mainly as a fatty acid translocase.

For a laboratory, the practical consequence is that hexarelin can be used in two quite different experimental designs. In pituitary or hypothalamic preparations, the endpoint is GHS-R1a signalling and GH release. In cardiomyocyte or isolated-heart preparations, the endpoint is CD36-dependent survival, contractile recovery or infarct size, and the experiment usually includes hypophysectomy, somatostatin suppression or a CD36-knockout arm to separate the two mechanisms. Mixing the two designs without those controls produces results that cannot be attributed.

There is a third, quieter reason hexarelin remains useful: it is a clean test of peptide quality. The molecule has two tryptophans that oxidise, a C-terminal amide that can be lost, and a non-natural residue that a supplier may substitute if the building block is unavailable. A batch that passes a careful identity check is evidence of a competent synthesis line, which is more than a purity percentage can tell you.

## What the Batch Record Should Show

Hexarelin's certificate should be read as a sequence problem first and a purity problem second. Six residues is short enough that a mass spectrometer can confirm the whole molecule, and short enough that a single-residue error is easy to miss if the supplier reports only the nominal mass.

**Identity by mass and fragmentation.** The monoisotopic mass of the free peptide is about 886.4 Da, and the commonly reported average mass is 887.1 Da. A certificate that lists 887.1 without a spectrum is quoting a catalogue value. Ask for the observed mass with charge states and, for a peptide this size, MS/MS fragmentation that covers the N-terminus, the 2-methyltryptophan and the C-terminal amide. The amide is worth checking explicitly: the free acid is about 1 Da heavier, and the two can co-elute on a short HPLC method.

**The non-natural residue.** D-2-methyltryptophan is not a standard building block. A supplier may substitute D-tryptophan or omit the methyl group, producing a peptide with a different mass and a different potency. The mass spectrum should show the expected increment, and an amino acid analysis or NMR check is reasonable for a first batch. This is the single most common identity failure in the hexarelin market.

**Purity with method detail.** RP-HPLC at 214 nm is standard. Ask for the column, gradient and integration method, and look at the peak shape: tryptophan-containing peptides often show a broad or split peak when oxidation products are present. A purity of 98 percent by area can still hide several percent of oxidised peptide if the oxidised forms co-elute.

**Oxidation and counterion.** Two tryptophans make hexarelin sensitive to oxidation during synthesis, purification and storage. The certificate should report the principal oxidised species or, at minimum, show a chromatogram clean enough to argue they are absent. The counterion is usually acetate or trifluoroacetate; it changes the mass of material in the vial and should be named. Residual TFA matters for cell work.

**Peptide content and fill.** A 5 mg vial contains peptide plus counterion plus water. Peptide content by amino acid analysis or nitrogen determination converts fill weight into usable mass. Without it, a nominal 1 mg/mL solution may be 15 to 25 percent off.

**Endotoxin and sterility.** Relevant for primary cell work and for any preparation that contacts cultured cardiomyocytes. Neither test is universal for a catalogue peptide, but the certificate should say whether it was run. ## Sourcing & Verification

Hexarelin sits in a crowded category where most listings use the same few phrases and very few show a spectrum. The questions below are the ones that separate a synthesis house from a repackager.

- Can the supplier show MS/MS coverage of the 2-methyltryptophan position, not just a nominal mass?
- Is the C-terminal amide confirmed, or is the certificate silent about the C-terminus?
- Is the counterion stated, and is residual TFA reported?
- What is the peptide content per vial, and how was it measured?
- Is oxidised peptide reported as a separate peak?
- Is the batch number on the certificate the same as the vial label?
- Does the supplier manufacture the peptide, or buy bulk powder and aliquot it?

A useful test is to ask for the chromatogram as a separate file rather than a page inside a PDF. Suppliers who generate their own data usually have it; resellers usually do not. ## Handling & Storage

Lyophilised hexarelin is stored at −20°C, dry and protected from light. The tryptophan side chains are the weak point: they oxidise under light, in the presence of peroxides, and at alkaline pH. Keep the powder away from sunlight, avoid prolonged exposure to air after opening, and do not store a reconstituted solution on the bench.

Let the vial reach room temperature before opening to avoid condensation. Reconstitute with water or buffer directed down the vial wall, then swirl gently. Do not vortex: hexarelin foams readily, and the air-liquid interface is where oxidation and aggregation begin. If the solution is cloudy, allow it to stand rather than adding more solvent.

Aqueous solutions are usually held at 2–8°C for short-term use. For longer experiments, aliquot into single-use portions and freeze at −20°C or below; repeated freeze-thaw cycles increase oxidation and adsorption losses. Use low-binding tubes, especially at low micromolar concentrations. If the assay is sensitive to trace metals, note that copper and iron accelerate tryptophan oxidation, so chelator-free buffers are not always the better choice.

For cell culture, filter through a 0.22 µm membrane and check recovery. The [reconstitution guide](/blog/peptide-lyophilization-reconstitution-guide-for-researchers/) and the [handling pillar](/peptide-handling-protocols/) cover the general workflow. Everything above is bench-scale handling of a research reagent; none of it is a clinical procedure.

## How It Compares

**GHRP-6.** The closest analogue and the usual comparator. GHRP-6 carries tryptophan at position two and is less potent at GHS-R1a; it also has a longer history in appetite and ghrelin research. Hexarelin is the sharper tool for potency comparisons, GHRP-6 the more common reference for feeding studies.

**GHRP-2.** 2-naphthylalanine at position two, with a different potency and selectivity profile. GHRP-2 is often used where a longer-acting secretagogue is wanted. The three peptides are not interchangeable in a concentration-response experiment, and the [family overview](/blog/growth-hormone-peptides-wholesale-a-2026-b2b-procurement-guide/) explains why.

**Ipamorelin.** A pentapeptide with greater selectivity for GH release and less effect on appetite-related pathways in some models. Researchers choosing between ipamorelin and hexarelin are usually choosing between selectivity and potency, not between "better" and "worse". The [ipamorelin sourcing guide](/blog/sourcing-ipamorelin-from-china-a-2026-b2b-procurement-guide/) covers the practical side.

**Ghrelin.** The endogenous ligand, 28 residues, with a unique octanoyl modification on serine three. Ghrelin activates GHS-R1a but also has effects that hexarelin does not reproduce. It is the wrong comparator if the question is CD36.

**GHRH analogues.** Sermorelin, CJC-1295 and tesamorelin act on the GHRH receptor, a different GPCR on somatotrophs. They are upstream of the same GH pulse but pharmacologically distinct. Combining a GHRH analogue with a GHRP is a common research design precisely because the two receptors are separate.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What makes hexarelin different from GHRP-6?**

The 2-methyltryptophan at position two, which raises potency at GHS-R1a, and the C-terminal amide. Hexarelin also has a better-documented CD36 interaction in cardiac tissue. GHRP-6 is cheaper and has a longer history in feeding research, but the two are not interchangeable at the same mass.

**Is hexarelin's cardiac effect independent of growth hormone?**

Yes, in the models where it has been tested. The protection persists in hypophysectomised animals and in the presence of somatostatin, which is why CD36 rather than the pituitary receptor is the proposed mediator. That does not make the effect universal or clinical; it is a preclinical observation with a specific experimental basis.

**Why does the certificate need MS/MS rather than only a mass?**

Because a six-residue peptide with a non-natural amino acid can be wrong in ways that a nominal mass nearly matches. Fragmentation confirms the position of the methyl group and the C-terminal amide. A single mass value is consistent with several plausible sequences.

**What purity do you supply?**

The catalogue specification is ≥98 percent by RP-HPLC, with identity confirmed by mass spectrometry. The certificate should be read for method detail and for oxidised species, not just for the headline number.

**How should the powder be stored?**

At −20°C, desiccated and protected from light. Tryptophan oxidation is the main degradation route, so light and peroxide exposure matter more than a few degrees of temperature.

**Can hexarelin be used in isolated heart or cardiomyocyte experiments?**

It is used in those systems in the published literature, usually with hypophysectomy or CD36 knockout controls to separate GH-dependent from GH-independent effects. The catalogue material is research-grade and not sterile by default; filter if the preparation requires it.

**Is hexarelin a controlled substance?**

It is not scheduled in most jurisdictions, but import rules for research peptides vary. Confirm the rules for your country and keep the material under research-use-only terms. The [compliance pillar](/peptide-compliance-risk/) covers the general framework.
