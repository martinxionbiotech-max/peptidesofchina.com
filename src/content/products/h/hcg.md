HCG is the one item in a peptide catalogue that is not really a peptide. It is a glycoprotein hormone of about 38 kDa, assembled from two non-covalently linked subunits and decorated with carbohydrate chains that carry a substantial share of its behaviour. That single fact changes almost everything a buyer should ask about it: how activity is measured, why the vial is labelled in international units rather than milligrams, why two batches with the same sequence can differ in potency, and why a certificate copied from a synthetic peptide template tells you almost nothing.

This page is written for laboratory buyers who need to characterise the material before an assay, not for anyone seeking a product for personal use. The compound is supplied strictly for in vitro and preclinical research. What follows covers the receptor biology that makes HCG a useful tool, the records a batch should carry, the handling details that affect reproducibility, and the questions worth asking before a first order.

## Why This Compound

HCG binds the luteinising hormone/choriogonadotropin receptor, LHCGR, a Gs-coupled GPCR found on Leydig cells, granulosa cells and luteal cells. The alpha subunit is shared with LH, FSH and TSH; the beta subunit is specific to HCG and supplies most of the receptor contact surface. The crystal structure solved by Lapthorn and colleagues in 1994 remains the reference picture of how that heterodimer sits on the receptor, and it explains why the beta subunit, not the alpha, is the immunologically and pharmacologically distinguishing half of the molecule.

The reason HCG appears in so many laboratory protocols rather than LH is duration. Native LH is cleared quickly, while HCG carries additional O-linked oligosaccharides capped with sialic acid, which slow hepatic and renal clearance. Choi and Smitz described the consequences in a pair of reviews: the two hormones activate the same receptor and the same cAMP/PKA cascade, but HCG holds occupancy far longer. For an assay that needs a stable, sustained receptor signal rather than a pulse, that difference is the whole point.

Three experimental formats recur. In Leydig cell work, HCG is the standard agonist for measuring steroidogenic capacity, with MLTC-1, MA-10 or primary Leydig cultures and readouts such as cAMP accumulation, StAR induction or testosterone secretion by immunoassay. In granulosa cell work, it is used to study luteinisation, progesterone synthesis and cumulus expansion. In receptor pharmacology, LHCGR-transfected cell lines allow a cleaner look at desensitisation, internalisation and beta-arrestin recruitment without the background of a steroidogenic cell.

There is also a caution built into the biology. Prolonged agonist exposure downregulates LHCGR and desensitises the steroidogenic response, a phenomenon documented repeatedly in Leydig cell studies. A researcher who treats HCG as a simple on-switch will misread a desensitisation experiment as a potency difference. That is an assay-design issue, but it is also a sourcing issue: if the IU content per vial is not what the label says, the desensitisation curve shifts and the conclusion changes.

Finally, HCG is a glycoprotein with real heterogeneity. Urinary preparations contain nicked forms, free beta subunit and beta-core fragments; recombinant preparations are cleaner but still carry glycan variation. A supplier who reports only "purity ≥98%" has measured one thing and implied another. The rest of this page is about closing that gap.

## What the Batch Record Should Show

A meaningful HCG certificate answers four questions in order: how much active hormone is in the vial, how that activity was measured, what else is in the vial, and which lot the numbers belong to. Most certificates answer the first badly and skip the rest.

**Activity assignment.** IU values are defined against a WHO international reference preparation. The assay behind the number matters. Classical in-vivo bioassays, cell-based cAMP or reporter assays, and immunoassays do not agree perfectly, and an immunoassay measures immunoreactivity rather than receptor activation. A certificate should name the method, the reference standard and the uncertainty. A bare "5000 IU" with no method is a label, not a measurement.

**Subunit integrity.** HCG can be nicked in the beta subunit or dissociate into free subunits, both of which reduce activity without changing the total protein mass. Reducing and non-reducing SDS-PAGE, plus a free-beta immunoassay where available, show whether the heterodimer is intact. This is the glycoprotein equivalent of checking a peptide's sequence.

**Glycosylation.** Sialic acid content and isoform pattern by isoelectric focusing are the two practical measures. They explain batch-to-batch differences in clearance and bioactivity that a purity number cannot. For urinary material, the isoform spread is usually wider; for recombinant material, it is narrower but not uniform.

**Aggregation and fill.** Size-exclusion HPLC gives monomer content; a glycoprotein can aggregate during lyophilisation or storage. Karl Fischer moisture and fill volume determine how much powder is actually in the vial. A 5000 IU vial that is overfilled by 10 percent is not a scandal, but the overfill should be stated rather than discovered.

**Process contaminants.** For urinary-source material, residual urinary protein is the relevant impurity; for recombinant material, host cell protein and DNA. Endotoxin by LAL and a sterility test belong in the package if the material will contact primary cells. Neither is automatic for a research-grade lyophilised powder, and the certificate should say whether they were run or simply not performed.

**Traceability.** Lot number matching the vial, manufacturing site, fill date, retest date. A certificate that could describe any HCG batch is not a batch record. ## Sourcing & Verification

HCG is regulated as a prescription medicine in many jurisdictions, and research supply sits in an uncomfortable space between laboratory reagent and controlled product. Before price, confirm that the material can be imported lawfully for the intended laboratory use and that the supplier will provide the paperwork the shipment needs. The [customs guide](/blog/research-peptide-customs-clearance-a-professional-b2b-sourcing-guide-2026/) covers the general documentation chain; HCG usually needs more, not less.

The compound-specific questions are these.

- Is the activity expressed in IU per vial, and by which assay method? Ask for the bioassay or cell-based assay, not only an immunoassay.
- Is the source urinary or recombinant? The impurity profile, isoform pattern and regulatory status differ.
- What is the free beta subunit content, and was nicking assessed?
- What is the sialic acid content or IEF isoform pattern?
- Is the fill volume and overfill stated?
- Was endotoxin tested, and by which method?
- Does the lot number on the certificate match the vial label and the shipping documents?
- Is there a cold-chain record for the international leg?

A pattern worth recognising: certificates that report a single HPLC purity figure for a glycosylated heterodimer, with no subunit or bioactivity data. That is a synthetic-peptide certificate applied to a protein. It does not prove the material is bad, but it proves the supplier has not characterised it. ## Handling & Storage

Lyophilised HCG is stable at −20°C for extended periods when kept dry and protected from light. Let the vial equilibrate to room temperature before opening, because condensation on a cold vial is a direct route to moisture-driven degradation. Wipe the septum, open in a low-humidity space, and work quickly.

Reconstitute gently. Add water or buffer down the wall of the vial rather than directing a stream at the cake, and swirl rather than vortex. Glycoproteins are more sensitive to interfacial denaturation than short synthetic peptides, and foaming at the air-liquid interface is a visible sign that you are damaging the material. If the solution is cloudy after gentle mixing, let it stand; do not add more solvent immediately.

Reconstituted HCG is typically held at 4°C for up to seven days. That window is a handling convention, not a licence to keep a working stock indefinitely. Divide the solution into single-use aliquots if the experiment runs longer, and avoid repeated freeze-thaw cycles, which promote aggregation and loss of subunit association. Low-binding tubes reduce adsorption of a protein that is present at low molar concentration.

For cell culture, filter through a 0.22 µm membrane if sterility is required, and check recovery: some membrane materials adsorb glycoproteins. The [reconstitution guide](/blog/peptide-lyophilization-reconstitution-guide-for-researchers/) covers technique, and the [handling pillar](/peptide-handling-protocols/) covers the broader laboratory workflow. None of this is administration guidance; it is bench handling for research material.

## How It Compares

**Luteinising hormone.** Same receptor, much shorter circulating life. LH is the better choice when the experiment needs a pulsatile signal or a rapid washout; HCG is the better choice when sustained occupancy is the variable under study. The two are not interchangeable in time-course work.

**Recombinant HCG.** Cleaner impurity profile and a narrower isoform distribution, at a higher price. For receptor-binding or signalling assays where batch consistency matters more than cost, the recombinant route is easier to defend.

**HMG (menotropins).** A urinary preparation containing both FSH and LH activity. HMG drives follicular development through FSHR; HCG acts through LHCGR. In rodent superovulation work they are used in sequence, and the two are not substitutes. The [HMG page](/catalog/hmg/) sets out the FSH-side story.

**Gonadorelin and GnRH analogues.** These act upstream at the pituitary GnRH receptor, while HCG acts directly on gonadal LHCGR. A study that uses a GnRH analogue to suppress the axis and HCG to probe Leydig cell reserve is testing two different levels of the same system. The [gonadorelin listing](/catalog/gonadorelin-acetate/) is the relevant catalogue entry.

**PMSG/eCG.** Equine chorionic gonadotropin is the animal-model workhorse for follicular stimulation, with a longer half-life and a mixed FSH/LH profile. It is a different molecule with different receptor behaviour, and the names are sometimes confused in catalogues.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why is HCG activity stated in IU rather than milligrams?**

Because the hormone's potency depends on glycosylation, subunit integrity and receptor binding, none of which a mass measurement captures. IU values are assigned against a WHO reference preparation, so they describe biological activity rather than protein weight. Two vials with identical protein content can differ in IU if one is nicked or partially deglycosylated.

**What is the difference between urinary and recombinant HCG?**

Urinary HCG is extracted from pregnancy urine and contains a broader mixture of isoforms plus residual urinary proteins; recombinant HCG is expressed in mammalian cells and purified, giving a narrower isoform profile and fewer process contaminants. The trade-off is cost and, for some assays, a subtly different glycosylation pattern.

**Why does HCG have a longer half-life than LH?**

The beta subunit carries additional O-linked oligosaccharides with sialic acid caps, which slow clearance. The receptor signalling is the same; the duration of occupancy is not. That is why HCG is used when a sustained LHCGR signal is wanted.

**What does "≥98% purity" mean for a glycoprotein?**

Usually it means one peak by RP-HPLC at 214 or 280 nm, expressed as area percent. It does not measure bioactivity, subunit integrity, glycosylation or aggregation. For HCG, the more informative fields are IU assignment, free beta content and SE-HPLC monomer content.

**How should reconstituted HCG be stored?**

At 4°C, protected from light, for a short working period, typically no more than a week. Aliquot if the experiment runs longer, and avoid repeated freeze-thaw. Do not vortex; swirl gently to avoid denaturation at the interface.

**What documents should accompany a batch?**

A lot-specific certificate with IU assay method, subunit integrity data, glycosylation or isoform data, moisture and fill, endotoxin and sterility results where performed, and a retest date. Plus a shipping record with temperature control for the international leg.

**Is HCG a controlled substance?**

It is a prescription medicine in many countries and is regulated as a hormone product in others. Research supply does not remove those obligations. Confirm the import and use rules for your jurisdiction before ordering; the [compliance pillar](/peptide-compliance-risk/) covers the general framework.
