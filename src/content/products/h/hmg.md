HMG is not a peptide and not a single molecule. It is a urinary extract containing two glycoprotein hormones, follicle-stimulating hormone and luteinising hormone, standardised so that each vial carries a stated number of international units of each activity. That description alone explains most of the sourcing difficulty: the product is defined by a biological assay, not by a chemical formula, and two vials that both say "75 IU" can differ in ratio, isoform composition and residual urinary protein.

This page is written for research laboratories using HMG in reproductive biology and developmental models. It covers the receptor biology, the batch documentation that makes a vial usable, the handling constraints of a urinary glycoprotein preparation, and the comparisons that matter when choosing between HMG, recombinant FSH and hCG. ## Why This Compound

FSH and LH act on two different cell types in the gonad, and the classic two-cell, two-gonadotropin model is still the clearest way to think about HMG. In the ovary, FSH binds FSHR on granulosa cells, driving proliferation, aromatase expression and estradiol production; LH binds LHCGR on theca cells and supplies the androgen precursor for aromatase. In the testis, FSH acts on Sertoli cells while LH acts on Leydig cells. A preparation containing both activities therefore does something that a selective agonist cannot: it activates the two receptor systems together, in the ratio the manufacturer has standardised.

The structural biology is well defined for the receptors. Fan and Hendrickson solved the FSH-FSHR complex in 2005, showing a hormone-receptor interface that involves both subunits and a conformational change on binding. FSHR and LHCGR are closely related GPCRs with large ectodomains, and their signalling is mainly Gs/cAMP, though beta-arrestin recruitment and biased signalling are active research areas. HMG is a convenient way to activate both receptors in a single preparation, which is why it appears in granulosa cell co-culture work and in mixed gonadal cell systems.

The other major use is in developmental biology. Rodent superovulation protocols commonly use a pregnant mare serum gonadotropin or an FSH-rich preparation to drive follicular growth, followed by hCG to trigger ovulation. HMG can substitute for the FSH step in some protocols, and the choice affects oocyte yield, cohort synchrony and downstream embryo experiments. The literature on superovulation is large, but it is also protocol-specific: strain, age, gonadotropin source and timing all change the result, which makes batch consistency more important than the nominal IU value.

Urinary HMG carries a feature that recombinant preparations do not: isoform heterogeneity. FSH is glycosylated and sialylated to different degrees, and the isoform distribution affects clearance and bioactivity. Ulloa-Aguirre and colleagues spent years characterising these isoforms, and their work explains why two preparations with the same IU assignment can behave differently in a time-course experiment. The IU number is a potency summary, not a full description.

## What the Batch Record Should Show

An HMG certificate has to answer a harder question than a peptide certificate: not "is this the right molecule" but "does this vial have the stated biological activity, in both components, with an acceptable impurity profile". The assays are older and less precise than modern LC-MS, and that is precisely why the method matters.

**FSH activity.** The classical in-vivo bioassay is the Steelman-Pohley method, which measures ovarian weight gain in immature rats against a reference standard. Modern laboratories may use cell-based cAMP or reporter assays, which are more precise and less variable. The certificate should name the method, the reference preparation and the result in IU per vial. An immunoassay alone measures antigen content, not receptor activation, and should not be presented as a potency assay.

**LH activity.** LH is present at a lower and more variable level than FSH in urinary preparations. The certificate should state the LH IU per vial and the assay used. A preparation described as "FSH 75 IU" with no LH value is not a full specification for HMG, because the LH component is part of what distinguishes it from purified FSH.

**Ratio and consistency.** The FSH:LH ratio is the defining feature of a menotropin. Ask for the ratio on the certificate and, for a multi-vial order, the range across the lot. A supplier who reports only total gonadotropin activity is hiding the variable that matters most.

**Protein content and urinary impurities.** Total protein per vial, and residual urinary protein, are relevant for cell work and for interpreting fill. HMG is a partially purified extract, not a homogeneous protein, so a "purity" figure in the peptide sense is not meaningful. What matters is the activity per mg protein and the absence of unexpected contaminants.

**Endotoxin, sterility and moisture.** A lyophilised urinary product can carry bacterial endotoxin from the source material if the purification is poor. LAL testing and a sterility test belong in the package for any preparation that will contact cells. Moisture affects stability and should be reported.

**Traceability and source.** Country of origin of the urine, manufacturing site, lot number, fill date and retest date. For urinary products, donor screening and viral inactivation are part of the quality story, and a certificate that omits them is incomplete. ## Sourcing & Verification

HMG is a regulated medicine in many jurisdictions, and the research-supply channel is narrower than the peptide market. Before comparing prices, confirm that the material can be imported for laboratory use and that the supplier can provide the documentation the shipment needs. The [customs guide](/blog/research-peptide-customs-clearance-a-professional-b2b-sourcing-guide-2026/) is the starting point.

The compound-specific questions are:

- What are the FSH and LH IU values per vial, and by which assay methods?
- What is the FSH:LH ratio, and what is the range across the lot?
- Is the source urinary, and what donor-screening and viral-inactivation steps apply?
- What is the total protein per vial, and what is the activity per mg protein?
- Was endotoxin tested by LAL, and was sterility tested?
- Is the fill volume stated, and is there overfill?
- Does the lot number match the vial and the shipping documents?
- Is there a cold-chain record for the international leg?

A pattern to watch for: certificates that report "FSH 75 IU" and nothing about LH, protein or endotoxin. That is a partial specification presented as a complete one. ## Handling & Storage

Lyophilised HMG is stored dry at 2–8°C for routine use and at −20°C for longer periods. Let the vial reach room temperature before opening to avoid condensation. Reconstitute gently with the diluent supplied or with sterile water, directing the stream down the wall and swirling rather than vortexing. Glycoproteins denature at interfaces, and foaming is a sign of damage.

Reconstituted HMG is usually used within 24 hours. That is a short window compared with a synthetic peptide, and it reflects the instability of the two-subunit glycoprotein hormones in solution. Prepare only what the experiment needs, keep the solution at 4°C and protected from light, and avoid repeated warming and cooling. Do not freeze a reconstituted solution unless the protocol specifically validates it; freeze-thaw damages the subunit association.

Low-binding tubes reduce adsorption losses, particularly at the low concentrations used in cell culture. If the preparation must be sterile-filtered, use a 0.22 µm membrane and check recovery; some membranes adsorb glycoproteins. Do not add reducing agents, and avoid buffers that chelate calcium if the assay depends on receptor signalling, because calcium is part of the FSHR activation mechanism.

## How It Compares

**Recombinant FSH.** A single, well-characterised protein produced in mammalian cells, with a defined isoform profile and no urinary protein background. It is the better choice when the experiment needs a clean FSHR agonist and no LH activity. HMG is the better choice when the two-cell system is the point.

**Highly purified urinary FSH.** A urinary product enriched for FSH and depleted of LH. It sits between HMG and recombinant FSH: human-derived isoforms, lower LH contamination, still a urinary extract. The choice depends on whether LH activity is a confounder or a requirement.

**hCG.** An LH-receptor agonist with a long half-life, used to trigger ovulation or to probe Leydig cell function. It is not a substitute for the FSH component of HMG, and the two are often used in sequence rather than interchangeably. The [HCG page](/catalog/hcg/) covers the receptor side.

**Gonadorelin and GnRH analogues.** These act at the pituitary receptor and release endogenous gonadotropins. HMG supplies the hormones directly. A study that uses a GnRH analogue to suppress the axis and HMG to replace the peripheral signal is testing two different levels of the system.

**PMSG/eCG.** A pregnant mare gonadotropin with mixed FSH/LH activity and a long half-life, widely used in rodent superovulation. It is not a human menotropin, and the IU scales are not comparable. The names are sometimes confused in catalogue copy.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the difference between HMG and recombinant FSH?**

HMG is a urinary extract containing both FSH and LH activity, with a heterogeneous isoform profile. Recombinant FSH is a single protein produced in cell culture, with no LH activity and a narrower isoform distribution. HMG is used when combined receptor activation is wanted; recombinant FSH when a clean FSHR agonist is wanted.

**What does 75 IU mean on an HMG vial?**

It is an activity assignment for FSH, traceable to an international standard, not a mass. The vial also contains LH activity, which should be stated separately. Two vials with the same FSH IU can differ in LH content and isoform profile.

**Why is the FSH:LH ratio important?**

Because the two hormones act on different cell types. A preparation with a higher LH component will produce more theca-derived androgen and a different steroidogenic profile than one with less. The ratio is part of the product definition, not a minor impurity.

**How should HMG be stored?**

Dry at 2–8°C, or at −20°C for longer storage. Reconstitute gently and use within 24 hours. Do not freeze the reconstituted solution, and protect it from light.

**Is HMG sterile?**

Not automatically. It is a lyophilised research-grade powder, and sterility is a separate test. For cell culture, filter through a 0.22 µm membrane and confirm that the filter does not adsorb the glycoproteins.

**Can HMG be used in rodent superovulation protocols?**

It is used in that literature, often in sequence with hCG. The protocol details are strain- and supplier-specific, and the IU values are not directly transferable between HMG, recombinant FSH and PMSG. The [model-organism guide](/blog/how-peptides-are-used-in-model-organism-research/) covers the general design issues.

**Is HMG a controlled substance?**

It is a prescription medicine in many jurisdictions and is regulated as a gonadotropin product. Research supply does not remove those obligations. Confirm the import and use rules for your jurisdiction before ordering.

## The Author's Take

**Position.** I would not buy HMG without an LH value and a ratio on the certificate. A vial that states only FSH activity is not a menotropin specification; it is half of one.

**Reasoning.** HMG is defined by two activities, and the second one is the reason to choose it over purified FSH. Suppliers who understand the product report both values, the ratio and the assay methods. Suppliers who do not are either repackaging a pharmaceutical intermediate or copying a template. The biological raw material also makes batch consistency harder than for a synthetic peptide, so the lot-to-lot range matters as much as the single-vial number. In a superovulation experiment, a shift in the LH component can change the steroidogenic environment and the oocyte cohort. That is not a difference a purity percentage will reveal.
